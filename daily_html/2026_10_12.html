SurgiCLAW: An Interpretable Atomic-Skill Agent for Precision Robotic Micromanipulation

Abstract (English): Recent multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and vision--language--action models have provided robots with new capabilities for long-horizon task understanding and planning. In surgical robotics, however, end-to-end prediction of low-level actions remains limited by insufficient precision, opaque decisions, and scarce data. This paper presents SurgiCLAW, a hierarchical skill-based framework for precision surgical manipulation. SurgiCLAW confines an MLLM to intraoperative visual understanding, structured explanation, and atomic surgical-skill selection, while delegating physical execution to verifiable deterministic control programs. For brain--machine-interface implantation, a representative high-precision surgical task, we construct a state-to-skill invocation dataset and expand it through visual and state perturbations. The model first learns structured skill invocation through supervised fine-tuning and is then optimized using three rewards for action correctness, operation prerequisites, and format validity, improving action selection, operational safety, and output parseability. We evaluate subtask success, offline model comparison, real-robot performance, and ablations. On a fixed test set, SurgiCLAW achieves 94.6% skill-selection accuracy and 93.0% macro-averaged F1 (Macro-F1). Across 50 closed-loop real-robot implantation trials, it achieves an 88.0% task success rate.

SurgCast: Action-Conditioned Future Skeletons for Controllable Surgical World Models

Abstract (English): Surgical world models must turn prospective robot actions into visually faithful, structurally consistent instrument motion. We introduce SurgCast, which predicts future instrument skeletons from current structure, robot state, and future actions, then uses the forecast to control video generation. Dual-path injection combines skeleton geometry with derived, instrument-specific motion descriptors through geometric residuals and conditioning modulation. Both paths complement native action conditioning and are retained in a four-step causal student. On SutureBot and SRTH-Porcine-Cholei, the teacher and student improve all seven reported quality and instrument-consistency metrics over their respective direct-action baselines; teacher FVD decreases by 37.6% and 67.3%. Ablations show that future actions improve skeleton prediction and that combining both injection paths performs best across all six metrics under predicted skeletons. These results support using predicted articulated structure and its motion semantics as a control interface for surgical video prediction. Project videos and examples are available at https://surgcast.netlify.app/.

A Reinforcement Learning Framework for Safety-Aware Haptic Shared Control in Surgical Robotics

Abstract (English): Haptic shared control in surgical robotics combines human-side and robot-side forces at a shared end effector while preserving operator involvement. However, the effects of geometric safety choices on task execution and force coordination remain unclear. We model the interaction as a two-agent cooperative Markov game under centralized training and decentralized execution, comparing learned human control with a task-tuned fixed-impedance baseline. This model-free multi-agent reinforcement learning framework supports joint policy adaptation without requiring a complete analytical interaction model. It compares MADDPG and rMAPPO with an epigraph-constrained approach under common geometric task definitions. In simulation, rMAPPO generally achieved the highest return and tracking performance, whereas the epigraph approach produced more conservative motions and lower geometric exposure, including no observed collision or dangerous-region exposure after geometry transfer. With real operator input, fixed-impedance-trained policies were faster and successful in all recorded trials. Policies trained with a learned partner exhibited greater command opposition, while the fixed epigraph condition produced the most aligned commands. This work provides a benchmark for studying human-control representation and constraint-handling trade-offs in haptic shared control.

An Open MuJoCo Simulation Framework for a Quadrotor Carrying a Tendon-Driven Soft Continuum Arm

Abstract (English): Combining aerial robots with soft continuum manipulators promises safe, compliant interaction with the environment, but the coupled dynamics of a free-flying base and a highly deformable, underactuated arm are difficult to study without a suitable simulator. This paper describes an open-source simulation framework, built on the MuJoCo physics engine, for a quadrotor that carries a tendon-driven soft continuum arm underneath its body. The arm is modelled as a pseudo-rigid-body chain of twelve segments joined by pairs of orthogonal compliant hinges. Three tendons, spaced \SI{120}{\degree} apart and routed through every segment, bend it in any direction. A geometric tracking controller on $SE(3)$ flies the vehicle; its rotor allocation matrix is generated directly from the model description. A closed-form tendon-tension allocation keeps every tension within its limits and gives the same bending moment in every direction. We report the full set of modelling choices and parameters, and we evaluate the framework in four simulated scenarios: a \SI{36}{\second} mission with a figure-eight and a tendon-driven tip sweep, an attitude-gain study, a quasi-static characterization of the bending workspace, and an impulsive disturbance applied to the arm tip. With the nominal gains the vehicle tracks the mission with \SI{3.1}{\centi\meter} RMS position error while the arm tip swings up to \SI{37}{\centi\meter} from the body axis. The gain study shows that a low-bandwidth attitude loop lets the arm excite sustained oscillations. The simulation runs about $15\times$ faster than real time on a laptop CPU. All models, controllers and scripts that reproduce the figures are released.

Hand2Robot: Object-Centric 4D Hand Reconstruction from Human Demonstrations for Robotics

Abstract (English): Human demonstrations can capture task-relevant manipulation without using the target robot, but the recorded hand motions cannot directly serve as robot action supervision. Hand reconstruction and motion retargeting provide a direct route to such supervision, yet recovering hand motion alone does not ensure physically consistent hand-object interactions. To address this issue, we introduce Hand2Robot, an object-centric 4D hand reconstruction framework for robot policy post-training. Given videos of humans performing the target task, Hand2Robot extracts interaction clips, reconstructs object geometry, and generates robot action supervision through hand-object interaction optimization and motion retargeting. For object tracking, we combine observation confidence with temporal motion constraints to select a globally consistent object pose trajectory from per-frame pose candidates. For hand refinement, we use object geometry and motion as a reference and combine staged 2D alignment with object-local contact constraints to refine hand trajectories and finger joint configurations. Experiments on DexYCB and HO3D-v3 show improved object pose accuracy and hand-object interaction quality. Experiments on three real-robot dexterous manipulation tasks further show that reconstructed and retargeted human demonstrations can effectively supplement limited robot data for target-task policy post-training.

TLDR: Hand2Robot reconstructs object-centric 4D hand motion from human demonstration videos and retargets it into robot action supervision, improving dexterous manipulation policies when robot data are limited.
