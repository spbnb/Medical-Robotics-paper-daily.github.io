From Presence Labels to Bounding Boxes: Can Multimodal Large Language Models Scale Surgical Instrument Localization?

Abstract (English): Spatial localization of surgical instruments through bounding-box annotations is a foundational component of surgical scene understanding, supporting detection, tracking, interaction analysis, and grounded vision-language applications. Yet existing annotations often cover selected frames, video subsets, or dataset variants rather than complete procedures. Manual extension is time-consuming and expert-dependent. Additionally, weakly supervised and detector-based pseudo-labels require task-specific training and may be imprecise. In this study, we design a scalable MLLM-based annotation framework that converts surgical frames and existing instrument-presence labels into accurate bounding boxes, and systematically analyze its reliability, cost, and design sensitivity. We instantiate the framework using six MLLMs and a multi-call configuration on laparoscopic cholecystectomy in Cholec80. Using a carefully designed stratification scheme, two annotators independently label 1,000 frames, and an expert surgeon reviews one annotation set to establish the reference. Results show that instrument-set F1 ranges from 88.2 to 92.7,whereas mIoU ranges from 0.180 to 0.735, showing that comparable recognition can conceal large localization differences. A structured multi-stage annotation pipeline achieves 0.784 mIoU, compared with 0.735 for the best single-call baseline and 0.814 human-human agreement. Using the best accuracy pipeline, we create and publicly release a spatial extension of Cholec80 comprising 285,977 bounding boxes across all 184,498 annotated frames from the 80 videos. The annotations and human references are made available on GitHub

MIS-VQA: An Annotation-Driven Surgical Vision-Language Dataset for Complex Surgical Understanding

Abstract (English): Modern surgical AI increasingly requires multimodal understanding rather than isolated perception, yet existing datasets remain largely limited to single-task supervision such as recognition or templated question answering. Current medical VQA resources often rely on synthetic templates, lack natural descriptive supervision, or provide sparse annotations that hinder reasoning about spatial relations and procedural context. We introduce MIS-VQA, a surgical vision-language dataset generated through an annotation-driven pipeline that converts heterogeneous surgical annotations into structured textual scene representations. Using EndoVis18 and CholecT50, we transform phase, instrument, interaction, triplet, and segmentation labels into unified contexts that guide large language models to generate clinically coherent question-answer pairs. MIS-VQA supports four response types-concise and detailed descriptions, perception-level grounding, and multi-step reasoning-enabling hierarchical evaluation from atomic visual grounding to compositional reasoning. To address annotation sparsity, we incorporate confidence-filtered segmentation and cross-annotation consistency checks, enabling spatially grounded supervision at scale. MIS-VQA establishes a unified benchmark for surgical vision-language understanding with spatial supervision and multi-tier reasoning evaluation. To demonstrate its utility, we introduce LLaVA-SurgXpert, a family of surgical vision-language models with visual encoders to analyze how visual representations influence multimodal reasoning. Experiments show consistent improvements over existing surgical VQA baselines while revealing capability gaps between perceptual grounding and higher-level reasoning hidden in prior benchmarks.

Semantic-Coupled Perception Framework for Autonomous Laparoscope Control

Abstract (English): In minimally invasive surgery (MIS), maintaining an appropriate field of view (FoV) is essential for surgical safety and efficiency. However, conventional autonomous laparoscope control methods often treat visual tracking and surgical phase recognition independently, limiting robustness under occlusion, entity overlap, and camera motion. This paper proposes a closed-loop autonomous laparoscope control framework that couples real-time surgical phase recognition with camera-motion-compensated visual perception. By exploiting phase-dependent relationships among surgical entities, the framework improves target state estimation under challenging visual conditions, supporting task-aware autonomous FoV control. Experiments on benchmark datasets evaluate the proposed framework in surgical phase recognition and target tracking, including scenarios involving occlusion and entity overlap. Phantom experiments further demonstrate the feasibility of autonomous FoV control, showing improved target visibility and task execution performance compared with fixed-FoV control. These results highlight the potential of incorporating task-level semantics into visual state estimation for autonomous laparoscope guidance.

HerHealthGPT: An Evidence-Grounded Conversational AI System for Women's Reproductive Health Copy title

Abstract (English): "Why is my period late?" Sometimes, the most useful response begins with another question. Yet conversational AI systems may respond to incomplete health queries without seeking essential context or grounding their guidance in verifiable evidence. This paper introduces HerHealthGPT, an evidence-grounded conversational AI system designed to address these challenges in women's reproductive health. HerHealthGPT integrates retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), context-aware clarification, and source validation within an interactive web interface. Rather than responding immediately to incomplete queries, the system maintains conversational context, asks targeted follow-up questions, checks for premature conclusions, and grounds its responses in retrieved evidence. We evaluate the AI components using 540 English messages derived from 90 reproductive-health cases and conduct a prototype questionnaire study with 30 adults. Evidence retrieval improves concern-category accuracy from 54.1% to 59.3% compared with fine-tuning alone. The clarification router achieves 85.6% recall and 86.2% specificity on constructed targets. In the user study, 83.3% of participants preferred the retrieval-enabled configuration, while 25 of 30 considered its follow-up questions relevant. These findings suggest that integrating evidence retrieval with interactive clarification offers a promising approach to more transparent, context-aware reproductive-health information systems, while further clinical validation remains necessary.

Doppler-aided Position-Velocity Tracking of Hidden Objects using mmWave Radar

Abstract (English): Safe indoor operation requires autonomous tracking of objects that are not directly visible to the robot. Optical non-line-of-sight (NLoS) methods depend on ambient lighting and scene geometry or on weak multi-bounce returns that need long acquisitions. Millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar offers an attractive alternative: walls act as reflectors for mmWave signals, and frequency-modulated continuous-wave (FMCW) radars enable fast, practical measurement of range, angle, and velocity. However, the low resolution of these radars leads to spatially ambiguous observations. The Doppler information, although particularly sensitive to target motion, remains underexplored in indoor NLoS localization: existing methods emphasize spatial representations and either collapse or discard the Doppler dimension before learning. To leverage the complete Range-Azimuth-Doppler (RAD) information, we acquire the first indoor NLoS radar dataset that pairs sequential RAD measurements with synchronized position and velocity ground truth under controlled variations in motion: 576 sequences totaling 720,856 frames across 12 trajectories of a mobile robot moving along varying speeds, starting positions, and material configurations. We then formulate NLoS tracking as sequential position-velocity estimation rather than frame-wise localization: a shared 3-D encoder processes the full RAD representation of consecutive frames, separate recurrent branches estimate position and velocity, and post-inference refinement combines the two into a motion-consistent trajectory. Our method achieves 8.95cm position and 1.75cm/s velocity errors on held-out trajectories and generalizes to unseen scenarios involving a moving human target. Together, our dataset and tracker establish a benchmark for tracking hidden moving targets with a low-cost radar.

Enabling Motion-Induced WiFi-CSI Sensing in Multi-Robot Systems: A Simulation Framework

Abstract (English): WiFi Channel State Information (CSI) enables fine-grained sensing of spatial relationships between wireless devices. The WiFi Sensor for Robotics (WSR) toolbox demonstrated that WiFi-CSI measurements collected during robot motion, combined with odometry, can be used to synthesize virtual antenna arrays, enabling motion-induced bearing estimation in multi-robot experiments. However, deploying the WSR approach has remained challenging due to its limited integration with standard robotics tools and reliance on deprecated WiFi-CSI acquisition hardware. This work presents an open-source simulation framework that integrates the WSR sensing pipeline within the robotic simulator Gazebo. The framework generates synthetic WiFi-CSI measurements with realistic physical signal properties and leverages realistic robot motion required for motion-induced sensing and downstream localization. It further supports processing both simulated and real WiFi-CSI data for direct comparison and validation. Our aim is to make WSR-style sensing accessible as a modular sensing modality for robotic research. We validate our framework by comparing simulated and real-world experiments using TurtleBot3 robots, and demonstrate its use for bearing estimation and robot localization. This work lowers the barrier to entry for WiFi-CSI-based sensing in multi-robot systems and provides a foundation for integrating WiFi-CSI sensing with multi-modal perception for localization and coordination. All code and experimental details are publicly available at https://github.com/baharhaghighat/DARS_2026_Dijkstra_et_al

Compressive microendoscopy in the presence of noise and misalignment: the translational correlation advantage of graded-index fibers

Abstract (English): Multimode fibers (MMFs) offer a minimally invasive platform for deep-tissue and multisite in vivo optical sensing. Computational microendoscopy methods extend them into the high-resolution imaging domain, but are quite sensitive to experimental errors. This study investigates the robustness of microendoscopy based on compressive sensing (CS) by comparing step-index (SI) MMFs with graded-index (GRIN) fibers. Mode-expansion-based simulations are used to assess the accuracy of image reconstruction in the presence of fundamental photon noise and lateral fiber shifts. We show that both fiber types exhibit comparable noise sensitivity, but GRIN fibers are significantly more robust to lateral misalignment errors. These results establish GRIN MMFs as an alignment-tolerant platform for robust computational microendoscopy.
