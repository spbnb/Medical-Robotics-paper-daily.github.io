Sparse-View 3D Coronary Reconstruction from Vessel Segmentations Using a Diffusion Prior

Abstract (English): Reconstructing three-dimensional (3D) coronary anatomy from sparse angiographic views could support percutaneous coronary intervention, but limited angular coverage and imperfect vessel segmentations introduce substantial geometric ambiguity. We propose a vessel-specific reconstruction framework combining a learned 3D diffusion prior, visual-hull initialization, and projection-based data consistency. The unconditional diffusion model is trained on coronary vessel masks without paired 2D--3D data and reused across different view counts without retraining. During inference, a scaled visual hull initializes reconstruction, while projection-based updates constrain diffusion refinement using patient-specific vessel observations. We evaluated the framework on simulated angiograms derived from ImageCAS computed tomography angiography volumes using two, four, and eight views at two contrast levels. Across 50 evaluation coronary-tree cases, the proposed method achieved higher mean Dice and clDice than visual hull, deep image prior (DIP), and hash-grid implicit neural representation (INR) at all view counts under reduced contrast. With eight views, improvements over DIP, the best-performing baseline at lower contrast, were approximately 0.13 in Dice and 0.22 in clDice. At higher contrast, performance remained competitive with hash-grid INR, the best-performing eight-view baseline. The preferred diffusion starting timestep depended on observation quality and view count, with excessive refinement reducing accuracy at higher contrast. Projection guidance improved both metrics across all conditions. Averaging stochastic reconstructions further improved accuracy, while sample disagreement localized errors with average-precision gains of 0.14--0.21 over error prevalence. These findings support diffusion-prior reconstruction from sparse vessel observations, although clinical validation remains necessary.

EndoS3:Endoscopic Semantic Splatting SLAM

Abstract (English): Purpose: Despite advances in 3D surgical scene understanding, semantic simultaneous localisation and mapping (SLAM) remains under-explored for two key reasons. First, it requires solving three challenging tasks in parallel: camera pose tracking, high-fidelity deformable reconstruction, and semantic mapping, making progress particularly demanding. Second, the scarcity of datasets providing the necessary ground truth to evaluate all three tasks comprehensively under conditions that closely reflect real surgical procedures environments. Method: We propose EndoS3, a Gaussian Splatting-based semantic SLAM framework that uses instance masks to guide all three tasks. An instance-level motion decomposition module separates static and dynamic instances by evaluating residual flow after compensating for camera motion, accounting for flow noise and comparing motion across neighbouring instances. The camera is tracked against the static Gaussians, while an instance-aware warp field gives each dynamic instance its own control nodes and deformation network. A semantic node allocation module redistributes a fixed node budget across instances according to their size and non-rigidity. We also introduce CRCD++, an ex-vivo Cholecystectomy dataset with RGB-D frames, camera pose and instance annotations for full semantic SLAM evaluation. Results: EndoS3 surpasses all state-of-the-art general-purpose and surgical semantic SLAM methods in tracking, reconstruction and semantic mapping on the CRCD++ and SemSup datasets. Conclusion: Semantic-aware motion and deformation modelling is vital to surgical semantic SLAM. EndoS3 and CRCD++ offer a method and benchmark for improved geometric and semantic understanding in endoscopy.

DUNEDepth: Bronchoscopic Depth and Pose Estimation via Hybrid Adaptation of a 3D-Vision Foundation Model

Abstract (English): State-of-the-art bronchoscopy guidance systems and computer-aided diagnosis methods have emerged as important tools for early lung cancer detection. Effective use of these tools depends on being able to accurately link a patient's bronchoscopic airway exam video to their pre-operative 3D chest CT scan. Unfortunately, the derivation of such links, which include bronchoscope depth and camera motion, from the exam video remains an open problem. Building on recently proposed 3D-vision foundation models, we propose DUNEDepth, a hybrid architecture that combines a shared DUNE ViT encoder, a twin MASt3R cross-attention decoder structure, and a prediction head that includes elements of the MASt3R and Reloc3r models. To fine-tune the architecture to our application, we utilize an integrated hybrid synthetic-supervised/self-supervised training strategy that draws on both bronchoscopic video and CT-derived virtual bronchoscopic views. Experiments drawing on bronchoscopic video sequences demonstrate that DUNEDepth more accurately derives depth information and camera motion trajectories than previously proposed self-supervised methods.

SurgEye: Instruction Tuning-Free Perception-Reasoning Framework for Surgical Scene Understanding

Abstract (English): Surgical scene understanding is pivotal for computer-assisted intervention, yet demands compositional reasoning across instruments, anatomy, and workflow. Existing surgical vision-language models rely on instruction tuning with large-scale question-answer pairs, entailing laborious data curation and costly retraining. To bridge this gap, we propose SurgEye, a perception-reasoning framework that grounds reasoning in a vision foundation model without instruction tuning. Specifically, we devise Routed Multi-Task Perception (RMP) that routes the vision foundation model through task-specific pathways learned from public perception labels, producing spatial and temporal evidence. We further introduce Agentic Compositional Reasoning (ACR) that employs a frozen large language model to coordinate RMP specialists and temporal memory as callable tools, composing evidence across tasks and time into grounded answers. Moreover, we construct SurgEye-Bench spanning eight surgical procedures, pairing each question with perception labels for verifiable evaluation. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SurgEye outperforms state-of-the-arts in surgical reasoning. The source code will be available.

SurgTransfer: Multi-Step Latent Action Modeling for Action Transfer in Surgical World Model

Abstract (English): Surgical world models have the potential to provide intra-operative guidance, accelerate risk-free training and facilitate transfer of expert surgical maneuvers. However, the use of world models for transferring surgical expertise has been largely unexplored. Moreover, recent approaches in this domain have mainly relied on ground truth kinematics actions or expert annotations for specific tasks, limiting their applicability beyond the narrow range of tasks with ground-truth annotations. In contrast, existing works in the natural domain have shown promising results in learning latent actions solely from unlabeled videos and transferring the learned actions across contexts. We observe that this paradigm is short-sighted for the surgical domain, failing to capture the hierarchical and goal-directed structure of surgical procedures. Thus, we propose a novel multi-step action learning framework to learn context-invariant latent actions that are easily transferable across various surgical contexts. We perform extensive experiments on the publicly available datasets, SurgToolLoc-2022 and PitVis-2023, verifying our approach with both quantitative metrics and qualitative evaluation by surgical experts. We have published our code and the implementation details at https://github.com/bhattarailab/Surgical-Vision-World-Model.

ActOR: Ambient AI-Based Agentic Orchestration for Humanoid Autonomy in Operating Rooms

Abstract (English): Operating rooms (ORs) increasingly contain ambient sensing infrastructure to enable AI-based optimization of workflows and utilization. We pursue an alternative path: using ambient AI to guide robot action and optimize utilization. Deploying humanoids in the OR without ambient AI is severely limited by cumbersome de novo orientation and planning due to partial onboard observations. We posit that this can be achieved by coupling an ambient AI-based agentic orchestrator with the humanoid robotic actor, providing shared perception and execution feedback for humanoid autonomy in the OR. ActOR fuses a wall-mounted ambient camera and the robot's head-mounted camera into a shared object-level digital twin (DT). A large language model agent plans over the DT using pre-programmed locomotion and manipulation skills without task-specific training. During execution, DT updates and execution outcomes inform retries, alternative approaches, or replanning. We evaluated ActOR in simulated and physical environments. Across three tasks in two simulated ORs reconstructed from real rooms, ambient perception reduced exploration from 1.05 to 0.12 Explore calls per trial when targets were initially outside the robot's view. Closed-loop feedback increased success from 46.7% to 83.3% under nominal execution and from 8.3% to 51.7% under injected navigation, object-relocation, and grasp perturbations. In a physical mock OR with a humanoid, ActOR completed 14 of 20 single-object disposal trials and demonstrated replanning after scene changes. Ambient AI-based observations provide scene state that improves robot action planning, increasing success and reducing exploration compared with robot-only observations. Feeding skill outcomes and DT updates back to the planner improves multi-step task success. Our results show the feasibility of humanoid task autonomy for multi-step disposal tasks in a mock OR.

A Guideline-Grounded Multi-Agent Framework for Personalized Exercise Prescription from Clinical and Wearable Data

Abstract (English): Translating population-level exercise guidance into individualized prescriptions requires integrating clinical and wearable data, including diagnoses, medications, functional limitations, and baseline activity, with condition-specific recommendations, a process difficult to scale beyond specialist rehabilitation settings. We developed FITTER, a multi-agent framework that integrates these data with condition-specific guidance to generate personalized FITT-VP prescriptions and applies deterministic safety audit and repair to correct rule-checkable violations. We evaluated FITTER using data from 400 All of Us participants (300 cancer, 100 stroke) against five LLM comparators through computational evaluation, ablation, and structured clinician review. FITTER achieved 98.5% safety-constraint compliance versus 75.8-85.8% for comparators and showed greater quantitative dosing precision and guideline alignment. Cancer-cohort ablation identified safety audit and repair as the largest contributor to safety-constraint compliance.
