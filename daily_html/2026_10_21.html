High-Precision RCM Motion for Ophthalmic Robotics via Hierarchical QP Control and Authority-Guided Recovery Planning

Abstract (English): General-purpose manipulators can position ophthalmic instruments under a software Remote Centre of Motion (RCM). However, at some reachable configurations that are nonsingular for the tip task alone, the RCM and tip-tracking objectives conflict and the servo oscillates. We formalise this failure as a constraint-induced task singularity: the RCM-projected tip Jacobian loses rank while the unprojected tip Jacobian stays well conditioned. Thus, the effective tip authority collapses although the target remains reachable. We present a high-precision RCM servo architecture with built-in singularity recovery: for each commanded target, an authority-guided planner selects a well-conditioned arm configuration that reaches it and plans an RCM-consistent route, executed by a fixed hierarchical-QP controller. On a 200-scenario kinematic benchmark, the complete nominal-and-recovery protocol reaches 128/128 near-singular targets and 71/72 near-joint-limit targets (100% and 99%, respectively), with the software-RCM residual below 0.142 mm. On a physical 6-DOF arm it executes across four RCM entry points, with a worst-case kinematically reconstructed RCM residual of 178 μm and a median tip-regulation error of 32μm, within the 200μm control requirement adopted for the ophthalmic task.

MMedAgent-Plus: Scaling Multimodal Medical Agents across Modalities, Tasks and Workflows

Abstract (English): Medical workflows often require multiple interdependent analyses, but existing specialist and generalist AI models are not designed to coordinate them. We introduce MMedAgent-Plus, a multimodal medical tool-use agent that combines a vision-language planner with 18 leading specialist models spanning 12 tasks and 8 modalities. Through a unified tool interface, the planner selects specialists, executes them in sequence, and synthesizes responses grounded in their outputs. The planner is instruction-tuned on 208,928 samples from 32 public medical benchmarks, covering single- and multi-tool interactions across single and multiple rounds. We separately evaluate tool orchestration, response synthesis, and end-to-end execution on held-out samples. MMedAgent-Plus achieves tool-call accuracies of 91.0%, 98.1%, and 80.7% in single-tool multi-round, multi-tool multi-round, and multi-tool single-round settings, respectively. Given correct tool outputs, MMedAgent-Plus achieves scores of 14.4-14.8 out of 15 for faithfulness, completeness, and conciseness across 600 evaluation samples. In end-to-end execution without pre-supplied tool outputs, MMedAgent-Plus correctly invokes the required specialist in 90.6% of 350 evaluation cases spanning seven tasks. Its final-answer accuracy reaches 0.98, matching the dedicated leading specialist and exceeding the strongest baseline (0.28).

A general-purpose System One decision model for clinical records

Abstract (English): Clinical care, health-system operations and biomedical discovery depend on extracting reliable answers from patient records, yet applying diverse clinical questions across entire populations remains computationally expensive. Here we present Clinical System One (CS1), a 4-billion-parameter decision model for fast, typed judgments from clinical records, along with two agentic harnesses in which CS1 works together with a generalist language model. CS1-4B is trained on 12.4 million questions and answers from 3.6 million patients across a multi-hospital health system, combining structured-data keys, observed outcomes and language-model supervision across clinical notes, diagnostic reports and patient timelines. It answers multiple typed questions in a single forward pass without task-specific retraining, and each answer carries a probability that can route uncertain cases to human review. On 963,408 held-out patients, CS1-4B outperformed open decision models up to 27 billion parameters on 13 of 14 tasks scored independently of its training teacher. Across 21 external data sets, its aggregate performance approached that of the strongest tested 27-billion-parameter model, with 6.4-fold faster inference in a matched workload. Integrating CS1-4B with a language model agent produced cited answers over complete multi-year patient histories and screened a patient population against the eligibility criteria of 17 registered clinical trials, placing four times as many judged-eligible patients among its top 20 candidates as keyword search while using 690-fold fewer language-model tokens than a language model reading every record. These findings support a division of labour in clinical AI, with small decision models performing repeated record reading and generative models formulating questions and synthesizing evidence.

EndoWorld-JEPA: Motion-Guided Global-Local Latent World Modeling for Endoscopic Video Representation Learning

Abstract (English): Endoscopic video contains state transitions induced by camera displacement, deformable tissue, and instrument interaction, yet existing pre-training methods treat dynamics implicitly. Inspired by action-conditioned joint-embedding predictive world models and by the observation that endoscopic optical flow contains both shared image-plane displacement and localized residual motion, we reformulate passive endoscopic video pre-training as causal motion-guided latent-state prediction. We present EndoWorld-JEPA, to our knowledge the first latent world-model pre-training framework designed for general-purpose endoscopic video representation learning. In place of recorded actions, calibrated poses, or robot kinematics, the model conditions prediction of the next latent state on motion observed up to the current state. Its Optical Motion-guided module separates dominant displacement from residual motion and converts the resulting evidence into an ordered temporal motion condition and spatial local-motion weights. Complementary global and motion-local predictors use these outputs: the temporal condition modulates both latent transitions, whereas the local weights define an auxiliary motion-local state that extends prediction from scene-level evolution to spatially selective dynamics. Patch-wise motion weighting emphasizes localized change while retaining anatomical context, and past-motion-window dropout randomly withholds the complete motion condition to reduce dependence on unreliable motion. This global--local formulation learns predictable visual--motion dynamics in representation space without reconstructing stochastic appearance artifacts. Evaluation shows that a common pre-trained checkpoint achieves strong transfer across four downstream benchmarks, supporting motion-guided global--local latent prediction as an effective pre-training paradigm for endoscopic video.

Velocity Optimization via Thermal Imaging Enhances Autonomous Tumor Resection

Abstract (English): Objective: To demonstrate and evaluate THERMO, a thermal-velocity optimization algorithm for autonomous electrosurgery, in ex vivo porcine tissue using the UR10e-based ASTR system. Methods: Thermography for Electrosurgical Rate Modulation via Optimization (ThERMO), a closed-loop algorithm that uses real-time thermal imaging data to minimize thermal injury while ensuring accurate cut margins, was tested on ex vivo porcine tissue. Histological analysis was performed to detect thermal injury extent at a cellular level, and 3D photogrammetric measurement was used to verify margin accuracy. ThERMO's performance was compared against two previously used fixed velocity methods. Results: ThERMO outperformed both fixed velocity comparison methods without requiring data-driven training or iterative tuning, reducing thermal injury by up to 52%, improving cut accuracy by up to 59%, and maintaining task completion speed. Conclusions: Closed-loop thermal feedback via ThERMO improves autonomous robotic electrosurgery by simultaneously reducing thermal injury and improving margin accuracy, reinforcing its value over fixed velocity approaches for tumor resection applications. Significance: This work advances autonomous robotic tumor resection by demonstrating that real-time closed-loop thermal feedback control can substantially reduce collateral tissue damage and improve surgical margin accuracy.

Offline Apprenticeship Learning for Human-Centric Environments

Abstract (English): Effective decision-making in human-centric environments such as Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITSs) and clinical healthcare requires policies that are adaptive, data-efficient, and ethically deployable. Apprenticeship Learning (AL) infers policies directly from expert demonstrations by recovering the underlying reward structure, enabling strictly offline learning without environment interaction. This dissertation develops a progression of offline AL frameworks organized around two challenges: reward modeling and demonstration quality. For reward modeling, EM-EDM clusters demonstrations by latent reward functions and induces cluster-specific policies, and THEMES partitions trajectories into temporally coherent sub-trajectories guided by a hierarchically learned reward regulator; across one educational and three clinical datasets, THEMES outperforms flat and hierarchical AL baselines. For demonstration quality, HALIDE integrates continuous quality signals into hierarchical policy induction, enabling principled reuse of mixed-quality data under distribution shift. CCHAL further attributes quality to individual decisions via Bayesian credit assignment, dual-arm criticality scoring that contrasts expert and non-expert behavior, and a Bradley-Terry contrastive IRL objective over failure trajectories, outperforming baselines including HALIDE on three clinical EHR datasets and an ITS dataset. Finally, a real-world classroom evaluation of StuEDM, an EDM-induced policy that preserves student agency and intervenes only on disagreement, shows significant learning gains over an Expert baseline and a higher pretest-adjusted posttest score than a CQL policy trained on a far larger corpus, while overriding students less than half as often. Together, this work establishes offline AL as a practical, data-efficient, and ethically viable paradigm for human-centric environments.

A Multi-Agent Framework for Automated Machine Learning with Evidence-based Planning for Medical Image Analysis

Abstract (English): Deep learning has become the default tool for medical image analysis, driven by the open-source movement. However, achieving the best performance on a given task remains difficult, with architecture selection and hyperparameter optimization (HPO) still considered "more of an art than a science". Automated machine learning (AutoML) automates parts of model training, yet helps little in medical imaging: general-purpose approaches lack medical architectures and preprocessing, and medical-focused ones demand engineering expertise and remain confined to narrow tasks such as 3D segmentation. Abstract (English): Realistic few-shot adaptation of medical vision-language models (VLMs) can leave low-prevalence classes without any labeled support. The training-free SS- Text+ solver remains operational in this setting, but its zero-support prototypes receive no visual adaptation and fall back to a purely textual class prototype. We in- troduce Gated Knowledge-Guided Prototype Transfer (GProtoT), a pre-repulsion module that transfers supported-class visual residuals to missing classes using VLM similarity and an ontology-based clinical distance. A validation-free evidence gate scales transfer based on semantic relevance and residual agreement, thereby limiting unsupported extrapolation.
