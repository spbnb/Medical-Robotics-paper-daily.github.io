Supervised Autonomous Bimanual Suction and Cauterization for Bleeding Control in Low Grade Splenic Trauma

Abstract (English): Uncontrolled hemorrhage remains a leading cause of preventable trauma mortality. Timely intervention is not always available, making autonomous systems that can aid in bleeding control a promising alternative. Given the safety-critical nature of the task, supervised autonomy, in which the system executes the technical steps while a human retains oversight, represents a necessary intermediate step toward this goal. In this work, we present a supervised autonomous bimanual robotic framework for injury cauterization (eletrosurgical coagulation) in low-grade splenic trauma. The system integrates real-time bleeding segmentation, markerless tool keypoint tracking, and deep tool-tissue contact detection to coordinate two robotic arms for synchronized suction and cauterization. Evaluated on a novel, cannulated ex vivo splenic hemorrhage model, the system achieved an average bleeding surface cauterization coverage of 82.9% across seven distinct injuries. This work demonstrates the feasibility of using a supervised autonomous bimanual system to deliver targeted coagulation across an injury site.

摘要（中文）: 无法控制的出血仍是可预防性创伤死亡的主要原因之一。及时干预并非总能实现，因此能够辅助止血的自主系统成为一种有前景的替代方案。鉴于该任务的安全攸关性质，监督式自主——即系统执行技术步骤而人类保留监督——是迈向该目标的必要中间阶段。在本研究中，我们提出了一种用于低级别脾创伤损伤烧灼（电外科凝血）的监督式自主双臂机器人框架。该系统集成了实时出血分割、无标记工具关键点跟踪以及深度工具-组织接触检测，以协调两条机械臂进行同步抽吸和烧灼。在一个新型插管离体脾出血模型上进行评估，该系统在七种不同损伤中实现了平均82.9%的出血表面烧灼覆盖率。这项工作证明了使用监督式自主双臂系统在损伤部位实施靶向凝血的可行性。

TLDR: This paper presents a supervised autonomous bimanual robotic system that integrates bleeding segmentation, tool tracking, and contact detection to perform suction and electrocautery for bleeding control in low-grade splenic trauma, achieving 82.9% average cauterization coverage on an ex vivo model.

SaddleSense: Saddle-Shaped Modules for 3D Shape Sensing with Single-Axis Off-the-Shelf Bend Sensors

Abstract (English): Accurate posture feedback is essential for precise control of continuum robots. Off-the-shelf bend sensors measure curvature about only one axis; without a suitable mounting arrangement, they can restrict robot motion and provide incomplete posture information. SaddleSense introduces a 3D-printed thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) saddle-shaped lattice that holds one commercial bend sensor along each curved edge. Because each sensor flexes within its own channel rather than spanning the segment axially, the lattice converts multi-axis body deformation into sensor curvature without tying segment length to sensor length. Each four-sensor module accommodates two-axis bending, contraction, and extension while quantifying all three deformation modes across its four readings. A hybrid calibration maps the sensor readings to piecewise constant curvature (PCC) parameters using a learned model paired with a physics-based fallback for out-of-distribution inputs. We demonstrate a six-module prototype with 24 off-the-shelf bend sensors on a 430~mm continuum body. Using only each module's four local readings, the physics-based model alone achieves a tip position root mean square error (RMSE) of 29.9~mm (7.0\% of body length). The data-driven model reduces the tip RMSE to 13.2~mm (3.1\%) on held-out samples of motions similar to the training set and 15.8~mm (3.7\%) on separately recorded trials. Reducing each module from four sensors to one nearly triples the tip error.

Where Does EEG Help Wearable Freezing of Gait Detection?

Abstract (English): Freezing of gait (FoG) detectors built on inertial measurement units (IMUs) are accurate while the legs move, but they become unreliable when the legs are nearly still, which is exactly when akinetic freezing, voluntary stopping, and standing must be told apart. Electroencephalography (EEG) captures motor-planning activity that leg sensors cannot, but EEG is impractical for daily use. We ask where EEG helps: as an input at inference time, or only during pretraining, so that the deployed detector still needs only IMUs. We assemble a pretraining corpus of synchronized EEG and IMU recordings (5 datasets, 184 people, 94 hours) and compare six settings that differ only in the modalities used for pretraining and as model input, each with tuned hyperparameters and five independent pretraining runs. On the BXHC FoG dataset (leave-one-patient-out, 10 patients with FoG), adding EEG as input raises FoG F1 from 0.663 to 0.740 (9 of 10 patients improve), and the gain in ranking performance is confined to the quartile of windows with the least leg movement (AUROC +0.063). Pretraining the IMU encoder jointly with EEG improves an IMU-only detector both within the cohort (FoG F1 0.663 to 0.705) and when it is trained on a small cohort and tested on another (FoG F1 +0.083, all 10 test patients improve). Repeating the study with PD-only and healthy-only pretraining corpora, each with its own hyperparameter search, shows that IMU-based detection is robust to the corpus, whereas the benefit of aligning EEG with IMU requires both. Cross-cohort analysis further shows that detectors trained without annotated voluntary stops classify nearly all stops as freezing. Finally, on PD versus non-PD classification in three external datasets, joint EEG+IMU pretraining improves PD detection from walking EEG over EEG-only pretraining (AUROC 0.750 vs. 0.683), while IMU-based detection does not depend on the pretraining.

摘要（中文）: 冻结步态（FoG）检测器在腿部运动时基于惯性测量单元（IMU）的准确度较高，但当腿部接近静止时其可靠性下降，而这一情形恰恰是需要区分无动性冻结、主动停止和站立的时候。脑电图（EEG）能够捕捉腿部传感器无法获取的运动规划活动，但EEG在日常使用中并不实用。我们探究EEG在何处发挥作用：是在推理时作为输入，还是仅用于预训练，从而使部署的检测器仍然只需IMU。我们构建了一个包含同步EEG和IMU记录的预训练语料库（5个数据集，184人，94小时），并比较了六种设置，这些设置仅在预训练所用模态和模型输入模态上有所不同，每种设置均采用调整后的超参数和五次独立的预训练运行。在BXHC FoG数据集上（留一患者交叉验证，10名FoG患者），加入EEG作为输入使FoG F1从0.663提升至0.740（10名患者中有9名改善），且排序性能的提升仅限于腿部运动最少的分位数窗口（AUROC +0.063）。将IMU编码器与EEG联合预训练，能够改善仅使用IMU的检测器，无论是在同一队列内（FoG F1从0.663提升至0.705），还是在小队列训练并在另一队列测试时（FoG F1 +0.083，10名测试患者全部改善）。使用仅含PD和仅含健康受试者的预训练语料库重复该研究，每次均进行各自的超参数搜索，结果表明基于IMU的检测对语料库内容具有鲁棒性，而EEG与IMU对齐的收益则需要两者共同参与。跨队列分析进一步显示，未经标注主动停止数据训练的检测器几乎将所有停止均判定为冻结。最后，在三个外部数据集上进行PD与非PD分类时，EEG+IMU联合预训练相较于仅EEG预训练改善了从行走EEG中检测PD的效果（AUROC 0.750 对 0.683），而基于IMU的检测则不受预训练内容的影响。

TLDR: This paper investigates whether EEG helps wearable freezing-of-gait detection only as an inference-time input or also during pretraining such that deployed detectors still use only IMUs. Using a large synchronized EEG+IMU corpus and multiple pretraining/input settings, they find EEG input improves FoG detection especially during low leg movement, and joint EEG+IMU pretraining can improve IMU-only FoG detectors without requiring EEG at deployment.
