The Model Knows Better: Defending Medical Multi-Agent Systems Against Prompt Injection

Abstract (English): Medical multi-agent systems, in which LLM agents discuss a case, are increasingly studied for clinical decision support. Prompt injection, text that an attacker adds to a model's input, can manipulate LLMs into wrong decisions. How it affects these systems, and how to protect them, remains unclear. Across two systems, two model families and three benchmarks, a single injected passage succeeds in up to 61% of cases, and collaboration does not consistently protect against it. Existing defenses help only partly: they miss injections that look like ordinary clinical text. Yet when asked to check the injected text against the patient's findings, a model that followed the injection usually rejects it. We therefore propose a training-free defense that verifies the clinical judgments behind the system's decision. Following clinical practice, it separates the patient's findings from clinical judgments, such as diagnoses, recommendations and instructions, and checks each judgment against the findings alone, so the decision rests only on supported judgments. Our defense outperforms all baselines on every benchmark, lowering attack success on MedQA to 4-7% while maintaining accuracy on cases without an attack, and remains effective with a medical model and against defense-aware and adaptive attacks.

KFS$^4$LAM: Adaptive KeyFrame Selection with Structure-Aware Mapping for Endoscopic Gaussian Splatting SLAM

Abstract (English): Gaussian-splatting Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) augments conventional SLAM with a dense, renderable 3D scene representation, enabling high-quality reconstruction and novel-view synthesis. However, its localization, reconstruction, and rendering performance depends strongly on which observations are retained for mapping. Existing systems commonly rely on fixed temporal intervals or simple pose- and overlap-based criteria for keyframe selection. In colonoscopy, non-uniform camera motion, repetitive anatomy, specularities, and visual artifacts can make these criteria unreliable, leading to either redundant keyframes or missed informative viewpoints. We present KFS$^4$LAM (KeyFrame Selection with Structure-aware mapping for endoscopic Gaussian Splatting SLAM), which couples motion-guided adaptive keyframe selection with structurally supervised mapping and restricted temporal deformation. Accumulated motion guides adaptive keyframe selection, with complementary cues suppressing redundant or unreliable candidates. Density-aware mapping with anatomical contour supervision (colon folds) emphasizes structurally informative regions, while restricted temporal deformation accommodates local tissue dynamics without unconstrained geometric drift. We evaluate KFS$^4$LAM against Gaussian-SLAM baselines on C3VD and C3VD-v2, together with cross-backbone and component ablations. KFS$^4$LAM achieves strong overall performance across trajectory, rendering, and depth metrics, with pronounced gains on more challenging C3VD-v2 dataset. In the matched comparison with SplaTAM on C3VD-v2, it reduces rotational relative pose error by 20.3% and depth RMSE by 38.4%, while improving PSNR by 0.78 dB. Cross-backbone experiments further show that the framework is not tied to a single underlying SLAM architecture. Code and visualization results are available at https://xinweiju.github.io/KFSLAM/.

On the possibilities and limitations of LLM-based multi-agent systems for multi-criteria decision-making in healthcare operations management

Abstract (English): Multi-criteria models for healthcare operations need weights that express how stakeholders trade off patient waiting time, provider workload, and operating cost, but these weights are difficult to quantify. Teams of large language model (LLM) agents, each prompted to represent a stakeholder, have been used to supply them. We propose a framework for assessing the reliability of LLM-generated preferences as inputs to multi-objective optimization and quantifying how their uncertainty affects decisions. We study explicit preferences by asking agents to rate the importance of the criteria in an outpatient appointment scheduling problem, and implicit preferences by inferring trade-offs from the agents' pairwise choices between emergency department configurations. The computational experiments show that the explicit preferences respond to the clinic's operating conditions and differ across roles, but deliberation among the agents can erase these differences, whereas simple averaging preserves them. The pairwise choices used to reveal the agents' implicit preferences depend largely on which option is presented first, and we find limited evidence that a statistical model using contextual information alongside presentation order predicts the agents' choices on new comparison pairs better than a model using presentation order alone. We then formulate robust weighted-sum optimization models to account for preference uncertainty. In most cases, these models select different schedules or configurations from those obtained using average preference weights. These results indicate that LLM-generated preferences need an audit of this kind before they are used as optimization weights in healthcare decision support.

When Evidence Vanishes: Distilling Visual-Evidence Dependency in Medical Vision-Language Models

Abstract (English): Compact medical vision-language models (VLMs) can answer clinical questions correctly without reliably using the image, relying instead on question wording or learned priors. Standard knowledge distillation copies a teacher's answer preference on the original image, but it leaves unchecked whether that preference is conditional on the visual evidence required by the question. We study this missing target as medical visual evidence dependency: support for the correct answer should decrease when the matched evidence is absent. We introduce Evidence-Conditioned Dependency Distillation (ECDD), which pairs each training image with a control matched to the evidence level and distills the teacher's drop in answer margin across the pair. A lower bound objective transfers positive dependency by penalizing only shortfalls, while original-image supervision preserves task accuracy. The same formulation applies when the evidence is a queried clinical region, a surgical instrument, or whole-image visual content. Across three medical VQA settings, ECDD keeps original-image accuracy competitive with distillation and alignment baselines while making answer support more sensitive to evidence removal. Under the DME headline objective, for example, the positive-case margin response to target rather than random masking rises from 0.786 with KD to 2.023 with ECDD, while original-image accuracy differs by 0.11 points. These results suggest that medical VLM distillation should transfer both the teacher's answer preference and its dependence on visual evidence.

摘要（中文）: 摘要：紧凑型医学视觉语言模型（VLMs）能够正确回答临床问题，但未必可靠地使用图像，而是依赖问题措辞或习得的先验知识。标准知识蒸馏会复制教师在原始图像上的答案偏好，但并未检验该偏好是否以问题所需的视觉证据为条件。我们将这一缺失目标研究为医学视觉证据依赖性：当匹配的证据缺失时，对正确答案的支持应当下降。我们引入了基于证据条件的依赖蒸馏（ECDD），该方法将每张训练图像与一个与证据水平匹配的对照组配对，并蒸馏教师在该配对上的答案边际下降幅度。一个下界目标通过仅惩罚不足部分来传递正依赖性，而原始图像监督则保持任务准确率。相同的公式同样适用于证据为查询的临床区域、手术器械或整幅图像视觉内容的情形。在三个医学视觉问答（VQA）设置中，ECDD在保持原始图像准确率与蒸馏及对齐基线相当的同时，使答案支持对证据移除更加敏感。以DME主要目标为例，正例边际响应针对目标掩码（而非随机掩码）的数值从KD的0.786提升至ECDD的2.023，而原始图像准确率仅相差0.11个百分点。这些结果表明，医学VLM蒸馏不仅应迁移教师的答案偏好，还应迁移其对视觉证据的依赖性。

Authors: Qizhen Lan, Yiling Ma, Ruiqi Chen, Mengchen Fan, Xi Xiao, Lin Zhao, Liang He, Baocheng Geng, Pulin Che, Tianyang Wang, Xiaoqian Jiang

Self-Reflective Chain-of-Thought (SRCoT): Evidence-Based Text Generation via a Closed-Loop Retrieve--Reflect--Revise Pipeline on Edge LLMs

Abstract (English): Large language models (LLMs) can generate fluent outputs that lack source support or conflict with local operational constraints. We introduce Self-Reflective Chain-of-Thought (SRCoT), a modular architecture that organizes generation as a closed Retrieve--Reflect--Revise loop. SRCoT separates environment state, persistent knowledge, accumulated experience, and short-term memory into provenance-labeled stores. BM25 retrieval supplies local context; subsequent passes expose intermediate drafts, missing-information markers, follow-up queries, and optional claim--evidence mappings. These artifacts inform retrieval, revision, clarification, or abstention decisions. We examine the architecture through qualitative, trace-based case studies in two fully synthetic settings: emergency medical services and Munich-Sim, an emergency-operations simulation. Local prototype runs on an Apple M4-family laptop with 128 GB memory illustrate source-labeled documentation, explicit gap marking, and structured dispatch-style outputs. The traces also reveal provenance-assignment errors, incomplete evidence coverage, and unsafe assumptions that remain possible despite prompting constraints. These cases demonstrate report-level inspectability and local execution, not comparative superiority, hallucination reduction, clinical validity, or operational safety. The architecture separates retrieval and control from the model interface, but behavior across different models remains to be evaluated. SRCoT is a research proof of concept requiring human review, with its principal contribution lying in an inspectable control procedure rather than a new model.

摘要（中文）: 大型语言模型（LLMs）能够生成流畅的输出，但这些输出可能缺乏来源支撑，或与局部操作约束相冲突。我们提出自反思思维链（SRCoT），这是一种模块化架构，将生成过程组织为封闭的“检索—反思—修订”循环。SRCoT 将环境状态、持久知识、累积经验和短期记忆分离到带有溯源标签的存储中。BM25 检索提供局部上下文；后续流程暴露中间草稿、缺失信息标记、后续查询以及可选的声明—证据映射。这些产物为检索、修订、澄清或弃答决策提供依据。我们通过两个完全合成场景中的定性、基于轨迹的案例研究来考查该架构：紧急医疗服务与 Munich-Sim（一种应急操作模拟）。在配备 128 GB 内存的 Apple M4 系列笔记本电脑上的本地原型运行展示了带来源标签的文档、显式缺口标记以及结构化调度风格的输出。这些轨迹还揭示了溯源分配错误、证据覆盖不完整以及尽管有提示约束仍可能出现的危险假设。这些案例证明了报告级别的可检查性与本地执行能力，而非比较优越性、幻觉减少、临床有效性或操作安全性。该架构将检索和控制与模型接口分离，但不同模型之间的行为仍有待评估。SRCoT 是一个需要人工审查的研究性概念验证，其主要贡献在于一种可检查的控制流程，而非新模型。

TLDR: SRCoT is an LLM-agent architecture for evidence-based text generation that uses a closed Retrieve-Reflect-Revise loop with provenance-labeled memory stores; it is demonstrated in synthetic emergency-medical simulations on an edge Apple M4 laptop, emphasizing inspectability and local execution rather than clinical superiority.

Authors: Fabian Broszio, Michael Hoffmann, Adrian Paschke

Conformal Policy Selection: When to Deploy AI Decisions for Targeted Intervention

Abstract (English): Artificial-intelligence (AI) policies are increasingly used to make consequential decisions in medicine, finance, education, and public services, yet deploying them responsibly requires knowing not only whether a policy helps on average, but for whom. Existing tools for off-policy evaluation and safe policy learning provide population-level guarantees, but some individuals may experience adverse treatment effects. We recast targeted deployment as a multiple-testing problem: for each candidate unit, test whether the outcome under the AI policy would exceed a user-specified threshold, and control the false discovery rate (FDR) among the selected set. Our method, Conformal Policy Selection, combines weighted conformal $p$-values that account for the distributional shift from behavior to target policy with a two-step selection-and-pruning procedure. We prove finite-sample FDR control and establish a doubly robust asymptotic guarantee, where FDR remains controlled if either the outcome model or the propensity model is correctly specified. Synthetic experiments verify these guarantees across four misspecification regimes, and a semi-synthetic study built on real electronic-health-record covariates illustrates how the same FDR control can act as a deployment safety gate, bounding the harmful treatments that a globally trained policy would otherwise administer to a high-risk subpopulation where it misgeneralizes. The framework offers a principled method for safe AI deployment in high-stakes applications.

摘要（中文）: 人工智能（AI）政策越来越多地被用于在医疗、金融、教育和公共服务领域做出具有重大影响的决策，但要负责任地部署这些政策，不仅需要知道政策在平均意义上是否有益，还需要知道它对哪些人群有益。现有的离线策略评估和安全策略学习工具提供了总体层面的保证，但某些个体可能会经历不利的治疗效果。我们将目标部署重新表述为一个多重检验问题：对每个候选单元，检验AI政策下的结果是否超过用户指定的阈值，并在所选集合中控制错误发现率（FDR）。我们的方法——保形策略选择（Conformal Policy Selection）——将考虑从行为策略到目标策略的分布偏移的加权保形\(p\)值与两步选择-剪枝程序相结合。我们证明了有限样本下的FDR控制，并建立了双重稳健的渐近保证：只要结果模型或倾向性模型中有任意一个被正确设定，FDR就能得到控制。合成实验在四种模型误设情形下验证了这些保证，而一项基于真实电子健康记录协变量的半合成研究则说明，同样的FDR控制如何作为部署安全门，限制一个全局训练的政策在高风险亚群中因错误泛化而本会实施的有害治疗。该框架为高风险应用中的安全AI部署提供了一种原则性方法。

TLDR: This paper proposes Conformal Policy Selection, a method that uses weighted conformal p-values and a selection-and-pruning procedure to decide which units should receive an AI policy's intervention, while controlling the false discovery rate under distribution shift and offering finite-sample and doubly robust guarantees.

Authors: Junyu Cao, Ruijiang Gao, Maytal Saar-Tsechansky, Mingzhang Yin

Enhancing RAG-based Medical VQA with Gated Multimodal Representations

Abstract (English): Medical Vision-Language Models (Med-LVLMs) have shown strong performance in Medical Visual Question Answering (MVQA), yet they may still produce factually inconsistent responses. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) can mitigate this issue by incorporating external clinical evidence; however, retrieval is often guided by a single modality, potentially overlooking the joint semantics of medical images and clinical questions. We introduce a multimodal RAG framework that learns a joint query representation through a Gated Multimodal Unit, enabling evidence retrieval to leverage information from both modalities while keeping the underlying Med-LVLM frozen. We evaluate the proposed approach on benchmark datasets. Results show that the multimodal query representation consistently outperforms a reference Med-LVLM and remains competitive with state-of-the-art methods. Our findings demonstrate the potential of multimodal query representations for more effective evidence retrieval in Medical VQA.

摘要（中文）: 医学视觉-语言模型（Med-LVLMs）在医学视觉问答（MVQA）中表现出强大性能，但仍可能产生与事实不一致的响应。检索增强生成（RAG）可通过引入外部临床证据来缓解这一问题；然而，检索通常由单一模态引导，可能忽略医学图像与临床问题的联合语义。我们提出一种多模态RAG框架，通过门控多模态单元学习联合查询表示，使证据检索能够利用来自两种模态的信息，同时保持底层Med-LVLM参数冻结。我们在基准数据集上评估了所提方法。结果表明，多模态查询表示始终优于参考Med-LVLM，并与最先进方法保持同等竞争力。我们的研究证明了多模态查询表示在医学VQA中实现更有效证据检索的潜力。

TLDR: Proposes a multimodal RAG framework for Medical VQA that fuses image and question embeddings via a Gated Multimodal Unit to retrieve clinical evidence while keeping the underlying Med-LVLM frozen; evaluated on benchmarks, showing gains over a reference Med-LVLM and competitiveness with state-of-the-art methods.
