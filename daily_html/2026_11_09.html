SurgWorld: Learning Surgical Representations Beyond Observation via Latent Prediction

Abstract (English): Surgical procedures are inherently partially observable, requiring surgeons to continuously infer evolving anatomical dynamics and future surgical states beyond the current observation. However, existing self-supervised learning methods primarily learn from observed surgical videos through masked reconstruction or predicting representations within the camera's current field of view, lacking the capability to explicitly model the out-of-sight surgical dynamics. Inspired by latent predictive world models, we introduce SurgWorld, a video-native, self-supervised framework for surgical representation learning, which treats a specified spatial or temporal shift as a pseudo-action and learns to predict the target latent representation from a partial observation. Specifically, we propose Out-of-Sight Dynamics Modeling (OSDM) as the pretraining objective for predicting representations conditioned on relative spatio-temporal shifts. We further regularize the learned latent dynamics from OSDM with a Geometric Visual Similarity (GVS) objective by aligning patch-level relational structures between predicted and target representations, preserving spatial geometry and local visual coherence for dense perception. These two objectives are integrated through an agglomerative training framework that consolidates complementary supervisory signals from multiple teachers to learn a unified latent representation of surgical dynamics and visual geometry. Extensive transfer learning experiments on 17 surgical benchmarks show that SurgWorld consistently outperforms existing vision foundation models across surgical workflow recognition, fine-grained action understanding, and dense visual perception. We further conduct tailored qualitative and quantitative analyses, demonstrating that SurgWorld effectively captures surgical dynamics beyond the directly observed context through latent prediction.

Remembering to Reason: Structured Memory for Scene and Workflow Understanding in Robot-Assisted Surgery

Abstract (English): Surgical video understanding is an important foundation for intelligent assistance in robot-assisted surgery. Similar surgical views can reflect different actions or procedural stages. Interpreting these views requires access to earlier observations, yet frame-centric prompting leaves this history unavailable. We introduce MemSurg, a framework that remembers surgical observations before reasoning over them. Its structured memory graph stores timestamped entity categories, locations, motion cues, and interaction summaries. Chronological links preserve recent context, while entity-associated links connect recurring observations across the procedure. Task-adaptive retrieval then supplies evidence-grounded prompts for scene and workflow reasoning, supporting both frozen inference and memory-conditioned supervised fine-tuning. Evaluation on robot-assisted prostatectomy and two complementary surgical benchmarks shows a 10.43-percentage-point gain over SurgRAW with GPT-4o. Memory conditioning adds 6.52 points under matched Qwen3-VL-8B fine-tuning, and an inference-time plug-in adds 7.04 points to EndoChat. These results support structured memory as a reusable source of procedural evidence for surgical video understanding. Our code will be publicly released.

DeepSoMo: Deformable Object Motion Estimation under Proprioceptive Partial Observability via Predictive Latent Dynamics

Abstract (English): Estimating the motion of deformable bodies in soft robotics from proprioceptive sensing is challenging due to partial observability, as internal observations provide only indirect access to global motion, contact, and shape evolution. In such settings, instantaneous observations are insufficient, and accurate estimation requires temporally consistent latent representations. This paper studies deformable motion estimation under proprioceptive partial observability from a latent dynamics perspective. We argue that a key requirement for long-horizon estimation is latent temporal consistency, where latent states remain predictive of future observations. To this end, we present DeepSoMo, a learning-based framework that enforces predictive structure in latent dynamics through a self-supervised latent prediction constraint. We evaluate the approach in a canonical deformable dynamics setting using high-fidelity finite-element simulation as a controlled source of ground truth. Results demonstrate consistent improvements in trajectory estimation, orientation recovery, shape reconstruction, and contact inference over architecture-matched recurrent baselines.

How to "train" a surgeon? Benchmarking generalist agents in surgical scene understanding

Abstract (English): Evaluating general-purpose multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for surgical scene understanding remains limited due to scarce surgical data and fragmented evaluation protocols. We address these challenges through a two-stage evaluation framework for surgical scene understanding. In Stage I, we introduce SurgKnowBench, a diagnostic benchmark that harmonizes public surgical datasets across procedures and tasks and evaluates proprietary and open-source MLLMs to map the boundary of pretrained surgical competence. SurgKnowBench reveals that current models retain partial visual priors but remain weak on relational and procedural understanding and workflow assessment. Building on this failure frontier and motivated by the scaffolded nature of surgical training, we introduce SurgAgentGym, an agentic environment where general-purpose MLLM agents can use bounded knowledge search over SurgWiki and general-purpose visual grounding for surgical tasks. SurgAgentGym evaluates whether generalist agents can improve surgical understanding at inference time through tool-mediated evidence use, without additional surgical training. Our results show that agentic inference narrows the gap mainly for perception-heavy tasks, while fine-grained localization and expert-style assessment remain persistent bottlenecks. This opens a path toward future studies of agentic surgical understanding.

AgentCT: A Vision-Language Agent for Universal CT Imaging

Abstract (English): Computed tomography (CT) restoration methods often use a single network for low-dose CT (LDCT), sparse-view CT (SVCT), and metal artifact reduction (MAR), despite the distinct physics of these degradations. We propose AgentCT, a vision-language agent that identifies the degradation and routes images to task-specific restoration models. A LoRA-adapted Qwen3-VL-4B controller predicts degradation type and severity, selects a specialist, and reassesses the restored image. The LDCT specialist uses an image-domain diffusion model, whereas the SVCT and MAR specialists combine projection-domain correction, data consistency, differentiable filtered back-projection, and image refinement. We evaluated the controller and specialists separately on patient-disjoint Mayo 2020 abdominal CT data across three severity levels for each degradation. The controller achieved 95.7% accuracy for degradation type and 93.9% for severity, with a 100% JSON parse rate. Using ground-truth degradation labels for routing, AgentCT achieved an overall PSNR/SSIM of 46.18 dB/0.984, compared with 44.93 dB/0.976 for AMIR and 44.29 dB/0.979 for TAMP, and attained the highest PSNR in all nine settings. For SVCT, its dual-domain specialist achieved 46.04 dB with 4.2 million parameters, compared with 44.43 dB for single-task AMIR and 43.57 dB for all-in-one AMIR with 23.5 million parameters. These results support degradation-specific CT restoration with controller-based model selection; evaluation under automatic routing and on independent clinical data remains necessary. Code is available at https://github.com/hao1635/AgentCT.

Task-adaptive anonymisation enables privacy-preserving sharing of clinical speech data

Abstract (English): Clinical speech is increasingly used for disease screening and clinical assessment, but directly distributing speech recordings poses privacy risks because they contain both speaker-identifying voiceprints and sensitive spoken content. Existing speech anonymisation approaches are primarily developed for general speech applications or task-specific clinical speech and overlook that different clinical tasks rely on distinct speech characteristics, necessitating task-adaptive anonymisation strategies to protect patient privacy while preserving clinical utility. Here we introduce CASA, a clinically adaptive speech anonymisation agent that transforms anonymisation from a fixed processing procedure into a task-guided optimisation process, dynamically tailoring voiceprint and content anonymisation to task-specific privacy and clinical utility requirements. Through recursive self-improvement (RSI), CASA iteratively evaluates and refines anonymisation strategies against task-specific privacy and clinical utility requirements, while learning from these outcomes to guide more efficient planning in subsequent clinical tasks. CASA is validated across 14 clinical tasks encompassing five languages, diverse elicitation paradigms, and mental health, cognitive and voice disorders. CASA preserved disease-related classification performance while meeting speaker-anonymisation requirements across all clinical tasks, versus only 8/14 for the strongest fixed method. Assessments of resistance to speaker re-identification by external attackers and identification and redaction of sensitive personal information in clinical speech further demonstrated CASA's dual protection of speaker identity and sensitive spoken content. Clinical expert assessments further supported the preservation of clinically relevant information and the acceptability of anonymised speech for controlled research sharing.

Conditional Diffusion-Based Proxy Representation Recovery for BCVA Prediction with Missing SLO Modality

Abstract (English): Preoperative prediction of postoperative best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) can support prognosis assessment and individualized decision-making in cataract surgery. Multimodal learning can integrate preoperative optical coherence tomography (OCT) images, scanning laser ophthalmoscopy (SLO) images, and clinical text to characterize patient status. However, SLO images are often missing in real-world clinical data due to acquisition or data-management issues, resulting in incomplete multimodal inputs. To address this problem, this paper proposes a conditional diffusion-based method for SLO proxy representation recovery and fusion prediction. Instead of reconstructing raw SLO images, the proposed method formulates SLO missingness as proxy representation recovery in the latent feature space. Specifically, OCT image features and clinical text features are first fused to construct an OCT-text conditional representation, which guides the reverse denoising process of the diffusion model to estimate sample-specific SLO proxy representations. The recovered SLO proxy representation is then used as a substitute for the unavailable SLO modality and incorporated into the downstream multimodal fusion prediction network for postoperative BCVA prediction. Experiments were conducted on 2,141 preoperative multimodal cataract samples, with an SLO missing rate of 58.80%. The proposed method achieved an accuracy of 0.9714 under the criterion of an absolute prediction error within 0.1 LogMAR and a mean absolute error of 0.0423, outperforming representative postoperative visual acuity prediction and missing-modality learning methods. These results indicate that representation-level proxy recovery can alleviate the impact of SLO modality missingness on postoperative BCVA prediction.
