Clinician-AI Collaboration in Endovascular Neurointerventions: In-Vitro User Study of Copilot Assistance Modes for Robotic Navigation Across the Autonomy Spectrum

Abstract (English): Mechanical thrombectomy (MT) is an effective treatment for acute ischemic stroke, yet access remains limited by the availability of neurointerventional expertise. Telerobotic and AI-assisted systems may help address this challenge, but it remains unclear how clinicians interact with different levels of autonomy and how autonomous navigation systems perform in realistic environments. This work introduces Copilot Assistance Modes (CAMs), a framework for clinician-AI collaboration in robotic endovascular navigation spanning the autonomy spectrum from manual operation to full autonomy. In the first in-vitro user study comparing clinician-AI collaboration strategies, 11 experts and two students evaluated six navigation modes in a patient-specific vascular phantom. Objective performance metrics were combined with subjective assessments, including workload and usability. Autonomous performance was additionally evaluated both in silico and in vitro to investigate sim-to-real transfer. Hands-on manual navigation achieved the strongest overall performance (100% success). While fully autonomous navigation achieved 81% success in simulation, performance dropped to 23% in vitro. In contrast, several clinician-AI shared-autonomy modes achieved success rates of 92-97%, approximately four times higher than full autonomy and comparable to direct robotic control (95%), demonstrating mitigation of sim-to-real domain shift through clinician-AI collaboration. Among the evaluated AI-driven approaches, user-directed task delegation and rotational shared control demonstrated the most favorable balance between performance and clinician acceptance. These findings suggest that effective clinician-AI collaboration and allocation of control authority may currently be more important for reliable robotic neurointerventional navigation than increasing autonomy alone.

Towards end-to-end co-design for one-shot printable task-specific soft robots

Abstract (English): Soft robot co-design methods can jointly optimise morphology and control, but the resulting designs are often difficult to manufacture, while manufacturable approaches typically restrict actuator placement or require manual assembly. We present a simulation-based co-design framework that jointly optimises material distribution, pneumatic actuator placement and orientation, and control, while producing designs that can be fabricated monolithically using fused deposition modelling. The framework uses a differentiable 2D plane-stress Material Point Method simulator, incorporating an experimentally validated material model and an empirically derived surrogate model of a 3D-printed inflatable actuator. We demonstrate gradient-based optimisation soft robotic arms for prescribed tip trajectories. Optimised designs are transferred directly from simulation to one-shot monolithic FDM fabrication, with integrated actuators and no post-assembly. Physical experiments demonstrate that the resulting arms reproduce the behaviour predicted in simulation. This framework provides a path from task-level objectives to physically realisable soft robots while retaining substantial freedom over both morphology and actuation.

SomaAlign: Reliability-Aware Multimodal Representation Learning for Soft Robotic Grasping

Abstract (English): Robust multimodal perception for soft robotic grasping is challenged by modality-dependent noise, semantic ambiguity, and limited coordinated multisensory data. We introduce Soma, a dataset containing 100 objects, 1,000 continuous grasping trajectories, and 74,914 multimodal samples grouped by software acquisition cycle, spanning remote RGB, hand RGB, depth, tactile, IMU, and structured text observations. We also collect Soma-Success, an independent grasp-outcome dataset with 1,000 trials (998 retained after quality control), to evaluate frozen-encoder transfer for post-hoc grasp outcome classification and early grasp success prediction. Built on Soma, SomaAlign learns multimodal representations in three stages: masked reconstruction estimates relative modality reliability; reliability-aware contrastive alignment uses pair-specific adaptive temperatures to adjust alignment sharpness; and conflict-aware semantic constraints improve intra-sample cross-modal consistency while preserving global feature geometry. SomaAlign achieves an average Recall@10 of 90.66% across 30 directed cross-modal retrieval tasks. The learned representations also support physical attribute recognition, multimodal question answering, cross-modal generation, and cross-dataset transfer to grasp outcome prediction. On Soma-Success, frozen SomaAlign encoders with lightweight probes achieve 94.59% post-hoc classification accuracy and 84.07% early prediction accuracy. Together, Soma, Soma-Success, and SomaAlign provide a data and representation-learning foundation for robust and transferable multimodal perception in soft robotic manipulation.

SurgPace: Complexity-Aware Progressive Distillation for Surgical Multimodal Large Language Models

Abstract (English): Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have shown strong potential in robotic surgery for question answering, scene understanding, and grounded assistance, but their high computational cost limits real clinical deployment. Knowledge distillation provides a practical way to build compact surgical MLLMs, yet direct distillation is not well suited to surgical data because surgical samples vary greatly in difficulty, visual ambiguity, and required clinical context, making uniform teacher-student transfer unstable. In addition, the role of supervision changes during training: early stages require stronger teacher guidance for multimodal alignment, while later stages rely more on task supervision for effective optimization. To address these challenges, we propose SurgPace, a complexity-aware continuous curriculum framework for progressive distillation of surgical vision-language models. SurgPace estimates sample complexity using predictive confidence and Lorentz geometric consistency, and uses the fused score to guide a continuous easy-to-hard training process. We further introduce hierarchical representation alignment, which aligns the student with the teacher at the feature, logit, and probability levels, while gradually shifting the training focus from teacher imitation to task supervision. This design separates sample-level curriculum from objective-level scheduling, leading to more stable and effective distillation. We evaluate SurgPace on Surg-396K, a benchmark built from four public surgical datasets and covering five dialogue paradigms. SurgPace consistently outperforms strong MLLM distillation baselines, demonstrating its effectiveness for building compact, memory-efficient surgical MLLMs with faster decoding under controlled benchmarks. Code is available at https://github.com/hezhicheng2002/SurgPace.

摘要（中文）: 多模态大语言模型（MLLMs）在机器人手术的问答、场景理解及 grounded 辅助任务中展现出强大潜力，但其高昂的计算成本限制了实际临床应用。知识蒸馏为构建紧凑型手术多模态大语言模型提供了一条实用途径，然而直接蒸馏并不适配手术数据，原因是手术样本在难度、视觉模糊性和所需临床上下文方面差异显著，导致统一的师生迁移过程不稳定。此外，监督信号在训练过程中的角色也在变化：早期阶段需要更强的教师引导以实现多模态对齐，而后期阶段则更依赖任务监督来进行有效优化。针对这些挑战，我们提出了 SurgPace，一种面向手术视觉语言模型渐进式蒸馏的复杂度感知连续课程学习框架。SurgPace 利用预测置信度与 Lorentz 几何一致性来估计样本复杂度，并使用融合得分引导连续的由易到难训练过程。我们进一步引入了层级表示对齐，在特征、logit 和概率三个层面使学生模型与教师模型对齐，同时逐步将训练重心从教师模仿转向任务监督。该设计将样本级课程与目标级调度分离，从而实现更稳定、更有效的蒸馏。我们在 Surg-396K 上评估了 SurgPace，该基准由四个公开手术数据集构建，涵盖五种对话范式。SurgPace 在强基线 MLLM 蒸馏方法上持续取得更优表现，验证了其在受控基准下构建紧凑、内存高效且解码更快的手术多模态大语言模型的有效性。代码已发布于 https://github.com/hezhicheng2002/SurgPace。

Interactive 3D Cutaway Visualization for Subsurface Target Localization in Robot-Assisted Surgery

Abstract (English): Purpose: Blended augmented reality (AR) overlays can provide ambiguous depth cues in which subsurface anatomy floats above the tissue surface. We investigate whether an interactive 3D cutaway visualization can improve robot-assisted target localization. Methods: We propose Virtual Box, an interactive 3D cutaway interface that allows users to independently manipulate the viewing perspective to inspect subsurface targets. In a controlled, counterbalanced within-subject tissue phantom study, 15 engineering students used Virtual Box and a depth-enhanced blended AR overlay to position a da Vinci instrument at virtual targets in a water tank. We used registered 3D transrectal ultrasound to measure 3D localization error and depth error. We also collected completion time and subjective ratings. Results: Virtual Box reduced mean 3D error from 12.92 to 4.96 mm (61.6% reduction) and depth error from 9.48 to 2.07 mm (78.2% reduction; both p < 0.001). Spatial understanding, depth perception, and confidence ratings were higher (all unadjusted p < 0.001), but completion time increased from 21.64 to 32.58 s (p = 0.008). Overall workload did not differ significantly. Conclusion: Virtual Box improved localization accuracy and perceived spatial understanding in this controlled task, with additional completion time, supporting further evaluation for surgical navigation.

Benchmark of THReD: Evaluating an LLM Agent and Its Preprocessing Tools for Turning Electronic Health Records into AI-Ready Datasets

Abstract (English): LLM agents are increasingly built into data-analysis software, yet little evidence shows how reliably they preprocess clinical data or whether the tools they invoke give correct, reproducible output. We report a benchmark of THReD, a web-based system that turns electronic health record (EHR) extracts into AI-ready datasets through a chat agent whose proposed steps run only after user confirmation. The agent benchmark has 141 tasks (100 templates) in seven categories, including goal-level requests, failure recovery, requests calling for no action, schema variation and prompt injection through the data, on synthetic tables calibrated to a regional health information exchange; success is judged by checks on the produced data. Over 2,550 episodes with Claude Haiku 5.5 in four context configurations, the deployed agent completed 92.7% (95% CI 88.1-96.4) of tasks on its first answer, abstained on every advice request, recovered from every injected fault, and never proposed an action requested by planted text. Without the data profile, completion fell to 58.0%; goal-level requests were hardest (80.6%). Six experiments on real records of two chronic-disease cohorts and on synthetic data at scale validate the tools: 91-92% of inferred patient classes and 99.7-99.8% of inferred vital-sign labels were correct in masked reconstruction; a dictionary builder combining curated dictionaries with LLM suggestions mapped 99.0% of 2,208 raw variants correctly; all 2,176 replays of 128 saved pipelines gave byte-identical files under pinned dependencies; subgroup differences in inference accuracy vanished within facility and lookup key; and the same-day consolidation rule changed 90-day emergency revisit rates by up to 3.2 points without shifting cohort composition by more than one point. Processing time grew roughly linearly to 5.5 million records while the prompt stayed at about 13 thousand tokens.

SMILE: A Structured Multi-Agent Clinical Reasoning for Liver Ultrasound Understanding

Abstract (English): Automated liver ultrasound report generation improves the diagnosis of liver diseases by producing clinically structured descriptions of anatomical and disease-related findings. However, current generative vision-language models (VLMs) often rely on a single reasoning pathway that maps ultrasound appearance directly to textual descriptions. In addition to the inherent vision-language modality gap, this design is limited for liver ultrasound because disease interpretation is constrained by subtle visual evidence, heterogeneous disease patterns, and limited paired supervision. To address these limitations, we propose SMILE, a Structured Multi-agent clInicaL rEasoning framework that uses multiple complementary specialist agents to interpret ultrasound images and consolidates their outputs through a judge-guided consensus mechanism.
