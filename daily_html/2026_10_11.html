Multi-modal Representation Fusion for Long-term Microsurgical Anastomosis Trajectory Anticipation

Abstract (English): Microsurgical anastomosis is one of the most challenging and sophisticated procedures for surgical robotics, requiring accurate manipulation of surgical instruments within a limited space to achieve precise operations. Accurate surgical trajectory anticipation is essential for surgical scene understanding, intraoperative planning, robotic manipulation, and reliable behaviour analysis. However, existing approaches mainly focus on features extracted from surgical videos and historical trajectories, while overlooking rich semantic and motion information that can provide more discriminative multi-modal representations for complex surgical instrument-tissue interactions. In this work, we propose a cross-modal feature fusion framework for long-term 2D and 3D microsurgical anastomosis trajectory anticipation. The proposed framework consists of a multi-modal representation extractor and a cross-modal feature fusion network. Semantic recognition of surgical objects and consecutive motion estimation are incorporated as multi-modal visual cues, providing complementary spatial and temporal guidance for instrument-tissue interaction characterization. A coarse-to-fine progressive fusion strategy with a long-short-term memory mechanism is introduced to capture temporal dependencies step by step. The training and evaluation are conducted on our established robotic platform and a comprehensive dataset containing both phantom and ex-vivo surgical scenarios is constructed. Extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework consistently outperforms existing methods and exhibits improved robustness across different surgical scenes, highlighting its potential for both intelligent robot-assisted and autonomous surgical systems.

Observation-Referenced Tissue Deformation Forecasting with Tool-Driven and Residual Dynamics

Abstract (English): Forecasting tissue deformation from future instrument motion could support predictive planning and increasingly autonomous surgical systems. We study this problem using a scene-specific differentiable surface model fitted to an observed tool-tissue interaction window. Rather than forecasting absolute simulated positions, we introduce an \textit{observation-referenced} formulation that anchors each tissue point to its last valid observation and adds only the deformation induced by the known, preplanned future tool trajectory. This reduces sensitivity to reconstruction, initialization, and persistent simulation offsets while preserving the predicted deformation response. The physical model uses differentiable, mesh-free position-based dynamics with scene-specific rigidity, attachment, region scale, and grasp coupling. We further introduce an optional \textit{observation-referenced residual model} combining graph-spectral spatial modes with periodic harmonic dynamics for structured motion not explained by the tool input. We evaluate the framework retrospectively on 12 surgical manipulation clips from three datasets. Observation referencing provides the largest gain, with a median within-clip error ratio of $0.598$ relative to the corresponding absolute simulation forecast. The optional periodic residual further improves forecasting when the remaining motion exhibits periodic structure. These results support observation-referenced deformation prediction as a practical building block for future surgical world models, digital twins, and generative simulators that must predict how tissue evolves under instrument actions.

Authors: Tom Olesch, Nan Xiao, Xiongye Xiao, Jonathan C. DeLong, Fei Liu

PC7 Model: Dynamics of Expanding and Contracting Soft Robots using Piece-wise Constant 7D Deformation Based on Similarity Transformation Group

Abstract (English): This paper proposes a novel soft robot model named piece-wise constant 7D deformation (PC7) model that can represent expanding and contracting deformation of, for example, artificial muscle actuator. In the proposed model, we newly define the 7D deformation vector based on SIM(3) - 3D similarity transformation group - whereas a 6D deformation vector was defined for Cosserat rod based on SE(3) - the special Euclid group - in the piece-wise constant strain (PCS) model proposed in the previous study. The additional dimension of the 7D deformation vector represents expanding and contracting deformation, which comes from the scaling parameter in SIM(3). Common mathematical properties between SE(3) and SIM(3) allow us to directly extend the kinematics and dynamics of the PCS model to those of the PC7 model. In particular, we derive the dynamical equation of the PC7 model based on Euler-Poincare equation in a mathematically precise manner. We validate the proposed model through dynamical simulations assuming a thin cylinder structure or McKibben-type artificial muscle actuator.

摘要（中文）: 本文提出了一种新颖的软体机器人模型，称为分段常数七维变形（PC7）模型，该模型能够表示例如人工肌肉执行器的膨胀与收缩变形。在所提出的模型中，我们基于SIM(3)——三维相似变换群——新定义了七维变形向量，而先前研究中提出的分段常数应变（PCS）模型则基于SE(3)——特殊欧几里得群——为Cosserat杆定义了六维变形向量。七维变形向量的额外维度表示膨胀与收缩变形，其来源于SIM(3)中的缩放参数。SE(3)与SIM(3)之间共有的数学性质使我们能够直接将PCS模型的运动学与动力学推广至PC7模型。特别地，我们基于欧拉-庞加莱方程以数学上精确的方式推导了PC7模型的动力学方程。我们通过假设薄圆柱结构或McKibben型人工肌肉执行器的动力学仿真来验证所提出的模型。

Spatiotemporal Recognition of Abdominal Wall Layers During Optical Trocar Entry

Abstract (English): Objective. To evaluate whether spatial context and temporal modeling improve tissue recognition during optical trocar entry, where multiple abdominal-wall tissues may be visible simultaneously. Methods. We retrospectively analyzed 71 recordings with 1,516 annotated keyframes and 29,151 clinician-annotated sectors. Each frame was divided into 28 polar-grid sectors assigned to nine tissue classes. Color, neighboring-sector, and positional features were used by a gradient-boosted emission classifier. The full pipeline added learned temporal smoothing, spatial regularization, an anatomically ordered hidden Markov model, and posterior blending. Five-fold cross-validation was grouped by recording, with cross-fitting to prevent within-recording leakage. Primary evaluation used clinician-derived targets; ablations assessed classification, probability quality, spatial consistency, and peritoneum-or-deeper onset timing. Results. The complete pipeline achieved accuracy 0.646, macro-F1 0.507, and macro-AUROC 0.882, versus 0.600, 0.440, and 0.861 for emissions alone. Macro-F1 improved by 0.066 across all five folds. Label changes decreased from 66.2 to 19.5 per sector-minute, and median onset error decreased from 15.58 to 2.45 seconds. Frames with a tissue-depth-code span of at least three decreased from 53% to 26%, versus 22% for clinician annotations. Performance remained limited for preperitoneal fat and bowel, and the full model retained a 44% premature-onset proportion. Conclusions. Combining appearance, spatial context, and temporal constraints improved retrospective tissue recognition and onset localization. Tissue-specific limitations, residual timing errors, and reliance on future observations warrant external validation and prospective evaluation of causal models before clinical use.

Modeling and Control of a Rigid-soft Hybrid-link Humanoid Robot

Abstract (English): Physical flexibility plays a significant role not only in human motions but also in robot motions. For example, a humanoid robot with passive and flexible elements, which can be regarded as a rigid--soft hybrid-link system, has the advantages of storing energy and mitigating impact in a dynamic motion. However, the motion design and control of a hybrid-link system are challenging to achieve and have only been discussed in the literature. In this study, we propose the framework of inverse kinematics and compliance optimization-based control for a hybrid-link humanoid system that represents the continuous deformation of a flexible structure using the piece-wise constant strain (PCS) model. The proposed method was validated using forward dynamics simulations for standing, jumping, and walking motions. Then, we built an experimental setup using a biped humanoid robot with leaf-spring type prosthesis leg, in which the prosthesis deformation was measured by a motion capture system. Using this setup, we experimentally validated the proposed method for standing balance control in both double- and single-support cases.

Toward System One Clinical Intelligence: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning and Counterfactual Explainability of Decision Foundation Models

Abstract (English): Automated disease classification from patient-reported symptoms requires balancing the speed of lightweight discriminative models against the contextual understanding and latency costs of autoregressive large language models (LLMs). Decision foundation models offer an alternative by mapping representations directly to candidate classes without free-form text generation. We adapt Clef-Flash, a 9-billion-parameter decision model built on a Qwen3.5 backbone, for 24-class disease classification on the Symptom2Disease benchmark. Using 4-bit quantized low-rank adaptation (QLoRA) with differential learning rates and a stratified split of 960 training and 240 test cases, the model achieves 95.42% diagnostic accuracy and a macro F1 score of 0.9551. This improves upon the reported zero-shot baselines of 61.67% for a remote API and 65.00% for a local unadapted initialization, and outperforms the ClinicalBERT baseline (90.90%). By evaluating candidate logits in a single forward pass without autoregressive decoding, inference takes under 80 milliseconds per record on a single GPU, yielding a reported 31-fold speedup over generative LLM baselines. To examine symptom-level evidence, we introduce a Leave-One-Symptom-Out (LOSO) counterfactual perturbation framework. Removing disease-defining symptom clauses in a psoriasis example reduces predicted-class confidence from 100.00% to 0.12% and shifts the prediction to impetigo with 99.59% confidence, while removing secondary systemic symptoms produces less than 0.01% change in confidence. These findings suggest that decision-based architectures can combine the linguistic capabilities of large foundation models with low-latency inference and symptom-level interpretability for clinical triage.

Authors: S. M. Mahbub Uddin Ahmed

Lingdan-V2: Large language models for clinically grounded reasoning in traditional Chinese medicine

Abstract (English): Large language models have advanced medical reasoning, but clinically grounded reasoning in traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) remains challenging because prescription decisions require integrating domain knowledge, syndrome differentiation, treatment principles, herbal prescription composition, and dosage determination. We develop a TCM language model family comprising models with 4 billion, 8 billion, and 14 billion parameters. The artificial intelligence contribution is a staged clinical alignment framework integrating large scale continued pretraining on TCM and clinical records, complementary reasoning supervision, and prescription-centered reinforcement learning (RL). We further formulate herbal prescription generation as a structured RL problem with prescription-level feedback, enabling direct optimization of clinical decision outputs beyond knowledge reproduction and generic instruction following. The engineering application is TCM clinical decision support, particularly herbal prescription recommendation. We evaluate the models on a public benchmark containing 25,620 expert-verified items across 13 TCM knowledge and clinical reasoning tasks and on a private retrospective dataset of 4,348 spleen and stomach disease prescription cases. The 14 billion parameter reinforcement aligned model achieves a prescription generation F1 of 37.0 on the public benchmark, compared with 32.3 for DeepSeek-R1 with 671 billion total parameters, and a herb-level F1 of 52.8 on the clinical dataset with improved dosage agreement. Reinforcement post-training consistently improves prescription recommendation across all scales, while physician-source analysis shows that expert heterogeneity affects reward stability. These findings demonstrate a scalable pathway from domain grounding and reasoning supervision to clinically oriented prescription decision support.

Text-Guided Medical Image Segmentation via Residual Aggregation and Dense Alignment Representations

Abstract (English): Specifying segmentation targets with natural language provides flexible semantic conditioning for medical image segmentation. However, existing methods still struggle to reliably ground high-level textual semantics into accurate pixel-level spatial prompts, especially under low-contrast lesions, ambiguous boundaries, and visually similar anatomical contexts. In this paper, we propose RADAR (Residual Aggregation and Dense Alignment Representations), a parameter-efficient framework for text-guided medical image segmentation. RADAR explicitly decouples and synergizes the medical semantic anchoring capability of BiomedCLIP with the prompt-driven mask decoding ability of MedSAM2, while enabling low-cost task adaptation via LoRA. Specifically, Guided Residual Depth Aggregation adaptively aggregates cross-layer features under semantic guidance to alleviate delayed textual injection and deep semantic dilution. Structured Dense Representation Alignment leverages a frozen BiomedCLIP to construct crop-level target, boundary, and context anchors, improving the local discriminability of dense features and the reliability of automatic prompts. Multi-modal Prompt Generation further converts cross-modal semantic responses into composite text, point, and mask prompts for MedSAM2 decoding. Experiments on three public medical image segmentation datasets show that RADAR achieves competitive performance with only 0.74M trainable parameters, demonstrating its effectiveness in parameter-efficient adaptation, semantic grounding, and dense representation enhancement. The source code is available at https://github.com/JGuiidev/RADAR.

ScienceBuddy-PV: A Verifier-Governed Recursive Improvement Protocol for Panvascular Intervention Research

Abstract (English): ScienceBuddy-PV adapts the ScienceBuddy recursive-in-recursive design to panvascular intervention research through a structured evidence ledger, device-vessel ontology, abstention contract and retained failure bank. In a constructed mask audit, it accepted 44.3% of reference candidates and rejected 55.7%, exposing the cost of abstention. Acceptance does not establish correctness or clinical safety. This public-data component audit makes support and abstention measurable and provides a reproducible protocol for future collaborative agent studies. It does not demonstrate recursive improvement, patient-level validity or clinical device recommendations.
