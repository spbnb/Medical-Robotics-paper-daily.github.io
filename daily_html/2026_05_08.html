Reconstruction or Semantics? What Makes a Latent Space Useful for Robotic World Models

Abstract (English): World model-based policy evaluation is a practical proxy for testing real-world robot control by rolling out candidate actions in action-conditioned video diffusion models. As these models increasingly adopt latent diffusion modeling (LDM), choosing the right latent space becomes critical. While the status quo uses autoencoding latent spaces like VAEs that are primarily trained for pixel reconstruction, recent work suggests benefits from pretrained encoders with representation-aligned semantic latent spaces. We systematically evaluate these latent spaces for action-conditioned LDM by comparing six reconstruction and semantic encoders to train world model variants under a fixed protocol on BridgeV2 dataset, and show effective world model training in high-dimensional representation spaces with and without dimension compression. We then propose three axes to assess robotic world model performance: visual fidelity, planning and downstream policy performance, and latent representation quality. Our results show visual fidelity alone is insufficient for world model selection. While reconstruction encoders like VAE and Cosmos achieve strong pixel-level scores, semantic encoders such as V-JEPA 2.1 (strongest overall on policy), Web-DINO, and SigLIP 2 generally excel across the other two axes at all model scales. Our study advocates semantic latent space as stronger foundation for policy-relevant robotics diffusion world models.

摘要（中文）: 基于世界模型的策略评估通过将候选动作在动作条件视频扩散模型中推演，为测试真实世界机器人控制提供了一种实用代理。随着这些模型日益采用潜在扩散模型，选择合适的潜在空间变得至关重要。虽然现状主要使用为像素重建而训练的自动编码潜在空间（如VAE），但最新研究表明，具有表征对齐语义潜在空间的预训练编码器能带来优势。我们通过在BridgeV2数据集上采用固定协议，比较六种重建与语义编码器来训练世界模型变体，系统评估了这些潜在空间在动作条件潜在扩散模型中的应用，并展示了在高维表征空间（无论是否进行维度压缩）中实现有效世界模型训练的可行性。进而提出评估机器人世界模型性能的三个维度：视觉保真度、规划与下游策略性能以及潜在表征质量。实验结果表明，仅凭视觉保真度不足以作为世界模型选择的依据。尽管VAE和Cosmos等重建编码器在像素级指标上表现优异，但V-JEPA 2.1（策略性能总体最优）、Web-DINO和SigLIP 2等语义编码器在所有模型规模上普遍在另外两个维度表现更优。本研究论证了语义潜在空间能为策略相关的机器人扩散世界模型提供更坚实的基础。

TLDR: This paper systematically evaluates whether reconstruction-focused or semantic latent spaces are more effective for training action-conditioned world models in robotics, finding that semantic encoders generally outperform on policy-relevant metrics despite weaker pixel-level reconstruction.

Structure-Preserving Gaussian Processes Via Discrete Euler-Lagrange Equations

Abstract (English): In this paper, we propose Lagrangian Gaussian Processes (LGPs) for probabilistic and data-efficient learning of dynamics via discrete forced Euler-Lagrange equations. Importantly, the geometric structure of the Lagrange-d'Alembert principle, which governs the motion of dynamical systems, is preserved by construction in the absence of external forces. This allows learning physically consistent models that overcome erroneous drift in the system's energy, thereby providing stable long-term predictions. At the core of our approach lie linear operators for Gaussian process conditioning, constructed from discrete forced Euler-Lagrange equations and variational discretization schemes. Thereby and unlike prior work, the method enables learning dynamics from discrete position snapshots, i.e., without access to a system's velocities or momenta. This is particularly relevant for a large class of practical scenarios where only position measurements are available, for instance, in motion capture or visual servoing applications. We demonstrate the data-efficiency and generalization capabilities of the LGPs in various synthetic and real-world case studies, including a real-world soft robot with hysteresis. The experimental results underscore that the LGPs learn physically consistent dynamics with uncertainty quantification solely from sparse positional data and enable stable long-term predictions.

It preserves geometric structure to ensure physical consistency, enabling stable long-term predictions and uncertainty quantification, which is demonstrated on synthetic and real-world systems, including a soft robot.
