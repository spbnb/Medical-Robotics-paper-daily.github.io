Robotic Optical Coherence Tomography-Guided Geometric Modeling of Soft Tissue: 3D Representations for Data-Driven Closed-Loop Shape Control

Abstract (English): In medical robotics, data-driven soft-tissue shape control requires deforming tissue toward desired morphologies through sequential physical interactions. This challenge is particularly important in microscale manipulation, where localized deformation is difficult to observe, represent, and control, while physics-based models can be computationally expensive and difficult to adapt to realistic environments. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) provides high-resolution volumetric perception of tissue deformation, but target-conditioned control requires effective representations of both observed and desired tissue states. In this work, we introduce an OCT-guided data-driven framework for tissue shape control, learned from user-guided demonstrations and executed with closed-loop OCT feedback. We study full-volume, tissue-masked sparse-volume, and voxelized point-cloud representations, with or without residual conditioning, using deterministic (Explicit) and generative diffusion (Diffusion) policies. Across 12 configurations and 576 phantom rollouts on 3-DoF depth and 6-DoF directional tasks, OCT-derived 3D representations support both Explicit and Diffusion policies, with performance depending on representation and residual conditioning. Residual conditioning reduces directional Shape RMSE from 0.748 to 0.555 mm for full-volume Diffusion, while voxelized point-cloud Explicit with residuals achieves 0.534 mm for depth and 0.443 mm for directional control, with comparable accuracy at intermediate unseen targets and consistent performance across deployments. We further evaluate the framework in 60 ex vivo porcine rollouts, extending to biological tissue with final geometric errors comparable to human demonstrations. These results establish volumetric OCT as current and goal representations for learned closed-loop tissue shape control across deterministic and generative action models.

摘要（中文）: 在医疗机器人领域，数据驱动的软组织形变控制需要通过连续的物理交互将组织变形至期望形态。这一挑战在微尺度操作中尤为重要，因为局部形变难以观察、表示和控制，而基于物理的模型可能计算成本高昂且难以适应真实环境。光学相干断层扫描（OCT）能够提供组织形变的高分辨率体积感知，但目标条件控制需要对观测到的和期望的组织状态进行有效表示。在本工作中，我们提出了一种OCT引导的数据驱动组织形变控制框架，该框架从用户引导的演示中学习，并通过闭环OCT反馈执行。我们研究了全体积、组织掩膜稀疏体积和体素化点云表示，在有或无残差条件的情况下，使用确定性（Explicit）策略和生成扩散（Diffusion）策略。在3自由度深度任务和6自由度方向任务上的12种配置和576次仿体验证中，OCT衍生的3D表示同时支持Explicit和Diffusion策略，其性能取决于表示方式和残差条件。残差条件将全体积Diffusion的方向形状均方根误差（Shape RMSE）从0.748 mm降至0.555 mm，而带残差的体素化点云Explicit在深度控制上达到0.534 mm，在方向控制上达到0.443 mm，在中间未见目标上具有相当的精度，且在不同部署中性能一致。我们进一步在60次离体猪组织验证中评估了该框架，将其扩展到生物组织，最终几何误差与人类演示相当。这些结果确立了体积OCT作为当前状态和目标状态的表示，用于跨确定性和生成性动作模型的闭环组织形变控制学习。

From Freehand Demonstrations to Autonomous Robotic Ultrasound: An Anatomy-Anchored Learning Framework

Abstract (English): Ultrasound imaging is highly operator-dependent, motivating robotic systems that reproduce expert scanning skills consistently. These skills involve coordinated interpretation of anatomical landmarks, ultrasound images, and probe-tissue interactions that are difficult to encode in hand-designed control strategies. Imitation learning offers an alternative, but its observation and action representations should reflect the anatomically structured nature of scanning. We propose an anatomy-anchored imitation-learning framework that combines anatomy-guided probe placement with learned ultrasound navigation. The placement module identifies a task-specific anatomical starting point from external visual observations and establishes the initial probe pose as the reference frame for scanning. A flow-based policy then generates scanning motions from anchor-relative probe poses, ultrasound images, probe-mounted camera views, and contact forces, while a termination head predicts task completion. This avoids continuous reliance on third-person vision and reduces dependence on the robot-patient configuration. A handheld acquisition platform enables multimodal demonstrations to be collected during natural freehand scanning without a robot. On 16 unseen participants, task success reaches 96%, 94%, 88%, and 94% for kidney, gallbladder, and liver standard-plane acquisition and radial artery sweeping, respectively. Compared with a fine-tuned pi_0 model, success on the abdominal tasks improves by 10-42 percentage points, with lower time-to-target, peak contact force, and trajectory jerk. Ablations support the complementary roles of anatomical anchoring, egocentric vision, and force feedback. End-to-end trials with automatic placement achieve 90-100% success, and the policies transfer without retraining to a second robot with 83-100% success.

MedCaseAgent: From Multimodal Clinical Evidence to Rare Disease Case Reports

Abstract (English): Although Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated potential in clinical documentation, generating the rigorous, evidence-based reasoning required for unprecedented, atypical, and rare disease case reports remains a significant challenge. We present MedCaseAgent, an end-to-end agentic reasoning paradigm that bridges this gap by synthesizing publication-quality reports from unstructured clinical notes. MedCaseAgent leverages a hierarchical structure, integrating patient-level data mining with iterative, tool-integrated reasoning. By dynamically querying medical literature and knowledge bases, the agent maintains high clinical fidelity and assesses case novelty. The framework is inherently modular, supporting various backbone LLMs in a plug-and-play fashion. To facilitate this research, we release MedCase-150K, a large-scale multimodal clinical case-report corpus, and MedCase-Bench, a robust temporally held-out evaluation suite. We also introduce an open-source model post-trained from Qwen3.5-9B and optimized specifically for this task. Under both LLM-judged and rule-based evaluation, MedCaseAgent outperforms existing general-purpose and specialized medical LLMs and agents. Blinded expert reviews and ablations further support the system's clinical relevance and evidence grounding.

The LLENS Platform for On-Premises Large Language Model Agents in a Japanese University Hospital: Development and Evaluation Study

Abstract (English): Large language model agents that use tools could take on part of the electronic health record and desk work that occupies physicians' time, but in many hospitals the medical information systems run inside closed networks, so the model and the agent harness must also sit inside. We designed, implemented, and deployed LLENS, a clinical support AI agent platform that runs inside the closed network of Hokkaido University Hospital on one host with 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs, the open-weight model Kimi K2.6 (~1 trillion parameters), and a web application rebuilt from Open WebUI. The harness has 10 components, including page image reading, full-text attachment input, a path-addressed document tree without a vector index, a network-isolated sandbox, artifact return, self-checking of generated output, subagents, and saved procedures. Kimi K2.6 was chosen from 3 open-weight candidates and scored 480/500 on the 2026 Japanese Medical Licensing Examination; it generated 119 tokens/s for one user and served 16 concurrent requests at 908 tokens/s in total. On 180 questions over hospital documents (3 seeds each), mean accuracy was 10.7% with no documents, 81.3% with fixed retrieval (top 10 chunks of one lexical search), and 97.2% with interactive access, in which the model reads with document tools and the sandbox (15.9 percentage points above fixed retrieval, 95% CI 10.9-21.3); the difference lay in filtering/counting questions (16.7% vs 87.8%). Five scenarios with synthetic data ran on combinations of the components: needlestick injury response, batch adverse event grading, scanned referral letter transcription, falls data aggregation and plotting, and investigation of 3 topics by subagents. The platform has run on the hospital network since September 14, 2026.

摘要（中文）: 摘要： 使用工具的大型语言模型智能体可以承担一部分电子病历和文书工作，从而节省医生的时间，但在许多医院中，医疗信息系统运行在封闭网络内，因此模型和智能体框架也必须部署在内部。我们设计、实现并部署了LLENS——一个临床支持AI智能体平台，它运行在北海道大学医院的封闭网络内，使用一台配备8块NVIDIA H200 GPU的主机、开放权重模型Kimi K2.6（约1万亿参数），以及基于Open WebUI重建的Web应用。该框架包含10个组件，包括页面图像读取、全文附件输入、无向量索引的路径寻址文档树、网络隔离沙箱、产物返回、生成输出的自检、子智能体和保存的流程。Kimi K2.6从3个开放权重候选模型中选出，在2026年日本医师资格考试中获得480/500分；它为单个用户生成119 tokens/s，并在16个并发请求下以总计908 tokens/s的速度提供服务。在针对医院文档的180个问题（每个3个种子）上，平均准确率在无文档时为10.7%，使用固定检索（一次词法搜索的前10个块）时为81.3%，使用交互式访问（模型通过文档工具和沙箱读取）时为97.2%，比固定检索高15.9个百分点（95% CI 10.9-21.3）；差异主要出现在过滤/计数类问题上（16.7% vs 87.8%）。使用合成数据在组件组合上运行了五个场景：针刺伤应对、批量不良事件分级、扫描转诊信转录、跌倒数据聚合与绘图，以及由子智能体对3个主题进行调查。该平台自2026年9月14日起在医院网络内运行。

LapaFM: A Laparoscopic Foundation Model via Multi-Stage Continual Learning

Abstract (English): Artificial intelligence-based analysis of intraoperative laparoscopic videos has the potential to improve surgical decision-making and precision. However, effective surgical assistance requires models that can generalize across diverse intraoperative tasks. Here we present LapaFM, a foundation model for laparoscopic surgery trained on more than 20,000 minutes of surgical videos using a multi-stage continual learning strategy that mitigates catastrophic forgetting and inter-task interference. LapaFM learns multimodal visual representations and supports multiple tasks, including smoke removal, surgical phase recognition, instrument and polyp segmentation, and lesion identification. The model was evaluated on 13 retrospective and two prospective cohorts collected from different imaging devices. LapaFM consistently outperformed task-specific models and existing foundation models across datasets, and also generalized to gastrointestinal endoscopy tasks, including polyp detection and classification. Notably, it recovered over 90% of smoke-obscured frames and achieved clinician-level or superior performance in lymph node metastasis prediction and prospective polyp classification.

ALLMTS: Automated LLM-based Medical Triage System

Abstract (English): Telephone triage is a safety critical task: from a brief intake note, a nurse must decide whether a caller needs an ambulance, an emergency department, an appointment, or self care at home. Queried directly, large language models (LLMs) are unreliable: they over-escalate routine cases and cannot justify their recommendations against an auditable clinical standard. We present the Automated LLM-based Medical Triage System (ALLMTS), which grounds every disposition in the same commercially maintained telehealth guidelines nurses use. Rather than asking an LLM to triage, ALLMTS makes it execute the guideline: it selects the appropriate guideline and evaluates its triage assessment questions. The disposition follows deterministically from the first positive answer, using the same traversal rule a nurse follows. We evaluate ALLMTS on 566 synthetic, expert-validated scenarios spanning 14 dispositions and designed to test specific guideline questions. ALLMTS achieves 0.95 accuracy, compared with 0.47 for a raw-LLM baseline. On a curated 50-scenario subset released as an open benchmark, ALLMTS reaches 0.98 accuracy, on par with the 0.944 correct disposition rate that a five nurse expert panel recorded on those same scenarios. Critically, on that subset the baselines have over-triage rates of up to 0.50 (0.44 on the full dataset), whereas ALLMTS reduces the over-triage rate to at most 0.02 while maintaining low under-triage. These results support coupling LLM language understanding with executable clinical guidelines for triage from intake notes.

Multimodal Generative AI Assists Comprehensive Echocardiographic Interpretation

Abstract (English): Echocardiography is the cornerstone of cardiovascular diagnostics, yet its global utility is constrained by a critical shortage of experienced interpreters. Current artificial intelligence (AI) solutions remain limited, typically operating as opaque "black boxes" or focusing on narrow tasks that fail to capture the holistic, guideline-driven reasoning of a clinician. Here we introduce EchoChat, a generative multimodal vision-language model that performs comprehensive echocardiographic analysis to support clinician interpretation.
