ClinVerse: A Realistic Multi-Agent Diagnostic Environment for Training and Evaluating Medical Agents

Abstract (English): Disease diagnosis is a key application of large language model (LLM) agents in healthcare. However, existing medical agents are primarily trained and evaluated with static question-answering data, providing limited opportunities to learn active information acquisition, examination decision-making, and dynamic clinical interaction. To address this limitation, we present ClinVerse, an interactive, real-data-grounded multi-agent environment designed for training and evaluating medical diagnostic agents. Built on ClinPatient, a scalable dataset comprising 150k diagnostic cases derived from real-world electronic health records, ClinVerse supports coordinated interactions among six specialized clinical roles. Specifically, a doctor agent starts from an initial patient complaint, progressively gathers clinical evidence through patient interviews and examination requests, and ultimately produces a diagnosis. For each interaction trajectory, a rule-based scoring framework quantifies information acquisition, examination ordering, and diagnostic accuracy. These trajectory-level scores serve as reward signals for agent training while also enabling scalable evaluation. In experiments, we evaluate various general-purpose and medical-domain models and show that nine self-evolution methods across three categories consistently improve agents' diagnostic capabilities within ClinVerse. We further analyze the interplay among model capability, environment feedback, and self-evolution methods.

摘要（中文）: 疾病诊断是大语言模型（LLM）智能体在医疗领域的一项关键应用。然而，现有医疗智能体主要基于静态问答数据进行训练和评估，缺乏主动获取信息、制定检查决策以及进行动态临床交互的学习机会。为解决这一局限，我们提出了ClinVerse，一个基于真实数据的交互式多智能体环境，专用于训练和评估医疗诊断智能体。ClinVerse构建于ClinPatient之上，后者是一个可扩展的数据集，包含15万例源自真实电子健康记录的诊断案例，支持六个专业临床角色之间的协同交互。具体而言，医生智能体从患者的初始主诉出发，通过患者访谈和检查请求逐步收集临床证据，并最终给出诊断。对于每条交互轨迹，一个基于规则的评分框架可量化信息获取、检查安排和诊断准确性。这些轨迹级分数既可作为智能体训练的奖励信号，也能支持可扩展的评估。在实验中，我们评估了多种通用型及医学领域专用模型，并表明ClinVerse中三大类共九种自我进化方法均能持续提升智能体的诊断能力。我们进一步分析了模型能力、环境反馈与自我进化方法之间的相互作用。

MedAdaptAgent: An Automatic Harness for Adapting Medical Imaging Models to New Sites

Abstract (English): Reusing pretrained medical-imaging models offers an alternative to training from scratch. A model trained for the same task may perform poorly at a new hospital because imaging protocols and preprocessing differ. Effective adaptation depends on the model architecture, domain shift, and available labels and compute. Adaptation can improve scores on a small labeled set yet reduce performance on unseen data. We present MedAdaptAgent, an automatic harness that turns a supplied model, target data, and a free-text request into a new-site adaptation workflow. Its LLM-driven Adaptation Planner uses model-site evidence to choose compatible experiments and revise subsequent adjustments. The harness requires evidence of improvement over the pretrained model, then delivers the selected model as a complete, runnable package. Evaluation spans 26 segmentation and classification transfers across architectures and compute settings, with segmentation annotation budgets ranging from zero-shot transfer to few-shot adaptation. With 20 local annotations, mean segmentation Dice rises from 0.483 to 0.774 across 10 transfers, a 60.1% relative gain, with no harmful replacements. Mean classification AUROC rises from 0.810 to 0.837 across 13 transfers, a 3.4% relative gain. Comparing adapted models with the pretrained model reduces harmful classification replacements by 30.8% versus selecting the highest-scoring model on the available labels, using identical candidates. Seven public-model deployments demonstrate adaptation and reproducible package delivery across contrast and modality shifts. A CT-to-MRI study extends the evaluation to four LLM planners, including two open-weight models. We release the code on GitHub.

Just-in-time volumetric imaging: Learning 2D-3D thoracic motion models during image-guided interventions

Abstract (English): Despite two decades of innovation, cutting-edge bronchoscopic techniques still fail to yield clear diagnostic decisions in 30% of cases and miss malignancies in 20% of cases. Intraprocedural 3D imaging drastically improves both tool positioning and diagnostic yield. However, each 3D spin represents a static snapshot of an anatomy that is continuously evolving as the patient is ventilated throughout the procedure. Here, we propose just-in-time volumetric imaging, in which a patient-specific 2D--3D motion model is trained on pre-intervention scans before it is needed and then supplies volumetric images from single fluoroscopic projections during sampling. Given the inspiration-hold CBCT already acquired for navigation and an additional motion-inclusive CBCT, our optimization regime named VoxelMap-DualScan (VM-DS), solves a low-rank motion model by alternating least squares and then amortizes it into a projection encoder trained on synthesized projections with known motion, all within a 10-minute window. On eight held-out cases from the public DIR-Lab 4DCT dataset, VM-DS achieved a mean landmark target registration error of 2.8 mm, a structural similarity index of 0.95 and a peak signal-to-noise ratio of 37.6 dB. Accuracy was maintained when reducing the number of projections used for training from 227 to 57. Inference takes 1.5 ms per projection, making the framework suitable for real-time use during image-guided interventions. Code will be made available at https://github.com/Image-X-Institute/Voxelmap/tree/VM-DS

AskGLP1: A Knowledge Graph LLM Agent for Exploring GLP-1 Experience from Online Patient Narratives

Abstract (English): GLP-1 medications are increasingly used for weight management, and many people share their experiences online. These narratives show why people take the drugs and what they experience, but they remain unstructured and disconnected from formal pharmacovigilance. We therefore present AskGLP1, a system that transforms online patient narratives into a knowledge graph (KG) and answers questions about medication experiences. Through an LLM-assisted pipeline grounded in the MedDRA terminology, AskGLP1 screens nearly 8.5 million Reddit posts for medication experiences and distills 163,135 of them, with 2,506 WebMD reviews, into a KG of 385,790 drug-side-effect and drug-purpose relations. We find that GLP-1 experiences are source- and drug-specific. Online narratives share the most common gastrointestinal symptoms with FAERS, but they foreground everyday burdens such as fatigue and food aversion, whereas FAERS reports more often include injection-site reactions and selected serious diagnoses such as pancreatitis. We further find that posts report less nausea and vomiting for tirzepatide than for semaglutide, more fatigue for Wegovy than Ozempic and for Zepbound than Mounjaro. Evaluations show that the agent answers with verifiable evidence and declines requests for medical advice. Our findings reveal gaps between what FAERS captures and what people report online, and they call for patient-centered approaches to pharmacovigilance. Code, prompts, and evaluation questions are available at https://github.com/ZhaoqianXue/AskGLP1.

GRASP: Group-Adaptive Hybrid RL-SFT Post-Training for Medical VQA without Reasoning Annotations

Abstract (English): Adapting general-purpose vision-language models (VLMs) to medical visual question answering (VQA) is valuable for helping clinicians read images and make diagnoses, but annotated data are scarce and provide only short answers, without reasoning annotations. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) fits these labels but overfits to them and amplifies their biases, eroding out-of-domain (OOD) generalization. Reinforcement learning (RL) better preserves generalization but never uses the gold answer as a training target: GRPO-style algorithms learn from the contrast between correct and incorrect answers within a group of sampled responses, so all-correct or all-wrong groups give no correctness signal. Existing SFT-RL hybrids either apply SFT and RL in separate stages or switch between them per question, or need reasoning traces from experts or stronger models, which medical VQA data lack. We propose GRASP, which combines RL and SFT in every update using only short answers: RL lets the model explore its own reasoning, SFT teaches the gold answer after that reasoning to supply the direct supervision RL lacks, and their balance adapts to how well the model answers each question. Tuning only about 1% of the parameters with LoRA, GRASP outperforms all compared training objectives both in domain and on OOD open-ended questions across Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3-8B and Gemma 3-4B. On an OOD benchmark spanning eight imaging modalities, GRASP-trained InternVL3-8B and Qwen3-VL-8B beat all 10 compared general and medical VLMs in open-ended accuracy, including models trained on up to 2,600 times more data or with over 250 times more trainable parameters. We release MedTerse, a dataset of 12.9K short-answer questions, and our code at https://github.com/sea-comet/GRASP-MedVQA.

Shifted Window Temporal Transformer for YOLO-Guided Phase Recognition in Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy Surgery

Abstract (English): Intelligent operating rooms require accurate awareness of surgical workflow for better intra-operative decisions, teamwork, and surgical documentation. Therefore, accurately recognizing surgical phase during surgery is crucial. However, previous artificial intelligence-based methods provide fluctuating predictions for the transitions, restricting their clinical use. To address this challenge, we propose a clinically meaningful framework that integrates a Swin Transformer and Temporal Transformer Encoder (TTE) for effective surgical phase recognition and a parallel YOLOv11s network for real-time surgical instrument localization. To avoid phase flickering and to produce stable workflow predictions, a causal temporal smoothing is applied. The proposed framework is tested on the Cholec80 dataset for the simultaneous recognition of the surgical phases and instruments. The overall phase recognition accuracy of this model was 92%, and the macro average F1-score was 0.86. This performance surpasses CNN based methods such as EndoNet (81.7%) and SV-RCNet (85.3%), as well as temporal convolutional and transformer-based approaches including TeCNO (88.6%), Trans-SVNet (90.9%), and LoViT (91.5%). Besides, the module of instrument detection achieved mAP@0.5 = 0.863. The reliability of the results is confirmed, obtaining a high recognition rate of the workflows, and a high accuracy of the localization of the instruments. The proposed framework can serve as a foundation for intelligent surgical assistance, enriching situation awareness, and potential robotic surgery applications. Code will be made publicly available at: https://github.com/ac005sheekar/LapChol-Surgery-Swin-Cholec80

Rethinking Data Imbalance in Class Incremental Surgical Instrument Segmentation

Abstract (English): In surgical instrument segmentation, the increasing variety of instruments over time poses a significant challenge for existing neural networks, as they are unable to effectively learn such incremental tasks and suffer from catastrophic forgetting. When learning new data, the model experiences a sharp performance drop on previously learned data. Although several continual learning methods have been proposed for incremental understanding tasks in surgical scenarios, the issue of data imbalance often leads to a strong bias in the segmentation head, resulting in poor performance. Abstract (English): Estimating forces acting on Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs) is challenging due to their underactuated nature. However, reliable contact estimation is crucial to precisely control them and enable safe manipulation tasks. Differently from the various approaches presented in the literature, this work adopts a probabilistic formulation where the DLO state and sensor data are related through conditional distributions. In particular, factor graphs are used to determine the magnitude, direction, and location of a 3D force acting on a DLO, while simultaneously reconstructing its shape. Since DLOs are not actuated and are typically thin, like cables and wires, strain gauges and actuation readings are usually impractical. Therefore, to overcome this issue, this work is tested using one type of sensing information only, thus developing a generic technique suited for a wider variety of tasks.
