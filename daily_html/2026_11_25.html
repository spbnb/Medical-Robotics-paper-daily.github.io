TACTUS: Unifying Surgical Action Understanding in Time and Space

Abstract (English): Advancing surgical automation requires a comprehensive spatio-temporal understanding of how instruments act on tissue throughout an action. Existing surgical benchmarks, however, largely define supervision through task-specific targets, leaving the relationships between action semantics, instrument motion, and tissue interaction incompletely specified. To address this limitation, we introduce TACTUS (Tool, Action, Contact and Trajectory Understanding in Surgery), a new dataset and benchmark that establishes a unified annotation framework for complete surgical actions. The framework jointly specifies tool and action identities, nested action and interaction windows, instrument-specific landmark trajectories, and tissue-region tracks. To construct these annotations at scale, we develop a two-tier pipeline combining human annotation, point tracking, and model-generated spatial prompts. Using this pipeline, we re-annotate 141 videos from five public sources, producing 22,995 action clips spanning 2.9 million frames. The dataset comprises 15,668 Gold clips with human spatial prompts followed by review and re-checking, and 7,327 Silver clips combining human semantic and temporal annotations with model-generated spatial prompts. We further introduce TactusNet, the companion reference network, to benchmark semantic, temporal, and spatial prediction from shared video features. Across TactusNet and 13 published baselines, tool-action accuracy reaches 95.8% with per-head encoder fine-tuning, while the best temporal recall and contact Dice reach 46.6% at temporal IoU 0.7 and 0.351, respectively. Moreover, adding Silver improves the default TactusNet's pair accuracy from 93.8% to 95.5%. Together, these contributions establish a foundation for learning and evaluating the instrument-tissue interactions underlying future surgical automation.

BendGS: Observation-Conditioned Gaussian Self-Modeling for Continuum Robots

Abstract (English): Visual self-models learn from images how a robot moves under motor commands. Continuum robots need them even more, as they bend continuously without measured joint angles or fixed link geometry. Existing continuum self-models predict point clouds, image-space curves, or future images, but not the next 3D shape, appearance, and tip pose together from an observed body. 3D Gaussians offer a renderable representation of shape and appearance, yet articulated Gaussian self-models rely on joint angles to drive deformation. In this work, we propose BendGS, which uses a virtual backbone estimated from observation to drive command-conditioned Gaussian deformation, enabling next-state prediction for continuum robots without measured joint angles. In Gaussian modeling, a feed-forward network reconstructs the robot as 3D Gaussians from multi-view images, and the backbone, a chain of coordinate frames, is fitted to back-projected depth points to estimate the bending state. In transition, a network predicts its change under a relative motor command to deform the bound Gaussians. The deformed Gaussians render the next images and the 3D shape, and the distal frame gives the tip pose. Training losses use RGB-D images and masks, without bending or tip labels. On simulated and real robots, BendGS achieves the lowest Chamfer distance, best image quality, and lowest tip errors among the compared methods.

摘要（中文）: 视觉自我模型从图像中学习机器人在电机指令下如何运动。连续体机器人更需要这种模型，因为它们在弯曲时没有测量到的关节角度或固定的连杆几何。现有的连续体自我模型能够预测点云、图像空间曲线或未来图像，但不能同时从观测到的机体预测下一时刻的3D形状、外观和末端姿态。三维高斯提供了一种可渲染的形状与外观表示，然而关节式高斯自我模型依赖关节角度来驱动变形。在本工作中，我们提出BendGS，它利用从观测中估计的虚拟骨架来驱动指令条件的高斯变形，从而实现对无测量关节角度的连续体机器人的下一状态预测。在高斯建模中，前馈网络从多视角图像中将机器人重建为三维高斯，而骨架——一条坐标帧链——被拟合到反投影的深度点上以估计弯曲状态。在状态转移中，网络预测其在相对电机指令下的变化，以变形所绑定的高斯。变形后的高斯渲染出下一图像和3D形状，末端帧给出末端姿态。训练损失使用RGB-D图像和掩码，无需弯曲或末端标签。在仿真和真实机器人上，BendGS在对比方法中取得了最低的Chamfer距离、最佳的图像质量和最低的末端误差。

TLDR: BendGS introduces a Gaussian-based visual self-model for continuum robots that estimates a virtual backbone from observations and deforms 3D Gaussians under motor commands to predict the next shape, appearance, and tip pose from RGB-D images without measured joint angles.

GeoSurge: Geometry-Grounded Multimodal Reasoning for Auditable Liver Resection Planning

Abstract (English): Surgical resection planning centers on generating executable resection surfaces from anatomical evidence and operative constraints. Medical multimodal foundation models enable anatomical understanding, and segmentation models predict resection regions, but neither translates these capabilities into explicitly constrained, executable resection surfaces. We present GeoSurge, an anatomy-grounded framework that combines mask-derived metrics with structure-aware point-cloud tokens to generate executable, editable resection surfaces. To make resection geometry learnable and editable, GeoSurge maps actions to parametric geometry through a surface-action grammar. Using this grammar, anatomy-oriented and tumor-centered templates generate candidates, which are filtered by hard constraints and semantic deduplication. Training supervision is then derived from candidate-expert geometric agreement and independently recomputed feasibility metrics. At inference, the reasoner uses patient evidence and surgical requirements to select or revise an action, which an independent geometry engine subsequently materializes and verifies. GeoSurge achieves the best overall scores on DeepTumour Liver and CRLM-QA, improving the constraint satisfaction rate from 73% to 100% on CRLM and from 56% to 99% on GeoSurge-Bench.

摘要（中文）: 外科手术切除规划的核心在于根据解剖学证据和手术约束生成可执行的切除面。医疗多模态基础模型能够实现解剖学理解，分割模型可以预测切除区域，但二者均无法将这些能力转化为明确约束下的可执行切除面。我们提出GeoSurge，一种基于解剖学约束的框架，结合掩膜派生度量与结构感知点云令牌，生成可执行、可编辑的切除面。为使切除几何结构可学习且可编辑，GeoSurge通过表面动作语法将动作映射为参数化几何结构。基于该语法，面向解剖学和肿瘤中心的模板生成候选方案，并通过硬约束和语义去重进行筛选。随后，训练监督信号来自候选方案与专家几何结构的一致性以及独立重新计算的可行性指标。在推理阶段，推理器利用患者证据和手术要求选择或修订动作，独立的几何引擎随后将该动作具体化并验证。GeoSurge在DeepTumour Liver和CRLM-QA上取得了最佳综合成绩，将CRLM上的约束满足率从73%提升至100%，在GeoSurge-Bench上从56%提升至99%。

TLDR: GeoSurge presents an anatomy-grounded multimodal reasoning framework for liver resection planning that maps surgical actions to parametric resection surfaces via a surface-action grammar, then filters, supervises, and verifies candidates with geometric constraints; it improves constraint satisfaction substantially on CRLM and GeoSurge-Bench.

Refining and Aligning Clinical Reasoning with Health Guidelines via Formal Constraints

Abstract (English): Clinical reasoning from incomplete or conflicting patient evidence must account for low-probability but high-risk concerns while remaining grounded in health guidelines. We introduce VerClin, an explainable framework that extracts guideline conditions, exclusions, recommendations, and priorities into executable knowledge and represents patient conversations as scoped cases. A deterministic four-valued reasoner produces traceable guideline matches while preserving uncertainty and enforcing exclusions. Multiple specialty agents then screen matched guidelines for case applicability, and a consultation model synthesizes accepted guidance through specialty recommendations and a sentence-preserving formal-constraint channel. We present accepted guidance in ascending order of the sum of its priority and trigger-strength ranks, which can surface high-risk guidance with weaker triggers. VerClin outperforms direct generation and Top-5 RAG on HealthBench Hard and HealthBench Professional. On the LLMEval-Med subset, it outperforms direct generation and ties Top-5 RAG. Component analyses further examine semantic closure, example-guided refinement, specialty screening, and constraint-conditioned delivery.

SurgGenesis: A Generative Surgical World Model for Future-Aware Surgical Understanding

Abstract (English): Surgical scene understanding is constrained by data scarcity and long-tail class imbalance, as annotating surgical videos demands scarce clinical expertise while rare procedural events remain underrepresented. We tackle this through data generation and propose SurgGenesis, a generative surgical world model that learns future surgical state transitions in a latent space from visual observations and textual procedural descriptions, serving as a scalable surgical data engine. To adapt a large-scale video diffusion model to the data-scarce surgical domain without catastrophic forgetting, we introduce a three-stage progressive LoRA strategy that incrementally injects endoscopic visual priors, procedural semantics, and spatial motion cues. Rather than judging generation by visual fidelity alone, we validate SurgGenesis along two axes that separate a world model from a generic generator: (i) future-awareness, by comparing predicted futures against the real future through a GT-latent prediction gap and temporal-horizon analysis, and (ii) action-faithful usefulness, by checking that generated clips contain the commanded surgical triplet and that using them to rebalance long-tail events and drive downstream understanding (segmentation, triplet recognition, 3D reconstruction) yields action-specific gains. Conditioned futures therefore complement conventional long-tail sampling. Code: https://zh-qr.github.io/SurgGenesis

TANDEM: Temporal Anchor Densification With Reliability-Aware Memory for Lightweight Sparse Depth Completion in Endoscopic Robotic Navigation

Abstract (English): For endoscopic robotic surgery, accurate dense depth perception serves as a foundational capability, which enables navigation, instrument localization, and tissue interaction. However, endoscopic video lacks reliable metric depth, while metric measurements obtained via tracking, time-of-flight sensing, or instrument contact only cover a small fraction of pixels in each frame, rendering sparse depth completion an urgent and unsolved challenge. In this work, we propose Temporal Anchor Densification with Reliability-Aware Memory (TANDEM), a causal sparse depth completion framework for endoscopic video. It adopts a lightweight design and leverages the video stream: past measurements re-projected into the current view recover the metric evidence unavailable within a single sparse frame. To keep this evidence interpretable, accurate, and efficient, an explicit memory scores each re-projected measurement with analytic re-observation, age, and motion-parallax factors without overriding current measurements. A compact network then reconstructs depth via reliability-weighted gradient integration and a frequency-gated reference prior. We evaluate TANDEM on the C3VD colonoscopy and StereoMIS laparoscopy datasets. With only 5.58 M parameters, TANDEM reduces the root mean square error by 11.7% and 32.0%, respectively, compared with the strongest baseline, and achieves state-of-the-art accuracy. It also remains accurate under pose errors of the magnitude reported for endoscopic pose estimation. Code will be released at https://github.com/Wk-cpu-art/TANDEM upon acceptance.

Self-Sensing Compliant Anthropomorphic Hand for Speed-Dictated Shared Force Control in Teleoperation

Abstract (English): In vision-based teleoperation of robotic hands, the operator receives no haptic feedback and therefore struggles to regulate the grasp force, while adding tactile sensors to the fingers increases wiring, cost and integration complexity. Compliant hands offer an alternative, as their mechanical compliance, usually treated only as a passive property for safe and adaptive grasping, can also serve as a source of information. Here, we show that the elastic transmission of a compliant, tendon-driven anthropomorphic hand can itself act as the sensor for shared-control telemanipulation. Series springs in the tendon routing are read by potentiometers in a forearm-mounted motor box, from which the hand estimates its joint states and finger forces without any external sensors. Building on this self-sensing, we develop a contact-aware shared-control framework for VR-based teleoperation. The robot hand is controlled through a mapping from the operator's hand kinematics until a contact is detected proprioceptively. Control then switches to a finger-level force controller whose set point is determined by the operator's grasping speed, and control authority can be allocated to individual fingers. Experiments validate the system's force estimation and increased grasp success rate compared with direct teleoperation for fragile and heavy objects. These results show that compliance can serve not only for passive adaptation but also as an intrinsic sensing resource for physical human-robot collaboration.

Generating Schema-Aligned Synthetic Dialogues and Measuring Their Diversity

Abstract (English): Training or evaluating a model that turns a conversation into a structured record requires paired data, meaning a transcript together with the exact record it should be converted into. Such pairs are expensive to annotate, and in sensitive domains they are also difficult to obtain at all. We therefore build them in the opposite order by sampling a schema-valid record first, then writing a dialogue from it, and optionally synthesizing speech from the dialogue, so the label exists before the text and we need no annotation step. Naively prompting a language model for such dialogues yields small variations of a single conversation, so we add a sampled variation configuration, personas, and a topic plan that orders the fields of the record as explicit mechanisms for variation. We measure the variation that reaches the output through embedding space, where the embedding variance spreads over about two and a half times as many independent directions with our full configuration compared to without (participation ratio 23.3 against 9.2). Two corpora of medical dialogue give values of the same order, with sample size, text length and language being additional factors there. We also show the positive effect of our method in a downstream test, where an 8B extraction model reaches 64.8% field accuracy when fine-tuned on dialogues from our method, against 57.5% from naive generation. We release the generation library and the generated corpus of German clinical dialogues with aligned records.

Seeing to Drive on a Single Board: Attention-Free Depth for Onboard Robot Navigation

Abstract (English): A small ground robot can carry one camera and a few watts of compute, but the depth foundation models that could turn that camera into a range sensor are vision transformers sized for GPUs. We bring one to a Raspberry Pi 5 with the Hailo-8L, an accelerator that runs every layer in integer arithmetic, attention included. Quantised for it, Depth Anything V2 loses large parts of the scene, and we measure why: at 449 tokens, 91% of softmax weights fall below one int8 step while carrying 17-33% of the attention mass. Our answer is to distil, not quantise. An attention-free ResNet-34 U-Net student of Depth Anything V2-Large (24.5M parameters) runs at 27.1 FPS on the chip, its int8 output matches its float twin (Pearson 0.99993), and on held-out scenes of its simulated yard it is the most accurate depth network on the chip (AbsRel 0.269 against 0.354-0.530). On 1,000 stereo-verified points of a real outdoor drive it is not resolvably different from the vendor's Depth Anything V2-Small build, while running faster (27.1 vs 21.6 FPS). Ranging obstacles by where they meet the floor needs only the camera height, so mapping, hybrid-A* planning and SLAM run on the Pi's own CPU. In real-time hardware-in-the-loop tests in a Gaussian-splat reconstruction of a real yard, the stack reaches the goal without contact in 7.9$\pm$1.1 of 10 scenes, against 9.2$\pm$0.4 with exact obstacles, and in 7.7$\pm$0.7 on its own pose with simulated wheel odometry (ten seeds each; 8.2$\pm$0.4 with the real Pi and Hailo-8L in the loop, five seeds). The source code, pre-trained models and data are available at https://github.com/itaim18/DANIEL.
