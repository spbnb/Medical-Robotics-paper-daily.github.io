A Model-based Visual Contact Localization and Force Sensing System for Compliant Robotic Grippers

Abstract (English): Grasp force estimation can help prevent robots from damaging delicate objects during manipulation and improve learning-based robotic control. Integrating force sensing into deformable grippers negotiates trade-offs in cost, complexity, mechanical robustness, and performance. With the growing integration of RGB-D wrist cameras into robotic systems for control purposes, camera-based techniques are a promising solution for indirect visual force estimation. Current approaches mostly utilize end-to-end deep learning, which can be brittle when generalizing to new scenarios, while existing model-based approaches are unsuited to grasping and modern grasper geometries. To address these challenges, we developed a model-based visual force sensing approach integrating an iterative contact localization with generalization to unseen objects. The system extracts structural key points from wrist camera RGB-D images of deforming fin-ray-shaped soft grippers, and uses these key points to define parameters of an inverse finite element analysis simulation in Simulation Open Framework Architecture. The iterative contact localization sub-system utilizes a deep learning-based online 3D reconstruction and pose estimation pipeline to dynamically update contact location, and is robust to visual occlusion and unseen objects. Our system demonstrated an average root mean square error of 0.23 N and normalized root mean square deviation of 2.11% during the load phase, and 0.48 N and 4.34% over the entire grasping process when interacting with different objects under various conditions, showcasing its potential for real-time model-based indirect force sensing of soft grippers.

Multi-frame Restoration for High-rate Lissajous Confocal Laser Endomicroscopy

Abstract (English): Lissajous confocal laser endomicroscopy (CLE) is a promising solution for high speed in vivo optical biopsy for handheld scenarios. However, Lissajous scanning traces a resonant trajectory and samples only the visited pixels per frame; at high frame rates, many pixels remain unvisited, creating structured holes. In this work, we introduce the first benchmark for high-rate Lissajous CLE, consisting of low-quality video clips paired with high-quality reference images. The reference images are wide-FOV mosaics obtained by stitching stabilized, slow-scan frames of the same tissue, enabling temporally aligned supervision. Using this dataset, we propose MIRA, a lightweight recurrent framework for Lissajous CLE restoration that iteratively aggregates temporal context through feature reuse and displacement alignment. Our experiments demonstrate that MIRA outperforms both lightweight and high-complexity baselines in restoration quality while maintaining a favorable computational efficiency suitable for clinical deployment.

摘要（中文）: Lissajous共聚焦激光内镜显微镜(CLE)是一种有望实现手持场景下高速活体光学活检的解决方案。然而，Lissajous扫描呈现共振轨迹，每帧仅对访问过的像素进行采样；在高帧率下，许多像素未被访问，形成结构化缺失。本研究提出了首个高帧率Lissajous CLE基准数据集，由低质量视频片段与高质量参考图像配对组成。参考图像为通过对同一组织进行稳定慢速扫描并拼接而获得的宽视野马赛克图像，实现了时间对齐的监督。利用该数据集，本研究提出了MIRA——一个用于Lissajous CLE图像重建的轻量级循环框架，通过特征复用和位移对齐迭代聚合时间上下文信息。实验表明，MIRA在重建质量上优于轻量级和高复杂度基线方法，同时保持适合临床部署的计算效率。

TLDR: A new benchmark dataset and a lightweight recurrent network (MIRA) to restore high-rate Lissajous confocal laser endomicroscopy images by aggregating temporal context.

简要说明: 提出首个高帧率Lissajous共聚焦激光内窥镜成像的基准数据集，并设计轻量级循环网络MIRA，通过时间上下文聚合实现图像修复。
