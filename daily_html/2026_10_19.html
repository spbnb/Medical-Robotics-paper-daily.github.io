ClinSDT: LLM-Encoded Clinical Semantic Guidance for Sepsis Treatment via Decision Transformer

Abstract (English): Offline sepsis treatment aims to learn treatment policies from retrospective ICU trajectories. Decision Transformer-based methods provide a natural sequence modeling framework for this task, but they mainly rely on raw physiological states and historical clinician actions. Such raw trajectory tokens may not explicitly capture clinical semantics that clinicians use in practice, such as patient severity, recent treatment response, and current risk priorities. To address this limitation, we propose ClinSDT, an LLM-assisted Decision Transformer framework for offline sepsis treatment. ClinSDT uses a frozen LLM as a semantic bridge rather than a treatment decision maker. It constructs three clinical semantic views from ICU trajectories, namely Profile, Context, and Advisory, and inserts their encoded representations as independent tokens into a Decision Transformer policy. To support policy refinement and rollout, ClinSDT further trains a Treatment-Response Forecaster to estimate the next physiological state, SAPS-II change, and predictive uncertainty from state-action inputs. In the second training stage, the forecaster screens local counterfactual treatment candidates, and only low-uncertainty candidates with lower predicted SAPS-II increase are distilled into the policy after semantic regeneration. During inference, ClinSDT performs closed-loop treatment rollout by refreshing semantic inputs from the currently available patient state. Experiments on MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV show that ClinSDT achieves consistent relative improvements over representative imitation learning, offline reinforcement learning, and Decision Transformer baselines under the same rollout protocol. Code and data are available at https://github.com/KAG778/ClinSDT.

摘要（中文）: 离线脓毒症治疗旨在从回顾性ICU轨迹中学习治疗策略。基于决策Transformer的方法为这一任务提供了自然的序列建模框架，但它们主要依赖原始生理状态和历史临床医生动作。此类原始轨迹标记可能无法显式捕捉临床医生在实践中使用的临床语义，例如患者严重程度、近期治疗反应和当前风险优先级。为解决这一局限，我们提出ClinSDT，一种用于离线脓毒症治疗的LLM辅助决策Transformer框架。ClinSDT使用冻结的LLM作为语义桥梁而非治疗决策者。它从ICU轨迹中构建三种临床语义视图，即Profile、Context和Advisory，并将其编码表示作为独立标记插入决策Transformer策略中。为支持策略细化和 rollout，ClinSDT进一步训练一个治疗反应预测器，以从状态-动作输入估计下一生理状态、SAPS-II变化和预测不确定性。在第二阶段训练中，预测器筛选局部反事实治疗候选，并且仅将低不确定性且预测SAPS-II增幅较低的候选在语义重新生成后蒸馏到策略中。在推理期间，ClinSDT通过从当前可用的患者状态刷新语义输入来执行闭环治疗 rollout。在MIMIC-III和MIMIC-IV上的实验表明，在相同 rollout 协议下，ClinSDT相对于代表性的模仿学习、离线强化学习和决策Transformer基线取得了一致的相对改进。代码和数据可在 https://github.com/KAG778/ClinSDT 获取。

TLDR: ClinSDT uses a frozen LLM to generate clinical semantic tokens (Profile, Context, Advisory) that guide a Decision Transformer for offline sepsis treatment, and adds a Treatment-Response Forecaster for counterfactual policy refinement and closed-loop rollout, achieving consistent improvements on MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV.

CasePlay: Self-Play Reinforcement Learning from Case Reports for Medical Reasoning

Abstract (English): Reinforcement learning (RL) is a promising way to improve the clinical reasoning abilities of large language models (LLMs), but current pipelines often rely on expert-labeled questions or costly synthetic-data generation. Meanwhile, the medical literature offers a wealth of high-quality unlabeled documents, including clinical case reports that trace the full clinical course from initial presentation to final treatment decisions. We study whether document-grounded self-play can convert these reports into effective RL training signals. We identify two challenges that limit existing self-play methods in this setting: knowledge-point fixation}, where the proposer repeatedly asks about a narrow set of salient clues in a source report, and difficulty-quality mismatch, where appropriately difficult questions may still be poorly formed or shallow, limiting their value for reasoner training. To address these challenges, we propose CasePlay, a self-play framework with two key components: (a) a Knowledge-Conditioned Proposer, which anchors question generation to case-specific knowledge points so that questions draw on a broader range of evidence from the source report; and (b) a Rubric-Judge Reasoner, which augments answer-accuracy feedback with rubric-based quality feedback on clue integration, reasoning depth, and option quality, guiding the proposer toward reasoning-intensive questions that better support reasoner training. Extensive experiments across seven medically related benchmarks demonstrate that CasePlay outperforms existing self-play baselines and improves the SFT warm-start model by 3.1 points on average, highlighted by a 6.3-point gain on LiveClin. This approach offers an effective path toward continually improving medical reasoning ability through self-play RL.

摘要（中文）: 强化学习（RL）是提升大语言模型（LLMs）临床推理能力的一种有前景的方法，但当前的流程通常依赖专家标注的问题或成本高昂的合成数据生成。与此同时，医学文献提供了大量高质量的无标注文档，包括从初始表现到最终治疗决策的完整临床过程的病例报告。我们研究文档驱动的自博弈能否将这些报告转化为有效的强化学习训练信号。我们识别出两种限制现有自博弈方法在此场景下应用的挑战：**知识点固定**——提问者反复询问源报告中少数显著线索；以及**难度-质量不匹配**——难度适当的问题仍可能构造不佳或流于表面，从而限制了其对推理者训练的价值。为应对这些挑战，我们提出 **CasePlay**，一种包含两个关键组件的自博弈框架：(a) **知识条件化提问者（Knowledge-Conditioned Proposer）**，将问题生成锚定到案例特定知识点上，使问题能够从源报告中更广泛的证据中取材；(b) **量规评判推理者（Rubric-Judge Reasoner）**，在答案准确性反馈的基础上，增加基于量规的线索整合、推理深度和选项质量反馈，引导提问者生成更具推理强度的问题，从而更好地支持推理者训练。在七个医学相关基准上的大量实验表明，CasePlay 优于现有自博弈基线，并使监督微调热启动模型平均提升 3.1 分，其中在 LiveClin 上获得 6.3 分的显著提升。该方法提供了一条通过自博弈强化学习持续提升医学推理能力的有效途径。

TLDR: CasePlay introduces a self-play RL framework that turns clinical case reports into training signals for medical reasoning LLMs, addressing knowledge-point fixation and difficulty-quality mismatch with a knowledge-conditioned proposer and a rubric-judge reasoner, and improves performance across seven medical benchmarks.

SurGPT: A Multi-Task Foundation Model for Robotic Surgical Skills Assessment and Gesture Recognition

Abstract (English): The assessment of surgical skills in robotic surgery training has traditionally relied on subjective evaluations by human experts, creating bottlenecks in surgical education and limiting scalability. We present a novel foundation model that automatically analyzes robotic surgical videos to provide comprehensive skill assessment and gesture understanding. Combining a visual encoder with a transformer architecture, the model performs three key tasks simultaneously: 1) surgical task classification (knot tying, needle passing, suturing), 2) skill level assessment on a standardized scale, and 3) natural language generation of surgical gesture descriptions. The model processes video sequences from the da Vinci surgical robot and generates human-readable text descriptions of surgical actions, similar to how language models generate text from prompts, using a multi-task learning framework with adaptive uncertainty weighting to optimize performance across all objectives. Our transformer-based architecture incorporates positional encoding for temporal understanding and attention mechanisms for spatiotemporal feature integration. Evaluated on the da Vinci Skills Simulator dataset, the proposed framework demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in surgical task recognition and provides interpretable skill assessments that correlate with expert evaluations, with the model's ability to generate natural language descriptions of surgical gestures opening new possibilities for automated surgical education, real-time feedback systems, and objective skill assessment in surgical training and certification programs.

NeuroAct: Advancing EEG Analysis and Decoding via Dual-Loop Autonomous Research

Abstract (English): EEG analysis and decoding underpin brain-computer interfaces, cognitive assessment, and sleep monitoring. Fixed pipelines and reward-driven search do not explicitly determine how signal structure, representations, predictor behavior, and training dynamics contribute to performance or generalization gaps, leaving intervention choice underdetermined. We introduce NeuroAct, an autonomous research agent framework for EEG analysis and decoding. NeuroAct implements dual-loop autonomous research across EEG foundation models, task-specific predictors, and model combinations. In an inner loop, research, development, and review roles use evidence on signal structure, representations, predictor behavior, and training dynamics to test hypotheses, select analyses or interventions, and refine candidates using performance, stability, and cost feedback. An outer loop distills outcomes into conditional, testable recommendations with task and model conditions, then validates and revises them on subsequent tasks.We evaluate four settings: (A) EEG General Analysis, (B) Cross-task Model Optimization, (C) Single-task Open Research, and (D) Collaborative Prediction. In Setting A, NeuroAct's 80.88 macro-average exceeds BrainAgent's 63.13 by 17.75 points. In Setting B, NeuroAct raises EEGPT's mean balanced accuracy (BA) by 7.34 points and HBN Pearson's (r) from 0.6894 to 0.7362; in Setting C, it improves ADFTD BA by 16.33 points; and in Setting D, it raises Workload BA from 79.69% to 85.02%. Experience reuse raises BCIC-IV-2a BA from 34.66% to 50.81% while reducing LLM tokens by 64.02%, indicating recommendations improve cross-task performance. NeuroAct enables evidence-guided EEG research improved by cross-task experience.

TLDR: NeuroAct is an autonomous dual-loop research agent framework for EEG analysis and decoding, using evidence-guided hypothesis testing and cross-task experience reuse to outperform existing pipelines across multiple EEG benchmarks.

Persistent Representational Misalignment Induces Vision-Language Model Failures

Abstract (English): Vision-language models (VLMs) fail at some tasks that are simple for humans---but why? We hypothesize that many such failures stem from VLMs' difficulties translating between vision and language representations, a misalignment the Platonic Representation Hypothesis predicts should disappear with scale. This paper is composed of two parts: in the first, we show that existing vision and language representations are linearly unalignable. This misalignment is an inherent property of the data and hence expected to persist regardless of either scale or the alignment's complexity. In the second, we demonstrate how this misalignment both leads to a predictably induced generalization failure and correlates with existing failure modes, where more representationally aligned VLM backbones accordingly exhibit fewer failures. Overall, these results imply representations will not become aligned simply given sufficient scale, providing evidence against a strong version of the Platonic Representation Hypothesis.

摘要（中文）: 视觉-语言模型（VLMs）在一些对人类而言十分简单的任务上会失败——但原因何在？我们假设，许多此类失败源于VLM在视觉与语言表征之间转换的困难，这种错位按柏拉图表征假说的预测应随规模增大而消失。本文由两部分组成：第一部分，我们表明现有的视觉与语言表征在线性意义上不可对齐。这种错位是数据本身的固有属性，因此无论规模大小或对齐复杂度如何，预计都会持续存在。第二部分，我们展示了这种错位如何既导致可预测的归纳泛化失败，又与现有的失败模式相关联，其中表征对齐程度更高的VLM骨干网络相应地表现出更少的失败。总体而言，这些结果表明，仅靠足够的规模并不能使表征变得对齐，这为反对强版本的柏拉图表征假说提供了证据。

TLDR: Argues that vision-language model failures persist because visual and language representations are inherently linearly unalignable, contradicting the strong Platonic Representation Hypothesis; the resulting misalignment predicts generalization failures, and more aligned VLM backbones exhibit fewer failures.

简要说明: 认为视觉语言模型的失败源于视觉与语言表征本质上无法线性对齐，且这种不对齐是数据固有属性，不会随规模增大而消失；实验表明该不对齐可预测泛化失败，且表征更对齐的模型失败更少。
