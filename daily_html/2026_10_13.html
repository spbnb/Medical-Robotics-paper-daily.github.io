ProCARE: Real-World Study Automation via Profile-Grounded Evidence Contracts

Abstract (English): Real-world studies (RWS) analyze routinely collected healthcare data, such as electronic health records and insurance claims, to estimate treatment effects, monitor safety, and identify risk factors outside randomized trials. Automating RWS with large language model agents is appealing but brittle: valid analyses depend on dataset-specific clinical semantics, including index dates, follow-up windows, patient/event-level units, and outcome boundaries that are often implicit in observational data. This leads to schema mismatches, temporal errors, inconsistent analytical granularity, and unsupported clinical claims. We present ProCARE, a profile-grounded framework that reformulates RWS automation as constrained compilation. ProCARE converts heterogeneous data profiles into Evidence Contracts specifying available variables, temporal attributes, distributions, and evidentiary limits, and compiles study objectives into RWS-IR, a strongly typed intermediate representation for variables, time relations, analysis units, and statistical tasks. Deterministic validation and localized repair then check and revise generated workflows across schema mapping, cohort construction, variable derivation, analysis, and interpretation. On 36 EHR-based benchmark tasks, ProCARE outperforms strong LLM-agent baselines in code executability, semantic consistency, and conclusion traceability, with automated violation diagnoses showing moderate-to-substantial agreement with blinded expert review.

摘要（中文）: 摘要： 真实世界研究（RWS）利用常规收集的医疗健康数据（如电子健康记录和保险索赔数据）来评估治疗效果、监测安全性，并识别随机试验之外的危险因素。使用大语言模型智能体来自动化真实世界研究颇具吸引力，但也很脆弱：有效的分析依赖于数据集特定的临床语义，包括索引日期、随访窗口、患者/事件层面的分析单位以及结局界定——这些在观察性数据中往往是隐含的。这会导致模式不匹配、时间错误、分析粒度不一致以及缺乏支持的临床论断。我们提出了ProCARE，一个基于数据画像的框架，将真实世界研究自动化重新构建为约束编译问题。ProCARE将异构数据画像转换为证据契约（Evidence Contracts），明确指定可用变量、时间属性、分布及证据限度，并将研究目标编译为RWS-IR——一种针对变量、时间关系、分析单位和统计任务的强类型中间表示。随后，确定性验证与局部修复机制对生成的工作流进行跨模式映射、队列构建、变量衍生、分析和解释等环节的检查与修订。在36项基于电子健康记录的基准任务上，ProCARE在代码可执行性、语义一致性和结论可溯源性方面均优于强基线大语言模型智能体方法，其自动化违规诊断与盲法专家评审结果具有中等至较高程度的一致性。

TLDR: ProCARE is a profile-grounded framework for automating real-world clinical studies from EHR/claims data; it converts heterogeneous data profiles into Evidence Contracts and compiles study objectives into a typed intermediate representation (RWS-IR) with deterministic validation and repair, outperforming LLM-agent baselines on 36 benchmark tasks.

X-Ragent: Tool-Augmented Reinforcement Learning for Evidence-Grounded Chest X-Ray Reasoning

Abstract (English): Chest X-ray (CXR) interpretation often requires integrating visual findings with task-specific evidence rather than relying on direct visual recognition alone. While tool-augmented medical agents can supply such evidence via external modules, they often lack explicit mechanisms for learning when auxiliary evidence is needed and for preventing inapplicable quantitative evidence from entering diagnostic reasoning. To address this, we propose X-Ragent, a post-training framework for tool-augmented reinforcement learning that decouples learned auxiliary-evidence seeking from validity-gated quantitative measurement acquisition for evidence-grounded CXR reasoning. We construct Rad-CoT, a 7.7K-sample corpus combining causally ordered tool-augmented trajectories with report-style diagnostic reasoning, and use it to initialize Qwen3-VL-8B via agentic supervised fine-tuning (SFT). We then optimize the policy with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using an accuracy-gated reward that applies process-level terms for measurement consistency and auxiliary-tool cost only to correct trajectories, preventing plausible process behavior from compensating for an incorrect diagnosis. Across three CXR benchmarks, X-Ragent achieves the strongest results, including an 8.6-point improvement on the fully held-out ChestAgentBench. By seeking evidence selectively and admitting only valid measurements, X-Ragent supports more faithful and evidence-grounded CXR reasoning.

PathPilot: An Adaptive Question-Conditioned Multi-Magnification Navigation Agent for Whole-Slide Image Analysis

Abstract (English): The diagnostic capability of WSI agents relies on effective slide navigation to identify diagnostically relevant regions and efficiently integrate evidence across the slide. While in clinical practice, pathologists dynamically examine slides across multiple scales, integrating global and cellular evidence in a sequential manner, existing AI navigation approaches mostly operate at a single fixed magnification or rely on predefined magnification traversal. This mismatch prevents existing methods from modeling cross-magnification interactions and adaptive magnification selection inherent to real diagnostic workflows. We propose PathPilot, a clinically consistent multi-magnification WSI navigation agent that explicitly models cross-magnification localization and evidence-adaptive exploration. Specifically, we introduce a CrossMagLoc that learns magnification-specific diagnostic localization from pathologist viewing behavior and integrates information from adjacent magnifications to identify the diagnostically most relevant region within the current ROI, thereby providing a diagnostically informed starting point for subsequent question-conditioned exploration. We further introduce an EviRegionSearch that starts question-conditioned exploration from this region, progressively accumulating question-relevant evidence and adaptively determining the next navigation action according to the remaining evidence requirement. Their iterative interaction enables PathPilot to sequentially explore across ROIs and magnifications until sufficient evidence is acquired. Extensive QA evaluations across five cohorts demonstrate consistent superiority over competing SOTA methods on inter/external cohorts, respectively, validating question-conditioned evidence acquisition across regions and magnifications that mirrors the sequential, knowledge-driven navigation of pathologists.

Dense Endoluminal Mapping and Localisation from Monocular Bronchoscopy

Abstract (English): Bronchoscopy inserts a camera through the airways to diagnose endoluminal disease. Locating the camera along the airway usually requires additional equipment, such as an electromagnetic tracker and a preoperative CT scan. In this paper, we investigate the use of the monocular camera stream to localise the bronchoscope without requiring additional sensing. We introduce a monocular SLAM system that localises the tip of a bronchoscope and reconstructs the airway surface around it from RGB video alone. A Depth Anything 3 backbone, fine-tuned on over a million photorealistic airway renderings, returns metric per-pixel depth and is the system's only source of scale. DINOv3 features routed through a custom cross-attention matcher supply sub-pixel correspondences on mucosa where classical descriptors fail. Both enter a factor graph alongside a dense constraint from projective iterative closest point (ICP) registration against the system's own running reconstruction, so surface evidence stabilises the directions the visual factors leave weakly observable. On six airways rendered from patient CT scans, the system reaches 26.1 mm mean absolute trajectory error and 77.5% mean surface F-score. Its mean surface accuracy exceeds that of five recent monocular SLAM and feed-forward baselines. MASt3R-SLAM, which fits a free scale to the ground truth, has a lower mean trajectory error (22.0 mm). ORB-SLAM3 completes only two of the six airways, whereas our system completes all six and also reconstructs two ex vivo specimens.

GUIDE: Computationally Augmented Consensus for Trustworthy Clinical Practice Guidelines

Abstract (English): Maintaining up-to-date clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) is essential for evidence-based medicine, but rapid evidence growth and complex expert consensus formation limit timely updates. We developed GUIDE, a multi-agent copilot system for human-AI collaborative guideline generation and updating. GUIDE delegates labor-intensive tasks to specialized AI agents while preserving expert judgment and final arbitration. In 12 NICE ophthalmology datasets, GUIDE achieved 88.85% literature recall, 86.06% alignment in certainty-of-evidence ratings, and 91.67% recommendation agreement, requiring 162.17 minutes per dataset on average (28.68% human intervention). Performance remained stable across 20 additional datasets from cardiology, endocrinology, gastroenterology, and respiratory medicine. Ablation analysis showed that removing expert intervention reduced performance in judgment-intensive steps such as Risk-of-Bias assessment. In six post-cutoff datasets, GUIDE maintained robust end-to-end performance on unseen evidence. Prospective real-world validation showed that GUIDE reduced expert workload and produced more clinically acceptable recommendations than the original decade-old guideline (mean score, 3.73 vs. 1.53; agreement, 66.7% vs. 13.3%), with outcomes comparable to human-only updating (P = 0.588) but substantially less time and effort. GUIDE offers a practical framework for timely, trustworthy, expert-centric guideline development and validates computationally augmented consensus.

REFLEX: A Learned On-Board Reflex for Robust Autonomous Collision Recovery of Soft Quadrotors

Abstract (English): Collision-resilient airframes allow quadrotors to survive impacts, but do not ensure autonomous post-collision recovery. Innovation gating in stock onboard estimators often rejects valid external velocity measurements after high-speed impacts, compromising recovery. We present REFLEX, a collision-recovery flight mode that runs entirely on the flight controller, requiring neither contact sensors nor a companion computer. Its proprioceptive recurrent policy stabilizes the vehicle without position, velocity, heading, or altitude estimates. The translational estimate is reinitialized while REFLEX is active, and a trust gate checks estimator consistency before returning control to the stock autopilot. The policy is trained in simulation using proximal policy optimization, with episodes initialized from post-impact states measured in 47 real collisions. In hardware experiments, REFLEX recovered 19 of 22 wall impacts at 5.03 $\pm$ 0.09 m/s, compared with 1 of 14 for the matched stock autopilot. These included 15 consecutive recoveries in a single flight. A separate exploratory flight demonstrated two additional recoveries at 6.02 and 6.05 m/s within the same flight, exceeding the previously reported velocity for autonomous wall-collision recovery by 64%.

PatientWM: Towards Generalizable Clinical World Models via Unified Patient States

Abstract (English): Clinical decisions depend on anticipating how a patient's condition will evolve as treatment is adjusted and new evidence becomes available. Existing medical world models capture patient-state evolution, but often define the evolving state around a particular modality or task, limiting reuse as inputs or prediction targets change. These models also retain history through observation sequences or recurrent or latent summaries rather than exposing preceding patient states and clinical actions as an ordered transition context. This distinction matters because similar current measurements may follow different recent changes, treatment exposures, and observed responses. To tackle these limitations, we propose PatientWM, a clinical world-model framework built around a unified patient state and sequential latent history. Availability-aware encoders integrate observations available at each decision time into the patient state, while typed decoders produce task-specific clinical readouts without defining the state around any single target. PatientWM further conditions each next-state prediction on the current state and supplied action together with a bounded, ordered history of preceding latent patient states and clinical actions, preserving recent treatment and response context. Experiments across MIMIC-III, MIMIC-IV, and three external clinical datasets show superior performance in clinical prediction, multi-step forecasting, and action-planning evaluation. Transfer experiments further demonstrate the generalizability of PatientWM's patient state. Together, these results establish its ability to forecast clinical evolution and support planning.
