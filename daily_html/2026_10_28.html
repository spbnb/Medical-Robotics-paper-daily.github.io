MCP-Net: Real-Time Coronary Perception Network and Topological Analysis for Robotic Guidewire Navigation

Abstract (English): Coronary guidewire navigation during percutaneous coronary intervention requires continuous interpretation of vessel anatomy, guidewire position, and target stenosis under two-dimensional X-ray fluoroscopy. While existing approaches commonly address vessel segmentation or device localization as separate tasks, a robotic system requires a more structured perception of the coronary scene to support downstream navigation. In this work, we present a simulation-driven framework centered on a Multi-Task Coronary Perception Network (MCP-Net) for robotic guidewire navigation, integrating coronary vessel segmentation, vascular topology extraction, and roadmap generation. Starting from three-dimensional coronary CT angiography, we generate simulated fluoroscopy images containing vascular structures, simulated guidewires, stenotic lesions with varying severity, and diverse vascular geometries, together with simulation-derived ground truth. MCP-Net jointly performs coronary vessel segmentation, guidewire-tip localization, and stenosis localization from a single fluoroscopic frame. These predictions are subsequently converted into a structured vascular graph containing centerlines, bifurcations, endpoints, branch directions, and branch angles. By associating the predicted stenosis with this graph, the framework generates a roadmap from the automatically selected graph root toward the target lesion. The results demonstrate that the proposed framework can jointly recover coronary perception targets and transform them into an interpretable vascular representation suitable for downstream robotic navigation.

State estimation for a hybrid rigid-continuum space manipulator system

Abstract (English): Spacecraft-mounted robotic manipulators can support long-term operability of orbiting assets by conducting tasks such as on-orbit assembly, repair, upgrade, and refueling. In this context, the integration of continuum/soft robotic mechanisms into space manipulator designs promises to reduce stowage volume and mass, as well as to improve operational safety through the inherent mechanical compliance of soft structures. This work presents a novel state reconstruction pipeline for a hybrid soft-rigid space manipulator, in which the distal link is a tendon-driven continuum robot. The estimation problem is formulated as a maximum a posteriori (MAP) inference problem over the hybrid manipulator's configuration, conditioned on discrete, noisy measurements from joint encoders and a vision-based sensing pipeline. The resulting soft-constrained optimization problem is transcribed as a factor graph, providing a compact representation for fusing heterogeneous measurements with the rigid-soft kinematic model. Furthermore, the adoption of the Geometric Variable Strain (GVS) approach yields a minimum-order parameterization of the configuration, a property expected to facilitate model-based feedback control design in future work. The proposed implementation is validated in simulation on a closed-loop trajectory where, for better realism, position measurements for the continuum link are obtained by means of a simulated visual pipeline leveraging synthetic photorealistic images. The state reconstruction accuracy and computational performance confirm the potential of the proposed framework for hybrid soft-rigid robotic applications in orbit.

Authors: Lorenzo Ticozzi, Patricio A. Vela, Panagiotis Tsiotras

From Biological Curves to Soft Modular Robots: Learning-Based Inverse Design with Uncertainty-Guided Refinement

Abstract (English): Animals exhibit diverse shape-changing behaviors, from the undulatory swimming of eels to the arching movements of inchworms. Reproducing these biological shapes with modular soft robots offers a promising approach to developing versatile bioinspired robotic systems. However, translating a desired shape into a feasible robotic configuration remains challenging. Existing cable-driven modular soft robots rely on computationally expensive physics-based modeling and combinatorial search for inverse design, making it difficult to efficiently reproduce diverse target shapes. To address this challenge, we present a learning-based real-to-sim-to-real framework that translates biological curves into robotic configurations through surrogate-assisted inverse design and uncertainty-guided refinement. The proposed method combines learned inverse prediction with targeted candidate exploration, using a fast forward surrogate to evaluate and select suitable designs. The learned forward surrogate reduces deformation prediction time from $1.53 \times 10^5$ ms to 0.0008 ms, achieving approximately a $1.9 \times 10^8$-fold speedup. At the same time, the proposed inverse design method reduces design time from $1.99 \times 10^6$ ms to 4.18 ms. On 5,000 test samples generated from previously unseen threading patterns, the method achieves a 93% shape reconstruction success rate under surrogate-based evaluation. Physical demonstrations with four bioinspired robots further illustrate the translation of biological curves into realizable robotic shapes.

MRI-to-CT Translation of Spinal Images via Anatomy-Guided Pseudo-Pairing and Adversarial Diffusion

Abstract (English): Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is routinely used to diagnose spinal disorders, but surgical planning often requires Computed Tomography (CT) for better visualization of vertebral bone anatomy. Synthesizing CT from MRI could avoid additional radiation exposure and streamline preoperative assessment. We propose a three-stage framework for spine MRI-to-CT synthesis. First, an anatomy-guided vertebral matching system identifies similar sagittal MRI and CT slices using spine segmentations and aligns them through rigid registration, creating pseudo-paired data from unpaired scans. Second, these pairs train an adversarial diffusion model for MRI-to-CT translation. Third, synthesized slices are assembled into 3D volumes and refined by a network that improves inter-slice continuity and structural coherence. The framework was trained on over 10,000 unpaired sagittal MRI and CT slices and evaluated on 1,911 slices from 83 MRI volumes spanning all spinal regions. Assessment combined distribution-based measures with vertebral segmentation metrics. Compared with diffusion alone, the proposed approach increased binary 3D vertebral Dice from 0.666 to 0.739. Volumetric post-processing reduced visible discontinuities between stacked slices and improved vertebral coherence in axial and coronal views. Combining anatomy-guided pseudo-pairing, adversarial diffusion and 3D refinement improves spine MRI-to-CT synthesis. Vertebral matching primarily enhanced anatomical alignment and fidelity, while volumetric refinement improved 3D consistency. These findings support further development of MRI-derived CT-like visualization for preoperative planning, particularly to preserve fine intensity details while maintaining volumetric coherence.

Dense Representation Learning and Differentiable Shape Modeling for Spine Landmark Detection

Abstract (English): Spine landmark detection from spinal X-ray is a key step for estimating spine deformation severity, for building biomechanical models, and for pre-operative planning. We reframe anatomical landmark detection as a pixel-level contrastive representation learning problem, matching dense pixel embeddings with landmark prototypes. Surprisingly, even with a generic U-Net backbone, this formulation results in more discriminative heatmaps compared to the traditional heatmap regression paradigm, leading to improved discrimination between vertebral corners on distinct vertebra. Furthermore, we combine the resulting heatmaps with learnable shape priors over the global configuration of spinal landmarks to resolve ambiguities in landmark locations, yielding a simple and practical approach to spinal landmark detection. Our approach is competitive, in terms of landmark accuracy and clinical parameter estimation, with the state-of-the-art on the public AASCE dataset and on two private spinal X-ray datasets. Our code and pre-trained weights are available at https://github.com/BioMedTP/Pix2Point.

OA-RAG: Ontology-based Agentic RAG for Faithful LLM Reasoning

Abstract (English): Large language models (LLMs) perform well across many real-world tasks but remain prone to hallucination, limiting their reliability in domains such as biomedicine and healthcare, where answers must be grounded in established knowledge. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) mitigates this problem by incorporating external information, but often overlooks logical relationships and relies heavily on the LLM's own reasoning, which can compromise answer faithfulness. We propose OA-RAG, an agent that integrates formal reasoning with LLM reasoning, drawing on both the logical structure and informal annotations of domain ontologies. For each answer, OA-RAG reports the ontology observations used as evidence and a model-assessed grounding level (entailed, supported, related, or unrelated) that characterises the strength of that evidence. Across benchmarks derived from three large-scale ontologies and six real-world medical question-answering tasks, OA-RAG achieves the best overall performance among the evaluated ontology-fine-tuned and popular RAG methods, with particularly strong results on reasoning-intensive tasks. The reported grounding levels also offer a useful indication of answer reliability, with stronger grounding generally associated with higher accuracy. Finally, our results demonstrate the value of ontologies as knowledge sources: OA-RAG is competitive with, and sometimes outperforms, domain-specific RAG systems that draw on sources such as textbooks.

Authors: Hui Yang, Jiaoyan Chen, Yongsheng Gao

Ontology-guided multi-agent reasoning for explainable differential diagnosis

Abstract (English): Effective differential diagnosis depends on well structured and unambiguous patient case descriptions, since incomplete or ambiguous clinical information readily leads to diagnostic errors and inconsistent reasoning. Large Language Models (LLMs) have recently shown promising capabilities in medical question answering and clinical reasoning; however, even when achieving competitive performance on medical benchmarks, they often rely on pattern recognition learned from training data rather than grounded clinical evidence, and can generate plausible yet unsupported diagnostic conclusions. Their reasoning remains largely opaque, making it difficult for clinicians to assess the evidence and rationale behind a given recommendation. To address this gap, we propose an ontology-guided, multiagent differential diagnosis framework that replicates the iterative reasoning process of physicians. The framework grounds diagnostic inference in a structured, expert-curated medical knowledge base, enabling interpretation of symptoms, clinical findings, or disease relationships, while decomposing diagnosis into explicit, auditable reasoning steps. This ontology-based grounding constrains inference to evidence-backed associations, reducing hallucination and improving diagnostic transparency, traceability, and consistency. The system then identifies the most discriminative missing symptoms and generates targeted follow-up questions to reduce uncertainty through a process that mirrors to clinical inquiry. Together, these components improve the reliability, explainability, and diagnostic accuracy of AI-assisted differential diagnosis. The source code is publicly available.
