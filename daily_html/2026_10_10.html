EndoRSI: Physical-Evidence-Driven Recursive Improvement of Endoscopic Robot Policies

Abstract (English): Artificial intelligence is beginning to automate scientific discovery, yet physical robot-policy research still relies on scientists and engineers to interpret experimental failures and decide how controllers should be revised. We present EndoRSI (Endoscopic Recursive Self-Improvement), a human-governed, self-improving robotic research system in which the embedded EndoForge coding agent iteratively develops and refines endoscopic robot policies using evidence from physical experiments. Physical execution remains isolated within a safety-constrained runtime under human authorisation. In motorised endoscopy, validation evidence first caused the agent to reject an initial action representation, while subsequent physical experiments falsified an offline-supported insertion strategy by revealing hazardous closed-loop behaviour absent from the source data. Failed physical rollouts were then incorporated into policy revision; insertion success subsequently increased from 2/21 in the first generation to 17/21 in the fourth. The frozen final policy retained competence across visual and hardware shifts and was the best performing autonomous policy across six independent ex-vivo porcine specimens. These results show that physical robot interaction can serve as experimental evidence for agent-driven robot policy research.

Multimodal Image-based Robotic Assistance and Guidance Environment (MIRAGE): A Novel Unified Framework for Surgical Navigation and Visualization

Abstract (English): This paper presents the computational architecture and quantitative preclinical evaluation of MIRAGE, a multimodal image-guided robotic surgery framework for real-time visualization and navigation. It integrates stereovision scene reconstruction, augmented-reality (AR) video overlay, quantified heads-up display (HUD) navigation, and intraoperative ultrasound within the da Vinci console's TilePro display (Intuitive Surgical). Additional modules include optical/electromagnetic tracker registration, stereo calibration, fiducial rigid registration with RMSE feedback, and 3D point-cloud reconstruction. Video I/O and rendering run in a C++ backend, while a Python GUI coordinates calibration, registration, reconstruction, visualization, and user interaction through an in-process zero-copy binding layer. The framework formalizes spatial transforms, module states, and synchronization of heterogeneous video and tracking streams. Preoperative calibration uses synchronized checkerboard image pairs and optimization, with estimated intrinsics, extrinsics, and rectification reused intraoperatively. MIRAGE was validated in phantom experiments across da Vinci S, Si, Xi, and SP platforms. Across clinically relevant working distances, mean 3D reprojection error was 0.6mm, with best 0.1mm RMSE at 100mm on Xi. Results support MIRAGE as a preclinical technical-validation platform for accurate, responsive, and extensible multimodal surgical guidance.

摘要（中文）: 本文介绍了MIRAGE的计算架构与定量临床前评估——一个用于实时可视化和导航的多模态图像引导机器人手术框架。该框架在达芬奇控制台的TilePro显示系统（Intuitive Surgical）中集成了立体视觉场景重建、增强现实（AR）视频叠加、量化平视显示（HUD）导航以及术中超声。其他模块包括光学/电磁跟踪器配准、立体标定、带RMSE反馈的基准刚性配准和三维点云重建。视频输入/输出与渲染在C++后端运行，而Python GUI通过进程内零拷贝绑定层协调标定、配准、重建、可视化及用户交互。该框架形式化了空间变换、模块状态以及异构视频和跟踪流的同步。术前标定使用同步棋盘格图像对和优化方法，并将估计的内参、外参和校正参数在术中复用。MIRAGE在达芬奇S、Si、Xi和SP平台上进行了体模实验验证。在临床相关工作距离内，平均三维重投影误差为0.6mm，在Xi平台上100mm距离处最佳RMSE为0.1mm。结果表明，MIRAGE可作为准确、响应迅速且可扩展的多模态手术引导的临床前技术验证平台。

TLDR: MIRAGE is a unified multimodal image-guided robotic surgery framework for da Vinci consoles, integrating stereo reconstruction, AR overlay, HUD navigation, and intraoperative ultrasound with calibration/registration, validated in phantoms with sub-millimeter reprojection errors.

Image-Based Path-Planning for Navigation of Soft-Growing Robots in the Spinal Sub-Arachnoid Space

Abstract (English): Recently, soft robotic procedures are being developed aiming at achieving targeted minimally invasive operations or drug administration in specific locations in the human body. Accordingly, specialized path-planning algorithms need to be investigated for the estimation of efficient paths that would protect the delicate tissue structures and simultaneously satisfy a set of requirements for the efficient navigation of the robot. In this context, we propose an image-based 3D pathplanning algorithm, which is an extension of the A* algorithm, for the navigation of a soft-growing robot inside the spinal subarachnoid space (SSS). The proposed algorithm is capable of estimating a safe pathway towards the goal location, while ensuring the establishment of anchor points that facilitate the efficient steering of the soft robot. The algorithm is evaluated using a highly detailed model of the SSS with respect to its capacity to satisfy the robot movement requirements and path efficiency.

摘要（中文）: 近年来，为实现人体特定部位的精准微创手术或药物递送，软体机器人技术正被广泛研发。相应地，需要研究专门的路径规划算法，以估计能够保护脆弱组织结构、同时满足机器人高效导航要求的有效路径。在此背景下，我们提出了一种基于图像的3D路径规划算法，该算法是A*算法的扩展，用于软体生长机器人在脊髓蛛网膜下腔（SSS）内的导航。所提算法能够估计通往目标位置的安全路径，同时确保建立有助于软体机器人高效转向的锚点。该算法利用SSS的高度精细化模型进行评估，以考察其满足机器人运动需求和路径效率的能力。

TLDR: Presents an image-based 3D path-planning algorithm extending A* to navigate soft-growing robots safely through the spinal subarachnoid space, supporting anchor points for steering and evaluating path efficiency on a detailed anatomical model.
