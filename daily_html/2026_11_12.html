Toward Cross-Rubric Surgical Skill Assessment via Joint Embedding Prediction

Abstract (English): Expert review of surgical videos is essential for surgical training but labor-intensive, motivating AI-assisted skill assessment to provide feedback at scale. Although surgical assessment rubrics may use different language to describe similar skills, existing automated methods commonly predict predefined skill levels or numerical scores tied to fixed rubrics, limiting their ability to transfer across semantically equivalent assessment criteria. We propose Predictive Rubric Alignment (PRA), a joint embedding prediction approach that scores surgical videos using natural-language descriptions of skill levels. Rather than matching level probabilities, PRA predicts a rater-weighted combination of skill-level description embeddings. Adjacent description features define a score-parameterized path, and the predicted embedding is projected onto this path to read the score. We introduce two variants: \textit{PRA-item}, which uses learned queries for predefined assessment items, and \textit{PRA-cond}, which generates queries from textual item names to enable assessment of unseen items without retraining. On AIxSuture, a dataset of expert-rated suturing videos, PRA outperforms published baselines in skill classification. We further evaluate zero-shot transfer to the smaller surgical skill assessment benchmark JIGSAWS. Trained on only two AIxSuture rubric items, PRA-cond assesses unseen JIGSAWS items without further training, yielding positive score correlations and greater Grad-CAM overlap with instruments and suture than a description-matching baseline. These results support predictive rubric alignment as a promising direction for flexible and explainable automated surgical skill assessment.

Actuation and Control of Passive Tendon-Driven Soft Extensions Through Rigid Manipulator Motion

Abstract (English): Industrial manipulators offer reach and precision but can damage nearby structures in confined spaces. Soft robots provide compliant contact but typically have limited reach. Rigid-soft systems combine their advantages, although they often require dedicated actuation and control hardware. We propose actuating a passive tendon-driven soft extension through manipulator joint motion. As the robot moves, the exterior spans of tendons anchored to upstream links change, altering tendon tension and deforming the extension. Inspection through narrow apertures, for example, constrains the motion of the end effector (EE). We develop a controller that regulates the soft tip while preserving such EE constraints. A differential model separates rigid transport from soft deformation, combining analytic arm and tendon kinematics with a locally identified span-to-deformation map. We also derive a local upper bound on the number of independent deformation directions transmitted by the tendons under the EE constraint, and conditions for the exponential convergence of the tip error. A quadratic program with control barrier functions imposes obstacle clearance, fulcrum limits, and model-validity conditions. Simulations and real experiments show tip and shape regulation under free, position-constrained, and fulcrum-constrained EE motion.

Subwavelength registration of skull-compensating acoustic holograms via nonlinear parametric array feedback

Abstract (English): Realizing the potential of optimized acoustic holograms for applications like transcranial ultrasound requires sub-wavelength alignment accuracy (less than 1.5 mm at 1 MHz), which is difficult to achieve with current non-MRI optical neuro-navigation methods. To address the critical challenge of alignment, we leverage the parametric array (PA) signal as an alignment surrogate, a highly penetrative, low-frequency difference wave generated by the nonlinear mixing of two primary high-frequency beams. Simulations show that a misaligned skull-compensating lens increases the primary acoustic energy within the skull bone (volumetric energy trapping), which strengthens the nonlinear source of the PA signal. We also tested whether shear-mode conversion in bone contributes: 2D elastic simulations showed only a small (3-5%) increase in shear-type motion with misalignment, and the transmitted ex vivo bursts did not lengthen as a shear path would require. Pseudo-sound and microbubble artifacts were examined and found unlikely to explain the signal, consistent with the PA signal arising from the intrinsic nonlinearity of the bone. Minimizing this PA signal yields a noninvasive real-time feedback mechanism that achieves sub-wavelength alignment accuracy, outperforming standard neuronavigation. Our findings present a robust framework that supports the design of simple, economical, and high-performance transcranial ultrasound systems for high-precision neuro-interventions.

MAGIC: Modality-Aware Gini Inspection for Clinical Hallucination Detection in LMMs

Abstract (English): Recent advances in medical large multimodal models (LMMs) have brought promising progress to clinical decision support, yet hallucination, the generation of confident but clinically unverifiable outputs, poses a critical threat to the reliability demanded in high-stakes medical settings. Existing hallucination detection methods, predominantly designed for unimodal settings, fail to capture the visual-linguistic dynamics inherent to medical imaging. We formulate the problem of hallucination detection in LMMs as disproportionate distribution of attention weights across the modalities during answer token generation. We quantify modality-specific attention concentration across layers and heads using the Gini coefficient and demonstrate statistically significant redistribution of attention mass between visual and textual tokens in hallucinated responses. We leverage these features to train a logistic regression-based classifier and evaluate it on two medical LMMs across three medical imaging modalities, demonstrating that our method achieves competitive or superior performance against contemporary hallucination detection baselines.

Curiosity-0826-4B: Generative Medical Event Sequence Modeling Across Pediatric and Adult Populations

Abstract (English): We introduce Curiosity-0826-4B, an updated version of the generative medical event foundation model trained on Epic Cosmos that simulates patient timelines to produce zero-shot clinical predictions. Curiosity-0826-4B incorporates a broader patient population and richer clinical event representations, while generally matching or exceeding Curiosity-0226-1B, the largest model in the previous Curiosity-0226 series, on evaluated tasks supported by both models. Laboratory value prediction improved, with MAE 18--46% lower than that of Curiosity-0226-1B. We evaluated prediction of disease events across pediatric cohorts aged 2--17 years. AUROCs for new asthma diagnosis (0.742--0.785) and asthma exacerbation (0.829--0.852) were comparable to those in the adult reference cohort, while AUROCs for sickle cell crisis (0.820--0.847) were lower than in adults (0.915). Explicit representation of documented death enabled new mortality prediction tasks among adults across hospice, inpatient, and ambulatory settings (AUROC 0.821--0.942). Scaling-law analysis guided compute-optimal initial pretraining followed by continued pretraining with context extension. The resulting Curiosity-0826-4B model has more parameters (4.3B versus 1B) and a longer context window (16K versus 8K). It processes 56% fewer training tokens and requires 2.2 times the training floating point operations (FLOPs) compared with its predecessor. By extending a common zero-shot prediction strategy to pediatric patients and additional outcomes, Curiosity-0826-4B broadens opportunities to develop clinical and operational applications without training separate task-specific models. Further validation should assess local calibration, performance across patient subgroups and event frequencies, timeline completion rates, and generalization to new settings, alongside prospective evaluation of clinical and operational utility.

A frontier machine intelligence for human neuroimaging

Abstract (English): Medical imaging is essential for diagnosing disease, guiding treatment and monitoring patients over time. Although artificial intelligence has advanced image interpretation, existing medical models often address restricted tasks, while general-purpose proprietary models lack access to private clinical imaging required to achieve medical expertise. Developing models that can interpret complex examinations and communicate their findings in the context of clinical decisions remains a major challenge. Here, we show that Neuro-VL, a vision-language model trained on nearly one million neuroimaging studies from routine clinical care, can interpret complete imaging examinations and engage in natural language conversations about their findings. Across internal and international datasets, Neuro-VL outperformed frontier general-purpose and specialized medical models in diagnosing, localizing, and describing abnormalities, as well as generating comprehensive radiology reports. The model also compared serial examinations to assess disease progression and more reliably resisted misleading clinical assumptions than leading frontier models from OpenAI and Google. These results extend health system learning beyond individual predictions to interactive image interpretation and longitudinal disease monitoring. They demonstrate how the collective clinical experience of health systems can be used to develop medical AI with specialized expertise and broad clinical capabilities.

Discharge Summary Generation Is Not Just Summarization: A Reality Check for Evaluation

Abstract (English): Discharge summaries combine a retrospective account of hospitalization with discharge-time decisions. Physician-written references therefore contain both facts supported by prior clinical notes and reference-only content, potentially reflecting clinical synthesis, handoffs, or discharge planning. Reference-only evaluation can conflate failure to recover available information with failure to reproduce content absent from the model input. We introduce FairSumEval, an evidence-aware, fact-level framework that assesses whether each reference and generated fact is supported by the model's evidence and captured by the other summary. The resulting outcome map separates matches, omissions, and additions. Two complementary metrics, Soft IoU-S and Soft IoU-NS, measure reference-generation agreement for facts with and without identified evidence support. On 200 MIMIC-III admissions, 18.8% of physician reference facts have identified support in the prior evidence, compared with 65.4% and 63.5% of facts generated by a general-purpose and a medically specialized model. Overlap is substantially higher for supported content (0.255 and 0.236) than for content without identified support (0.030 and 0.026). The two scores are weakly correlated across admissions (rho = 0.22 and 0.17), and conventional metrics do not consistently capture both dimensions. FairSumEval distinguishes evidence-grounded summarization from agreement on content without identified support, revealing what models omit and add in each category.

Medical Image Segmentation: Foundation Model Adaptation versus Task-Specific Training

Abstract (English): Foundation models pretrained on large natural-image collections are increasingly adapted for medical image segmentation, in the expectation that they will replace models trained for each task. Most comparisons, however, use data from the training distribution and a single task-specific baseline whose configuration is rarely justified, and therefore provide limited evidence of performance at new clinical sites. It also remains unclear how much of a foundation model must be adapted, and what determines this. We adapted a foundation model, DINOv2, with strategies ranging from tuning only its bias terms to full fine-tuning, and compared it with U-Nets and ResU-Nets trained from scratch. Experiments covered colonoscopy, breast ultrasound and dermoscopy, with testing on held-out data from the training source and on independent datasets from other institutions. The adapted foundation model generalised better on every external dataset. Within the training distribution, the task-specific models remained competitive when training data were abundant, and delineated boundaries more precisely, while the foundation model more often missed small lesions. Only a small fraction of the foundation model required adaptation, and the capacity needed was determined by the imaging domain rather than by the adaptation method. A widely used polyp test set was also found to duplicate images from another benchmark. Foundation-model adaptation is therefore warranted where models must work across sites, with capacity selected on the target domain.

A first application of split conformal prediction for dose prediction in knowledge-based radiotherapy planning

Abstract (English): Radiation therapy treatment planning is a labour-intensive process prone to inter-expert variability. Deep learning (DL) dose prediction can accelerate this process, but most DL models do not quantify their reliability. In this study, we construct prediction intervals for DL-based radiotherapy dose prediction using split conformal prediction (CP), a model-agnostic and distribution-free method for uncertainty quantification that provides finite-sample coverage guarantees. We propose patient-level nonconformity scores obtained by aggregating voxelwise scores. Specifically, we formulate a maximum aggregation construction, which guarantees simultaneous coverage of the true dose in all voxels of a future patient with at least a user-defined probability, and a quantile-based construction, which guarantees, with the same probability lower bound, that a future patient attains a prescribed minimum level of voxel coverage. We also investigate groupwise calibration for region-specific guarantees and predictive uncertainty scaling for locally adaptive prediction intervals. The methods are evaluated on head-and-neck cases using a deep ensemble model. The proposed constructions achieved empirical coverage consistent with the nominal levels. Maximum aggregation led to conservative prediction intervals, whereas quantile aggregation produced narrower intervals. Groupwise calibration and uncertainty scaling reduced interval widths in some anatomical regions, while providing region-specific guarantees and local adaptivity. This is the first application of split CP to radiotherapy dose prediction. The explicit patient-level coverage guarantees offer a more interpretable characterisation of prediction reliability than raw uncertainty estimates. Split CP could help clinicians assess the reliability of model predictions and identify cases requiring further review.
