4D Triangle Splatting Reconstruction of Dynamic Endoscopic Scenes from Monocular Videos

Abstract (English): Robust 4D reconstruction of deformable endoscopic scenes from monocular videos remains challenging due to non-rigid tissue deformation, illumination changes, occlusions, and unknown camera trajectories. Reliable dynamic reconstruction of endoscopies could significantly enhance visualization, diagnostic accuracy and intraoperative guidance of minimally invasive procedures. We propose SurgicalTS, a self-supervised triangle-splatting framework for dynamic endoscopic scene reconstruction from static monocular cameras. The scene is represented by a canonical set of triangles jointly optimized with a deformation network. We introduce a quaternion-based deformation model, an improved color parametrization, spatio-temporal priors, and a redefined initialization strategy that improves completeness under occlusions. Extensive evaluation on challenging in-vivo endoscopy videos from diverse procedures shows that SurgicalTS successfully models soft-tissue deformations and tool-tissue interactions over time, while consistently outperforming state-of-the-art methods. Our method also reduces training times while maintaining competitive geometric and photometric accuracy results, highlighting the effectiveness of quaternion-based deformation modelling with physical consistency for robust and efficient 4D reconstruction of endoscopic scenes.

SurgStream: A Workflow-Anchored Proactive Streaming Framework and Benchmark for Real-Time Surgical Video Understanding

Abstract (English): Surgical video understanding is essential for intraoperative assistance such as workflow monitoring, instrument preparation and procedural documentation. Delivering such assistance in real time requires a model to jointly perceive the ongoing scene, remember past surgical events, and respond before clinically relevant moments pass. Existing approaches provide limited support for jointly retaining procedural evidence and deciding when to respond in streaming surgical video. In this work, we propose SurgStream, a workflow-anchored proactive streaming framework for real-time surgical video understanding. We first introduce Workflow-Anchored Memory (WAM) to record structured instrument-action-target observations and fold them into phase-level summaries, providing compact and queryable surgical memory. Deadline-aware Transition Learning (DTL) is then devised to supervise response states within clinical windows, enabling timely responses before procedural deadlines. We further introduce SurgStream-Bench, comprising 11.7K streaming interactions across various tasks covering real-time perception, backward memory queries, forward proactive QA, proactive alerts and caption memory. Experiments on SurgStream-Bench show gains over state-of-the-art methods, especially in memory and forward proactive QA. The source code will be available.

ERIA: An Instruction-Optimized Agent for Clinical EEG Reporting

Abstract (English): Clinical EEG interpretation requires integrating technical quality, background and behavioral states, interictal and ictal abnormalities, and overall clinical context across long recordings. We introduce ERIA (EEG Reporting and Instruction-Optimized Agent), a locally deployable framework in which a single 27B language-model controller analyzes whole-recording EEGs by orchestrating specialized parametric and signal-processing tools. ERIA combines learned tools for background abnormality, sleep-wake state, seizure-like activity, and interictal epileptiform discharges with deterministic spectral, amplitude, symmetry, montage, and signal-quality analyses. Rather than fine-tuning the controller or using multi-agent orchestration, ERIA keeps the controller weights, specialist tools, and safety constraints fixed and optimizes only its natural-language analysis policy with GEPA (Genetic-Pareto), using feedback from expert-written EEG reports. GEPA improves physician-aligned whole-recording report quality over the raw policy, and the optimized policy transfers to external EEG benchmarks. On CerebraGloss-Bench and BrainBench Foundational Analysis, ERIA remains competitive with the models evaluated in the original benchmarks. These results show that specialist tools and language-level policy optimization can extend the clinical and general EEG capabilities of a single locally executable model while preserving local inference and traceable tool use.

CUFormer: Modality-Aware CT-Ultrasound Point Cloud Registration for Orthopedic Navigation

Abstract (English): Registering intraoperative Ultrasound (US) bone-surface point clouds to preoperative Computed Tomography (CT) models can support radiation-free orthopedic navigation. Differences in imaging physics produce distinct surface sampling patterns, complicating cross-modal feature matching. Bone strongly reflects ultrasound and casts an acoustic shadow, so US captures only the probe-facing bone surface and leaves most CT points unmatched. This partial coverage makes correspondences ambiguous on near-symmetric bones. We propose CUFormer, a modality-aware registration framework. We introduce a Modality-Aware Transformer (MAT) that encodes input modality through identity embeddings and updates features through modality-specific adapters within a shared geometric matcher. We model imaging visibility and use predicted reference-side visibility to weight coarse and fine correspondences. CUFormer achieves the lowest mean registration errors among evaluated methods on two CT-US datasets, UltraBones100k and UltraBonesHip, and the CT-RGB-D dataset SpineDepth. On UltraBones100k, it reaches mean rotation error 4.53$^\circ$, translation error 3.19\,mm, and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) 2.54\,mm, reducing RMSE by 65.8\% relative to the best-performing baseline by this metric. These results demonstrate CUFormer's effectiveness in aligning partial bone surfaces with preoperative CT across anatomical sites.

State-Aware Virtual Patient Simulation for Training Psychiatrists in Challenging Clinical Scenarios

Abstract (English): Psychiatrists must be prepared to manage patients exhibiting challenging psychological states, including suicidal ideation, hallucinations, and other high-risk behaviors. However, opportunities to safely practice such interactions are limited. While recent conversational agents can simulate patients, existing approaches largely rely on large language models (LLMs) to determine patient responses, making it difficult to consistently maintain psychological state, adapt to the clinician's responses, and generate clinically plausible challenges that meaningfully test the clinician. We present a state-aware virtual patient simulation pipeline for training psychiatrists in challenging clinical scenarios. The proposed system maintains an evolving patient state throughout the interaction and orchestrates patient behavior rather than leaving response generation entirely to an LLM. At each interaction, a learning-based behavioral strategy selector determines the type and level of challenge appropriate to the current patient and clinician context. The selected strategy, patient state, clinician response, interaction history, and relevant clinical knowledge are then provided as grounded context to an LLM to generate a semantically relevant and psychologically consistent patient response. Following each session, the system analyzes the interaction and provides feedback to the clinician on their communication and management of the virtual patient.

Accuracy Is Not Risk: Why Medical AI Should Not Report Only Its Most Likely Label

Abstract (English): Background. Many medical AI models are trained to reproduce a single reference label and hand the most likely label to the next step in a clinical workflow. A classifier can select that label correctly while providing an inadequate summary for estimating downstream risk. Methods. We use Jeffrey's rule of conditioning to specify when probabilities over intermediate states, such as diagnoses or imaging categories, can be carried forward to an outcome risk. We separate two errors of reporting only the most likely label, discarding the probability of the other states and applying the risk of patients who have the leading diagnosis to a patient who only probably has it. We also give conditions under which the label and the full distribution lead to the same decision, and we propose guardrails for device developers, regulatory reviewers, and health insurers. Results. In a hypothetical chest-pain differential, reporting only the benign leading diagnosis greatly understates the patient's risk of death or cardiac arrest, so a patient who warrants evaluation could be discharged or denied imaging. In a hypothetical BI-RADS example, the most likely category is "probably benign," yet the propagated malignancy risk exceeds the ceiling that defines that category. Conclusions. A predicted label is not an observed state, and a calibrated classifier is not automatically a valid outcome model. Clinical and insurance coverage decisions should rest on the patient's outcome risk, judged against a threshold justified by the consequences of acting and not acting. Developers should state what each model output means and check that every step between the model and the decision preserves the patient's risk.

摘要（中文）: 摘要 背景：许多医学人工智能模型被训练用于重现单一参考标签，并将最可能的标签交给临床工作流的下一步。分类器可以正确选择该标签，但同时却可能无法为下游风险评估提供充分的摘要。方法：我们使用杰弗里条件化规则来明确在何种情况下，中间状态（如诊断或影像类别）的概率可以延续至结局风险。我们区分了仅报告最可能标签时产生的两种误差：丢弃其他状态的概率，以及将具有主要诊断患者的风险应用于仅可能患有该诊断的患者。我们还给出了标签与完整分布导致相同决策的条件，并为设备开发者、监管审评人员和健康保险公司提出了保障措施。结果：在一个假设的胸痛鉴别诊断中，仅报告良性的主要诊断会大大低估患者死亡或心脏骤停的风险，因此本应接受评估的患者可能被出院或拒绝影像检查。在一个假设的BI-RADS示例中，最可能的类别是“可能良性”，然而传导的恶性肿瘤风险超过了定义该类别的上限。结论：预测标签并非观察到的状态，校准后的分类器并不自动成为有效的结局模型。临床决策和保险覆盖决策应基于患者的结局风险，并以根据采取或不采取措施的后果所确定的阈值进行判断。开发者应说明每个模型输出的含义，并检查从模型到决策之间的每一步是否保持了患者的风险。

Authors: Jeffrey Humpherys, Makoto Jones

Prior-Guided Physics-Informed Deep Learning for Fast 4D MRI Reconstruction

Abstract (English): Background and purpose: We propose PGPI4MR (prior-guided, physics-informed 4D MRI reconstruction), a deep learning method that leverages information from other respiratory phases or a setup scan, the first 30 s of the same scan, to improve 4D MRI reconstruction without extra scan time. Materials and methods: PGPI4MR is an unrolled network that reconstructs 16 respiratory phases from 60, 30, or 15 s of data, and single 3D volumes, each from 0.29-0.49 s of data, using a setup-scan image. The networks were trained once, on other volunteers, and were not retrained for new volunteers. We evaluated PGPI4MR on measured k-space from three datasets: free-breathing MR-linac (1.5T) and abdominal (3T) scans, and thigh scans with cuff compression (1.5T). All methods, including comparative models, received identical samples and were scored similarly. Results: In vivo, PGPI4MR had the lowest held-out error for 4D MRI at every scan time, and its 16 phases were ready a median of 7.5-13 s after the scan on the 2 GPUs tested. Coupling the 16 respiratory phases reduced the error at 60 s by 9.1% compared with reconstructing each phase alone. For sub-second MRI, the error at 0.49 s was 15.5% lower than that of the best comparison method, classical reconstruction toward the same prior, and on the faster GPU, every 0.29 s window was acquired and reconstructed within 0.5 s. Without retraining, PGPI4MR matched the best methods on the 3T abdominal scans but not on the thigh scans; after retraining on thigh data, it reduced the held-out error by 6.6% relative to the best comparison method. Conclusion: Prior information allowed PGPI4MR to reconstruct 4D and sub-second MRI more accurately than compressed sensing and a published 4D network, within seconds and without extra scan time. This could support 4D MRI for treatment planning and 3D motion monitoring during MR-guided radiotherapy.

Accounting for Irregular State Transitions through Belief Estimation in Online Medical Consultation

Abstract (English): While online medical consultation broadens access, unprompted patient disclosures and topic shifts frequently disrupt clinician inquiry, leaving clinical states progressively revealed. We characterize these dynamics using both existing evaluation metrics and task-specific interaction metrics designed in this work. Through an empirical study across 16 medical dialogue datasets, we show that authentic online interactions exhibit substantially lower contextual continuity and higher unpredictability than offline and simulated benchmarks. We propose PRISM (Policy Reasoning with Inferred States for Medicine), a framework inspired by partially observable Markov decision processes that decouples latent state estimation from action selection to address drifting dialogue trajectories. Specifically, PRISM factorizes clinical triage into belief estimation, policy optimization, and response realization. By maintaining an evolving posterior belief over consultation states and conditioning action selection on the maximum a posteriori state, PRISM advances clinical goals while accommodating patient-initiated shifts. Extensive evaluations show that PRISM substantially outperforms frontier LLMs and clinical agents, achieving a 22.40 percentage point gain in clinical decision accuracy alongside decisive improvements in response generation, surpassing the strongest baseline by 5.51 points in BERTScore_F1. Furthermore, ablation studies show that providing oracle consultation states consistently boosts response quality across all evaluated baselines by guiding their action reasoning, yielding average gains of 5.58 points in BERTScore_F1.
