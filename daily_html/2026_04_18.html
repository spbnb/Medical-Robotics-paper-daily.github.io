Contact-Aware Planning and Control of Continuum Robots in Highly Constrained Environments

Abstract (English): Continuum robots are well suited for navigating confined and fragile environments, such as vascular or endoluminal anatomy, where contact with surrounding structures is often unavoidable. While controlled contact can assist motion, unfavorable contact can degrade controllability, induce kinematic singularities, or introduce safety risks. We present a contact-aware planning approach that evaluates contact quality, penalizing hazardous interactions, while permitting benign contact. The planner produces kinematically feasible trajectories and contact-aware Jacobians which can be used for closed-loop control in hardware experiments. We validate the approach by testing the integrated system (planning, control, and mechanical design) on anatomical models from patient scans. The planner generates effective plans for three common anatomical environments, and, in all hardware trials, the continuum robot was able to reach the target while avoiding dangerous tip contact (100% success). Mean tracking errors were 1.9 +/- 0.5 mm, 1.2 +/- 0.1 mm, and 1.7 +/- 0.2 mm across the three different environments. Ablation studies showed that penalizing end-of-continuum-segment (ECS) contact improved manipulability and prevented hardware failures. Overall, this work enables reliable, contact-aware navigation in highly constrained environments.

Factor Graph-Based Shape Estimation for Continuum Robots via Magnus Expansion

Abstract (English): Reconstructing the shape of continuum manipulators from sparse, noisy sensor data is a challenging task, owing to the infinite-dimensional nature of such systems. Existing approaches broadly trade off between parametric methods that yield compact state representations but lack probabilistic structure, and Cosserat rod inference on factor graphs, which provides principled uncertainty quantification at the cost of a state dimension that grows with the spatial discretization. This letter combines the strength of both paradigms by estimating the coefficients of a low-dimensional Geometric Variable Strain (GVS) parameterization within a factor graph framework. A novel kinematic factor, derived from the Magnus expansion of the strain field, encodes the closed-form rod geometry as a prior constraint linking the GVS strain coefficients to the backbone pose variables. The resulting formulation yields a compact state vector directly amenable to model-based control, while retaining the modularity, probabilistic treatment and computational efficiency of factor graph inference. The proposed method is evaluated in simulation on a 0.4 m long tendon-driven continuum robot under three measurement configurations, achieving mean position errors below 2 mm for all three scenarios and demonstrating a sixfold reduction in orientation error compared to a Gaussian process regression baseline when only position measurements are available.

摘要（中文）: 从稀疏、有噪声的传感器数据重建连续体机械臂的形状是一项极具挑战性的任务，这是由于这类系统的无限维特性所致。现有方法大致在两类方法之间权衡：一类是产生紧凑状态表示但缺乏概率结构的参数化方法；另一类是在因子图上进行Cosserat杆推理的方法，该方法提供了原则性的不确定性量化，但代价是状态维度随空间离散化而增长。本文通过在因子图框架内估计低维几何可变应变（GVS）参数化的系数，结合了两种范式的优势。一种由应变场的Magnus展开推导出的新型运动学因子，将封闭形式的杆件几何编码为将GVS应变系数与主干姿态变量相联系的先验约束。所得公式产生了一个可直接用于模型预测控制的紧凑状态向量，同时保留了因子图推理的模块化、概率处理和计算效率优势。所提方法在三种测量配置下针对一根0.4米长的腱驱动连续体机器人进行了仿真评估，三种场景下的平均位置误差均低于2毫米，且与高斯过程回归基线相比，仅使用位置测量时方向误差降低了六倍。

TLDR: This paper introduces a factor graph‑based shape estimation method for tendon‑driven continuum robots that combines a low‑dimensional Geometric Variable Strain (GVS) parameterization with a novel Magnus‑expansion‑derived kinematic factor, achieving sub‑2 mm position errors and a sixfold reduction in orientation error over a Gaussian‑process baseline in simulation.
