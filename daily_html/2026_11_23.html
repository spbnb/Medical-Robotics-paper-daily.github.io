A colour-marker tracker for pose estimation of slender surgical instruments: vision-language-model-guided adaptive colour calibration

Abstract (English): Slender surgical instruments need small, narrow markers for camera-based pose estimation. A colour-stripe marker fits this requirement, but its colours in the image drift from the design values with lighting, printing and camera settings. Because the colours can only be corrected after the marker has been found, methods that rely on the design colours face a circular problem. We propose vision-language-model (VLM)-based adaptive colour calibration and compare it with two rule-based methods, which re-measure the colours from detections made with the design colours: Initial colour update does so once after tracking starts, and Colour adaptation continuously during tracking. VLM guidance uses a fine-tuned VLM to find the markers without relying on the design colours and builds the colour profile from their pixels. Recall/F1 (%) for Initial colour update, Colour adaptation and VLM guidance were 92.0/95.7, 87.8/93.3 and 98.2/99.1 on 672 synthetic image pairs; 51.3/67.2, 48.8/65.0 and 83.8/87.0 on eight degraded laboratory videos; and 38.9/55.2, 19.7/29.7 and 100/100 on a surgical-robot (da Vinci) video with strongly shifted colours. In that video the rule-based methods never found the purple/cyan marker, whereas VLM guidance found all 234 markers with no false positives. In a simulation, the hue boundary between the two stripes moved by less than 0.1 pixel for exposure gains of 0.5-2.0, compared with 2.9-4.0 pixels for a boundary found with a fixed brightness threshold. When the degradation is mild, the rule-based methods, which need no training and no GPU, are sufficient. When the colours change greatly, the gain comes from a VLM-guided candidate search that does not rely on the design colours. Combining colour-stripe markers with adaptive colour calibration enables tracking of slender surgical instruments with small markers.

It demonstrates robust pose estimation performance on synthetic, laboratory, and da Vinci surgical videos, especially under strongly shifted colours.

CT-Navigator: Towards Interpretable and Generalizable CT Understanding through Agentic Perception and Tool-Invocation

Abstract (English): Clinical computed tomography (CT) interpretation inherently requires progressive visual exploration, where radiologists navigate from whole scans to individual slices and regions of interest and interact with external tools when necessary. However, existing 3D medical multimodal large language models (MLLMs) typically connect a specialized 3D visual encoder to a language model, without fully leveraging the native 2D visual and agentic capabilities of generalist MLLMs. Trained on large-scale one-hop CT instruction-following data, these models also have limited ability to perform iterative reasoning and explicitly ground their conclusions in visual evidence, limiting their support for traceable CT interpretation. To solve these problems, we propose \textbf{agentic perception} as an alternative paradigm inspired by radiologists' interpretation workflows. We introduce \textbf{CT-Navigator}, a framework that harnesses generalist MLLMs' 2D visual and agentic capabilities to progressively ground CT interpretation in explicit, traceable visual evidence through hierarchical visual exploration and radiology tool invocation. To build these capabilities, we curate 8,904 visual chain-of-thought (V-CoT) trajectories for cold-start agentic supervised fine-tuning. We then optimize the policy through reinforcement learning on 33,219 questions, with data selection and dynamic rollout sampling prioritizing informative learning signals. Finally, we introduce CTNavi-Bench, a 3D CT visual question answering benchmark comprising 7,000 questions spanning anatomical regions throughout the human body. CT-Navigator achieves 79.50\% accuracy on CTNavi-Bench, surpassing the strongest baseline by 17.59\%, and demonstrates strong generalization across three external CT benchmarks. The code, model, and benchmark will be released on \href{https://github.com/fyb99/ct_navigator}{GitHub}.

摘要（中文）: 临床计算机断层扫描（CT）解读本质上需要渐进式的视觉探索，放射科医生需从整体扫描逐步深入到单个切片和感兴趣区域，并在必要时与外部工具交互。然而，现有的三维医学多模态大语言模型（MLLMs）通常将专用的三维视觉编码器连接到语言模型，未能充分利用通用型MLLMs固有的二维视觉与智能体能力。这些模型在大规模单跳CT指令遵循数据上训练，也缺乏进行迭代推理并将其结论明确建立在视觉证据上的能力，限制了对可追溯CT解读的支持。为解决这些问题，我们提出**智能体感知**（agentic perception）作为受放射科医生解读工作流程启发的替代范式。我们引入**CT-Navigator**框架，该框架利用通用型MLLMs的二维视觉与智能体能力，通过分层视觉探索和放射学工具调用来渐进式地将CT解读锚定于明确、可追溯的视觉证据之上。为构建这些能力，我们整理了8,904条视觉思维链（V-CoT）轨迹，用于冷启动智能体监督微调。随后，我们在33,219个问题上通过强化学习优化策略，并利用数据选择与动态 rollout 采样优先处理信息量丰富的学习信号。最后，我们推出CTNavi-Bench，一个包含7,000个问题、覆盖全身各解剖区域的三维CT视觉问答基准。CT-Navigator在CTNavi-Bench上达到79.50%的准确率，超越最强基线17.59%，并在三个外部CT基准上展现出强大的泛化能力。代码、模型和基准将在GitHub上发布。

TLDR: CT-Navigator proposes an agentic perception framework for interpretable 3D CT understanding, where generalist multimodal LLMs progressively explore CT volumes and invoke radiology tools, trained using visual chain-of-thought trajectories and reinforced learning; it also introduces CTNavi-Bench and reports strong accuracy and generalization gains.

Decoupling Perception from Generation for Agentic 3D CT Report Generation

Abstract (English): Chest CT reports repeatedly assess the same anatomical regions for findings and their attributes, such as whether a lung nodule is present and where it is located. These assessments suggest learning to answer visual questions directly, rather than coupling perception to open-ended text generation. Yet current CT report generation models largely follow generic vision-language modeling, which may miss subtle abnormalities when training data are limited. We therefore formulate report generation as a set of visual question-answering decisions coordinated by a large language model (LLM) agent. This decouples visual perception from reasoning and report writing: perception can be learned as a discriminative, non-generative task, while the LLM focuses on reasoning over patient context and posing relevant visual queries. We introduce Rad-VL-JEPA, which unifies report retrieval, finding recognition, and fine-grained attribute characterization through vision-language joint-embedding prediction. Building on this perception module, an agent trained with reinforcement learning (RL) acquires patient-specific visual evidence, while a separate editor incorporates the evidence into the final report, further separating evidence acquisition from report realization. Experiments show that Rad-VL-JEPA achieves strong performance on fine-grained CT understanding tasks with lower amortized per-query inference time than a generative VLM baseline trained with the same CT encoder and VQA data. The resulting reporting system outperforms strong VLM-based and agentic baselines.

摘要（中文）: 摘要： 胸部CT报告反复评估相同的解剖区域中的发现及其属性，例如肺结节是否存在及其位置。这些评估表明，直接学习回答视觉问题，而非将感知与开放式文本生成耦合，可能更为合适。然而，当前的CT报告生成模型大多遵循通用视觉-语言建模，在训练数据有限时可能遗漏细微异常。因此，我们将报告生成表述为由大语言模型（LLM）代理协调的一组视觉问答决策。这将视觉感知与推理及报告撰写解耦：感知可以作为判别式、非生成式任务来学习，而LLM专注于对患者上下文进行推理并提出相关的视觉查询。我们引入了Rad-VL-JEPA，它通过视觉-语言联合嵌入预测统一了报告检索、发现识别和细粒度属性表征。在此感知模块的基础上，使用强化学习（RL）训练的代理获取患者特定的视觉证据，而独立的编辑器将证据整合到最终报告中，进一步将证据获取与报告实现分离。实验表明，与使用相同CT编码器和VQA数据训练的生成立VLM基线相比，Rad-VL-JEPA在细粒度CT理解任务上取得了强劲性能，且每次查询的平均推理时间更低。由此产生的报告系统优于强VLM基线和代理式基线。

TLDR: This paper proposes Rad-VL-JEPA, an agentic system for 3D chest CT report generation that decouples visual perception from language generation by treating perception as discriminative visual question answering and using an LLM/RL agent to gather evidence before a separate editor writes the report. It reports stronger fine-grained CT understanding and lower per-query inference cost than generative VLM baselines.

Authors: Chenyu Wang, Mingyu Chen, Wenchao Li, Kayhan Batmanghelich

SAE-Audit: Clinical-Trial Prediction via Inference-Time Optimization of Black-Box LLMs Audited by Sparse Autoencoder Evidence

Abstract (English): Clinical trials are costly and often take years to complete, making early outcome prediction valuable for trial planning. Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to forecast clinical-trial outcomes, yet their predictions remain unreliable and their rationales may not faithfully reflect the evidence underlying their answers. On tasks such as predicting whether a registered clinical trial will complete or terminate early, frontier LLMs perform only modestly above chance in our experiments. We introduce SAE-Audit, which optimizes a black-box LLM at inference time with evidence obtained from a deployable open model. A frozen sparse autoencoder (SAE) on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is converted into a vocabulary of clinical concepts. Sparse probes fitted on past trials determine which concepts are associated with the outcome. For a new protocol, the concepts detected by the probe are written as evidence, each with the direction and strength of its association with the outcome, and the LLM being audited optimizes its answer in the light of this evidence. Without additional training, SAE-Audit raises balanced accuracy of deepseek-v4-flash by 9.6--12.0 points on five clinical-trial prediction tasks and raises GPT-5.6-terra by 8.0 points on trial completion, exceeding other agentic inference-time optimization methods by a large margin. As an auxiliary, SAE probes module within SAE-Audit is itself accurate and auditable, providing natural-language explanations directly derived from verified concept contributions and protocol evidence, without requiring additional LLMs at inference time. SAE-Audit provides verifiable, concept-level evidence for more accurate and auditable clinical-trial prediction. It can also be embedded into biomedical AI agents such as Biomni to optimize their performance.

摘要（中文）: 临床试验成本高昂，且往往需要数年才能完成，因此早期结果预测对试验规划具有重要意义。大语言模型（LLMs）越来越多地被用于预测临床试验结果，但其预测仍不可靠，且其推理依据可能无法忠实反映支撑其答案的证据。在预测已注册临床试验是否会完成或提前终止等任务中，前沿大语言模型在我们实验中仅略优于随机水平。我们提出了SAE-Audit，该方法在推理阶段利用可从可部署开源模型中获取的证据来优化黑盒大语言模型。我们将Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct上的冻结稀疏自编码器（SAE）转化为临床概念词汇表。基于过往试验拟合的稀疏探针用于确定哪些概念与结果相关。对于新的试验方案，探针检测到的概念被写作证据，每个概念都附带其与结果关联的方向和强度，而被审计的大语言模型则根据这些证据优化其答案。在不进行额外训练的情况下，SAE-Audit在五个临床试验预测任务上将deepseek-v4-flash的平衡准确率提升了9.6至12.0个百分点，并将GPT-5.6-terra在试验完成预测上的准确率提升了8.0个百分点，大幅超越了其他基于智能体的推理时优化方法。作为辅助功能，SAE-Audit内部的SAE探针模块本身既准确又具备可审计性，可直接基于经过验证的概念贡献和方案证据提供自然语言解释，且推理时无需额外的大语言模型。SAE-Audit为更准确且可审计的临床试验预测提供了可验证的概念级证据。它还可嵌入诸如Biomni之类的生物医学AI智能体中以优化其性能。

TLDR: SAE-Audit is an inference-time optimization method that extracts interpretable clinical-concept evidence from a sparse autoencoder on a small open LLM and uses it to guide black-box LLMs in predicting clinical-trial outcomes, yielding substantial balanced-accuracy gains without additional training.
