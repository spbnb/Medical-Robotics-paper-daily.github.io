An Open-Source ROS 2 Framework for Shared Control in Surgical Teleoperation: Integrating the Haply Inverse3 with the da Vinci Research Kit

Abstract (English): Surgical teleoperation deprives surgeons of touch, forcing visual inference of tissue forces; the commercial platforms now restoring force feedback remain closed, preventing open research on haptic shared control. This paper provides a system contribution: it presents an open-source, ROS 2-native framework that integrates the compact, three-degree-of-freedom (3-DOF) Haply Inverse3 haptic device with the da~Vinci Research Kit (dVRK). The framework includes a novel mapping strategy that combines 3-DOF translational device input with separately tracked VerseGrip orientation into 6-DOF patient-side manipulator commands. Two virtual-fixture-inspired guidance modes serve as reference implementations to evaluate the framework in a within-subjects user study on a teleoperated hot-wire benchmark task in simulation, and the architecture is further demonstrated on physical dVRK hardware. Relative to unassisted manual control, linear guidance significantly reduced both the number of wire contacts and the task completion time, whereas the progressive-stiffness guidance produced consistent but statistically non-significant trends in the same direction. Subjective ratings of workload, agency, and satisfaction showed favorable descriptive trends for the guidance modes. The results demonstrate the feasibility of haptic shared control with a compact device coupled to the dVRK and provide an openly available, reproducible platform for future research. They further suggest that simpler linear feedback may be more robust for inexperienced operators than more complex profiles.

A user study shows that linear guidance significantly reduces wire contacts and completion time, demonstrating feasibility of compact haptic shared control for surgical robotics research.

Design and Evaluation of Epira: An Automated Interview System for Case Investigation and Contact Tracing

Abstract (English): Objective: Case investigation and contact tracing are labor-intensive. Web forms struggle when respondents need clarification before disclosing contacts, and open-ended chatbots may deviate from protocols. We developed and evaluated Epira, a protocol-controlled large language model (LLM) interviewer for multi-disease case investigation and contact tracing. Materials and Methods: Epira pairs LLM dialogue with a rule-based controller enforcing required questions, safety handoffs, and a structured case record, and adapts to new diseases by questionnaire upload. Local (Qwen2.5) and cloud (GPT-5.4) backends were tested. Study 1 used 108 simulated cases with ground-truth fields and contacts for measles and gonorrhea under Epira and open-ended conditions (864 sessions). Study 2 enrolled 72 volunteers in a 2$\times$2 disease-by-model interview assessing completion, usability, acceptability, and trust. Results: Across Study 1, Epira with a local LLM captured 88.9% of required fields and 60.1% of ground-truth contacts. Open-ended interviewers recovered 31.1-41.9 percentage points fewer required fields than the same models in Epira. In Study 2, completion was 100%; mean adapted System Usability Scale (SUS) was 82.95 $\pm$ 16.17 on a 0-100 scale and mean trust was 3.85 $\pm$ 0.77 on a 1-5 scale. Discussion: Protocol control drove completeness more than model choice. Local and cloud deployments produced similar records, supporting privacy-preserving local use with human review of routed interviews and missed contacts. Conclusion: Epira recovers required fields and contacts under diverse disclosure conditions, works with local and cloud LLMs, and received favorable usability and trust ratings.

Evaluations show it captures more complete data than open-ended chatbots, with high completion, usability, and support for local/cloud LLM deployment.

Ferrofluid Shape Control with Deep Reinforcement Learning

Abstract (English): Ferrofluids are liquids containing magnetic nanoparticles, enabling shape control through external magnetic fields. This deformability supports their use as soft robotic bodies that can navigate confined spaces and manipulate objects. Their nonlinear continuum dynamics, however, make reliable control challenging. Existing work shows how spatial magnetic fields from electromagnet arrays can establish and maintain desired ferrofluid configurations. A complementary objective is to exploit the fluid's dynamics under limited actuation to reach prescribed shapes that may not be sustainable at equilibrium. Here, we use deep model-based reinforcement learning to control these transient shapes. Learning directly through physical interaction, our policies select current commands for a single fixed electromagnet based on visual observations, guiding the moving ferrofluid toward prescribed geometries. The learned policies repeatedly attain a variety of shapes spanning polygons, asterisks and pentominoes. These results demonstrate that learning directly from physical interaction enables reliable fluid shaping, opening new possibilities for controlling ferrofluid-based soft robots.

Auditing black-box clinical prediction models with a large language model through queries alone: a blinded test with planted flaws

Abstract (English): Large language models (LLMs) are used to explain clinical prediction models, on the promise that they can say what a model computes, find its faults and even reveal new knowledge in it. We tested the three promises where the answers could be checked. From the International Stroke Trial we trained four kinds of model (logistic regression, explainable boosting machine, gradient-boosted trees, neural network) for two outcomes on patients randomised earlier, planted one flaw in each of 24 copies (an under-predicted subgroup, a reversed risk factor or an implausible step) and mixed them with the 8 clean models. GPT-6 Astra audited each model three times, blind, through a tool that only returned the model's predictions; the judging rules were committed before any run and applied by program. Its surrogate models, with a median of 128 terms, reproduced the audited models' log-odds with a mean $R^2$ of 0.976 (0.959 for an additive surrogate of 42 terms fitted by an analyst). It found the planted flaw in 69 of 72 runs (96%; 95% CI 80% to 99%); a script written for the purpose found all 24. It also reported many more problems: 60% of 1,089 checkable reports failed the pre-specified check against the model's average behaviour; post hoc, most of these were real but small steps, and 15% of the checkable reports were not found in the model. Of 601 unexpected relationships it read out of the clean models, 11% held in patients randomised later (random pairs 6%; a regression scan's shorter list of its strongest candidates, 28%); after a check on the training data, 33% against 30%. With query access alone, an LLM can describe a black-box model faithfully and find nearly every planted flaw, as a purpose-written script also did; its reports of problems must be checked on the model and its discoveries on data.

摘要（中文）: 大型语言模型（LLMs）被用于解释临床预测模型，其承诺是它们能够说明模型计算了什么、发现模型的缺陷，甚至揭示其中的新知识。我们对其中可以检验的三项承诺进行了测试。基于国际卒中试验的数据，我们对较早随机分配的患者训练了四种模型（逻辑回归、可解释提升机、梯度提升树、神经网络）用于两个结局，在24个副本中各植入一个缺陷（一个预测不足的亚组、一个反向风险因素或一个不合理的阶梯），并将其与8个干净模型混合。GPT-6 Astra通过一个仅返回模型预测的工具，以盲法对每个模型进行了三次审计；评判规则在运行前预先设定并由程序执行。其替代模型（中位项数为128）再现了被审计模型的对数几率，平均$R^2$为0.976（由分析师拟合的42项加性替代模型为0.959）。它在72次运行中发现了69次植入的缺陷（96%；95% CI 80%至99%）；为此目的编写的脚本发现了全部24个。它还报告了更多问题：在1089个可检查报告中，60%未通过针对模型平均行为的预设检查；事后分析表明，这些大多是真实但微小的阶梯，且15%的可检查报告在模型中未被发现。在它从干净模型中读出的601个意外关系中，有11%在后来随机分配的患者中成立（随机配对为6%；回归扫描对其最强候选者的较短列表为28%）；在对训练数据进行检查后，比例为33%对30%。仅凭查询访问，LLM就能忠实地描述黑箱模型，并发现几乎所有的植入缺陷，正如专用脚本所做的那样；其报告的问题必须在模型上检查，其发现必须在数据上验证。

TLDR: This paper tests whether a large language model, with query access alone, can faithfully audit black-box clinical prediction models by describing their behavior, detecting planted flaws, and discovering new knowledge, using blinded runs on stroke outcome models.

Disentangling Monitor Hardware Artifacts and Smoothing Lag for Shift-Robust Intraoperative EEG and Hemodynamic Risk Calibration

Abstract (English): Commercial intraoperative electroencephalography (EEG) and blood pressure monitors introduce cyclic impedance pulses, smoothing delays, direct-current (DC) drift, and cuff holds that confound risk forecasting and uncertainty calibration. We developed a causal signal-conditioning and shift-robust conformal calibration framework across 5,439 public anesthesia procedures. Streaming filters blank 10.13-min 64-Hz impedance checks, apply unclipped post-notch conditioning, separate 63-s suppression-ratio lag, and distinguish three-channel cuff inflations from dropouts across propofol-remifentanil total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA; N = 3,214), volatile anesthesia (N = 2,046), European endoscopy sedation (N = 171), and DC-coupled surgery (N = 8). De-impedanced cortical tree ensembles were compared against deep sequence models, paired with patient-cluster Subgroup-Covariate-Weighted Conformal Risk Control (CRC) and lag-buffered online Conformal Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) tracking. Pre-filter blanking removed 97.90% and 97.60% of false flatline conflicts while preserving 99.23% and 98.96% of deep suppression. On unseen TIVA surgeries (N = 525), cortical features achieved area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC) 0.3135 (1.94% prevalence) and 0.1627 (0.82% lag-free prevalence), with no detectable difference from deep encoders (p >= 0.36) and no cortical gain for hypotension (0.3432 vs. 0.3437, p = 0.784). Zero-retraining volatile transfer (N = 1,048) showed no detectable difference from an end-tidal gas oracle on lag-free suppression (0.0820 vs. 0.0736, p = 0.472). Subgroup-Covariate-Weighted CRC bounded all 70 TIVA shift cells at <= 4.38% (alpha = 5.0%) in 66.32 ms per 30-s epoch. Causal artifact disentangling and shift-aware conformal calibration support reliable real-time intraoperative risk bounds within explicit physiological observability limits.

It shows filtered cortical features match deep sequence models and risk bounds are controlled across shifts.

VoxForge: A Unified Foundation Model for Dense 3D Features Across MRI, CT and PET

Abstract (English): A unified 3D medical foundation model promises reusable, frozen features that serve a wide variety of tasks such as segmentation, classification and registration on MRI, CT and PET as well as specialist models do. Generalist models still need separate backbones for localized and global tasks, specialist models are confined to a single modality, with the concern that spanning modalities under-emphasizes fine anatomy, and both typically rely on end-to-end fine-tuning to be competitive. We present VoxForge, a single encoder that integrates recent advances in 3D architecture, self-distillation and cross-modal curricula to learn transferable features from permissively licensed MRI and CT volumes. We evaluate it strictly frozen, the test a reusable foundation model must pass, against state-of-the-art generalist and specialist models on segmentation, classification and registration across MRI and CT, and on PET, which it never sees. VoxForge has the best or tied-best average rank in every task group, serves localized and global tasks across modalities at the level of state-of-the-art specialists, and is extremely label-efficient, matching top-performing models on TotalSegmentator CT with a tenth of the labels.

摘要（中文）: 一个统一的3D医学基础模型承诺提供可重用、冻结的特征，服务于MRI、CT和PET上的分割、分类和配准等广泛任务，其表现可与专科模型媲美。通用模型仍然需要为局部和全局任务分别使用不同的骨干网络，专科模型则局限于单一模态，且存在跨模态会弱化精细解剖结构关注的担忧，同时两者通常依赖端到端微调才能具有竞争力。我们提出VoxForge，一个单一编码器，整合了3D架构、自蒸馏和跨模态课程学习的最新进展，从许可开放的MRI和CT体数据中学习可迁移特征。我们对其进行严格冻结评估——这是可重用基础模型必须通过的测试——并与最先进的通用和专科模型在MRI和CT的分割、分类和配准任务以及其从未见过的PET上进行比较。VoxForge在每个任务组中都取得最佳或并列最佳的平均排名，以最先进专科模型的水平服务于跨模态的局部和全局任务，且极其标签高效，仅用十分之一的标签即可在TotalSegmentator CT上匹配顶尖模型的表现。

TLDR: VoxForge is a single 3D medical foundation encoder trained on MRI/CT via self-distillation and cross-modal curricula, yielding frozen dense features that match specialist models on segmentation, classification, and registration across MRI/CT and unseen PET, with strong label efficiency.
